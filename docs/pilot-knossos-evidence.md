## Place record (Pleiades, https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/589872, status: published)

- Title: Knosos / Col. Iulia Nobilis Cnosos. Type: settlement.
- Pleiades description: a major ancient settlement of Crete, c. 5 km south-east of Heraklion,
  occupied from the Neolithic period to the time of the Roman Empire.
- Attested names: `ko-no-so` (Linear B, 1600-1080 BC in Pleiades' dating), Knosos (750 BC-AD 640),
  Col. Iulia Nobilis Cnosos (30 BC-AD 300), Knossos (modern), Cnoso.
- Representative point: 35.297847 N, 25.163106 E.
- Related: "Bronze Age Settlement at Knossos" (places/781961476), "Labyrinth of Knossos" (places/74287421).
- Wikidata: Q173527.

## Ancient texts (Scaife Viewer, Perseus editions)

- Homer, *Odyssey* 19.172-179 (tlg0012.tlg002.perseus-grc2): Crete has ninety cities and mixed
  peoples (Achaeans, Eteocretans, Cydonians, Dorians, Pelasgians); among them Knossos, "a great
  city", where Minos ruled for nine-year periods and was "familiar of great Zeus".
- Strabo 10.4.8 (tlg0099.tlg001.perseus-grc2): Knossos was formerly called Kairatos after the
  river beside it; Minos is reported to have been a notable lawgiver and the first to rule the sea,
  founding Knossos on the north-facing side of the island.
- Thucydides 1.4.1 (tlg0003.tlg001.perseus-grc2): Minos, the earliest ruler known by report, acquired
  a navy, controlled most of the Greek sea, ruled the Cyclades and was first coloniser of most of them.
- Not yet retrieved: Iliad 18.590-592 (the dancing floor "which Daedalus made in broad Knossos";
  the Scaife line range returned the wrong text), Pausanias 1.27.10 (server error), Plutarch *Theseus*.

## Evans, *The Palace of Minos*

The archive.org item `cu31924081667812` (cataloged 1921, Macmillan) is the **whole work**
(4 vols in 6). The OCR text retrieved from it opens with a preface dated Youlbury, 10 Sept 1934 and a
frontispiece plate XXVII, so it is a **late volume**, not vol. I of 1921. The volume, year and page
numbers must be confirmed before any citation. Passages seen (volume unconfirmed):

- The deposit beneath the later palace consists of "layer after layer of primitive Neolithic
  settlements" for about seven metres to the virgin rock, and the Kephala tell was partly rased
  for the Central Court (author's preface).
- Evans describes acquiring "proprietary rights on the site from its then Turkish owners", later
  transferred to the British School at Athens, and says the quest behind "the traditions of Minos
  and Daedalos, and of the fabled Labyrinth" led him to Knossos.
- The existing `evans1921` source record is vol. I. Add separate records for other volumes only
  after reading the title pages.

## What is blocking the pilot

The claims that make Knossos worth a full page (dating of the palaces, the destruction horizons,
Linear B and the Mycenaean question, Evans's reconstruction and its critics) rest on modern
scholarship that is paywalled or on hosts this environment cannot reach. Without reading it, those
claims cannot be cited. See the decision in the session notes.
