<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian general of the early fourth century BC whose light-armed peltasts destroyed a Spartan regiment in 390 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
Famous for showing that disciplined light infantry could defeat Spartan hoplites, and as the model of a professional commander.

<!-- field: body -->
Iphicrates, the son of a shoemaker according to tradition, rose to prominence as a commander of mercenary peltasts in the Corinthian War. Peltasts took their name from the light shield, the *pelte*, and fought as skirmishers with javelins. In 390 BC his force ambushed a Spartan regiment returning from escorting a festival party near Lechaeum, the harbour of Corinth, and killed about 250 men. It was a shock to the reputation of Spartan hoplites.

Later sources, including Diodorus, credit him with reforms of equipment, such as shorter boots, a lighter shield and a longer spear and sword, and some scholars link them to the Macedonian army reforms, although there is little evidence. He took service with Persia to retake Egypt in 373 BC, and then fought for Athens in Thrace, in the Hellespont and at Corcyra.

In the Social War of 356 BC he and other generals were tried for failing in their command, and he was acquitted. He died not long afterwards.
