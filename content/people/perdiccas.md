<!-- field: summary -->
The Macedonian general who became regent of Alexander's empire on his death in 323 BC and was murdered three years later.

<!-- field: significance -->
The last man to try to hold Alexander's empire together; his failure began the wars of the Successors.

<!-- field: body -->
Perdiccas (c. 355–320 BC) was a Macedonian noble who commanded a battalion of the phalanx in Alexander's campaigns and became one of his seven bodyguards. After Hephaestion died in 324 BC he was the king's leading officer, and ancient sources say the dying Alexander handed him his signet ring.

At the settlement of Babylon in 323, the generals agreed that Alexander's half-brother Philip III Arrhidaeus and his unborn son by Roxana, Alexander IV, would rule jointly. Perdiccas became regent and commander of the royal army in Asia, and assigned provinces to the other generals: Egypt to Ptolemy, Thrace to Lysimachus, while Antipater and Craterus held Macedon and Greece.

Perdiccas soon aroused suspicion. He broke off a marriage to Antipater's daughter to seek the hand of Alexander's sister Cleopatra, which would have brought him close to the throne. When Ptolemy seized Alexander's body on its way to Macedon and took it to Egypt, Perdiccas invaded. In 320 his attempt to cross the Nile near Memphis ended in disaster, with many soldiers drowned or eaten by crocodiles. His officers, including Seleucus, murdered him in his tent.
