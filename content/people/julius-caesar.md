<!-- field: summary -->
The Roman general and dictator who won the civil war against Pompey, allied with Cleopatra VII, and was assassinated in 44 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
His alliance with Cleopatra and his civil wars, fought partly in Greece and Egypt, shaped the final years of the last Hellenistic kingdom.

<!-- field: body -->
Gaius Julius Caesar (100–44 BC) was a Roman politician and general who conquered Gaul in 58–50 BC and then fought a civil war against Pompey and the Senate. Much of that war was fought in the Greek world: Caesar defeated Pompey at Pharsalus in Thessaly in 48 BC.

Pompey fled to Egypt, where he was murdered on arrival. Caesar followed him to Alexandria and became involved in the struggle between Cleopatra VII and her brother Ptolemy XIII. Supporting Cleopatra, he was besieged in Alexandria in the Alexandrian War of 48–47 BC; during the fighting fire spread from the burning ships in the harbour and destroyed some of the city's books. After Ptolemy XIII's death, Caesar placed Cleopatra on the throne. She bore him a son, Ptolemy XV Caesar, called Caesarion, and later visited Rome.

Caesar admired Alexander the Great: Plutarch pairs their lives, and tells how Caesar wept at a statue of Alexander because at his age he had achieved so little. His reform of the Roman calendar in 46 BC, the basis of today's calendar, drew on the knowledge of the Alexandrian astronomer Sosigenes.

Caesar became dictator for life and was assassinated by a group of senators on 15 March 44 BC. The civil wars that followed set his adopted son Octavian against Mark Antony and Cleopatra.
