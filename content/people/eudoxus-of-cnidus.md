<!-- field: summary -->
A mathematician and astronomer of the fourth century BC who devised the theory of proportion and the first geometrical model of planetary motion.

<!-- field: significance -->
His mathematics underlies Book V of Euclid, and his spheres were the first systematic geometrical model of the heavens.

<!-- field: body -->
Eudoxus was born in Cnidus in south-western Asia Minor about 390 BC. He studied with Archytas in Italy, visited Egypt and then spent time in Athens, where he was associated with Plato's Academy. Later he taught at Cyzicus, and returned to Cnidus, where he was a law-giver and had an observatory.

In mathematics, he developed a theory of proportion that gave a rigorous account of ratios between incommensurable magnitudes, such as the side and diagonal of a square. It appears in Book V of Euclid's *Elements*. He also used the method of exhaustion, a way of proving results about curved figures by approximating them with polygons, which Archimedes developed further.

In astronomy, Eudoxus explained the irregular movements of the planets by combining the motions of several concentric spheres. Aristotle later adopted and extended the system. His work on the constellations provided the basis for Aratus' *Phaenomena*, and Hipparchus later criticised it. Eudoxus' own writings are lost and are known through later authors.
