<!-- field: summary -->
The Persian prince and satrap who paid for Sparta's victory in the Peloponnesian War and was killed in 401 BC in an attempt to seize the throne with Greek mercenaries.

<!-- field: significance -->
His revolt produced the march of the Ten Thousand, and showed how far an army of Greek hoplites could go inside the Persian empire.

<!-- field: body -->
Cyrus was the younger son of Darius II and Parysatis, who preferred him to his elder brother Artaxerxes. In 407 BC he was sent west as satrap of Lydia and Phrygia and as commander of all Persian forces on the coast, with orders to support Sparta. He formed a close friendship with Lysander, who received the money to pay Spartan crews that helped win Aegospotami.

After Darius died in 404 BC, Cyrus was accused of plotting against Artaxerxes but was sent back to his satrapy. He prepared a rebellion and hired Greek mercenaries under Clearchus, claiming he was only fighting rebels in Pisidia. In 401 BC he marched them to Mesopotamia and met the king's army at Cunaxa. His Greeks beat the troops opposite them, but Cyrus rode in a charge at his brother and was killed.

Xenophon, who served as a junior officer, wrote an account in his *Anabasis* that treats Cyrus with admiration. The army's march home from Babylon to the Black Sea became a celebrated story.
