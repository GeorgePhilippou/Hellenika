<!-- field: summary -->
The Achaean general from Megalopolis who led the League against Sparta and Macedon, called "the last of the Greeks".

<!-- field: significance -->
The last great general of independent Greece, who kept the Achaean League strong in the shadow of Rome.

<!-- field: body -->
Philopoemen (253–183 BC) came from Megalopolis in Arcadia. As a young man he helped defend his city against Cleomenes III of Sparta, and at the battle of Sellasia in 222 BC he won fame by leading a cavalry charge on his own initiative. He then served for ten years as a mercenary commander in Crete.

Returning, he became the leading figure of the Achaean League after the death of Aratus, and was elected its general eight times. He reformed the League's army, re-equipping its infantry as a Macedonian-style phalanx and training its cavalry hard. In 207 BC he defeated the Spartans at Mantinea and killed their tyrant Machanidas with his own hand.

As Roman power grew after 200 BC, he tried to keep the League independent while staying on good terms with Rome. In 192 he brought Sparta into the League, and in 188, after a revolt, he tore down its walls and abolished its ancient Lycurgan way of life.

In 183, aged seventy, he was captured while campaigning against rebel Messene and forced to drink poison. The young Polybius carried his ashes at his funeral. Plutarch reports that a Roman called him "the last of the Greeks", meaning that Greece produced no great man after him.
