<!-- field: summary -->
The Ptolemaic princess who was queen of Thrace and Macedon and then of Egypt, and was worshipped as a goddess after her death around 270 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first Ptolemaic queen to be deified in her own name, and the model for later powerful queens down to Cleopatra VII.

<!-- field: body -->
Arsinoe II (c. 316–270 BC) was the daughter of Ptolemy I and Berenice I. Around 300 BC, aged about sixteen, she was married to the elderly Lysimachus, king of Thrace, as part of an alliance. She had three sons by him and was accused of plotting the death of his heir Agathocles. She built a large round hall, the Arsinoeion, at the sanctuary of the Great Gods on Samothrace.

After Lysimachus died at Corupedium in 281, she married her half-brother Ptolemy Keraunos, who had seized Macedon; he then murdered two of her sons. She fled to Egypt, and around 279–273 married her full brother Ptolemy II. The marriage shocked Greeks but followed the practice of the pharaohs. Ancient and modern writers have debated how much power she had at court; she appears beside her brother on coins and inscriptions.

After her death, usually dated to July 270 BC, Ptolemy II made her a goddess. Her cult spread across Egypt, a statue of her was placed in every Egyptian temple, and a district of the Fayum and many towns around the Mediterranean were named Arsinoe after her. Callimachus wrote a poem on her death and deification.
