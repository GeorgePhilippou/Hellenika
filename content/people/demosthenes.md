<!-- field: summary -->
The greatest Athenian orator, who spent his career warning that Philip of Macedon meant to end Greek freedom — and was right.

<!-- field: significance -->
His speeches are the finest surviving Greek oratory and the fullest contemporary record of resistance to Macedon.

<!-- field: body -->
Plutarch reports that Demosthenes overcame a speech impediment by declaiming with pebbles in his mouth and speaking over the noise of waves. The story is probably embroidered; the intensity of preparation it describes is consistent with the speeches.

The *Philippics* and *Olynthiacs* argue relentlessly that Philip is not a conventional rival but an existential threat, and that Athenians must fund and serve in campaigns themselves rather than hiring mercenaries. He assembled the Theban alliance that fought at Chaeronea in 338 BC and lost.

After Alexander's death he supported a final revolt. When it failed he took poison in a temple of Poseidon rather than be captured. He was, on the central question, correct and unsuccessful.
