<!-- field: summary -->
A statesman of Mytilene on Lesbos, elected with sole power around 590 BC to end civil strife, and counted among the Seven Sages.

<!-- field: significance -->
A rare example of an archaic ruler given extraordinary powers by his fellow citizens who gave them up again.

<!-- field: body -->
Pittacus belonged to the aristocracy of Mytilene on Lesbos, where the poet Alcaeus and his friends fought the ruling faction. He first appears as a warrior in a conflict with Athens over the town of Sigeum on the Hellespont, where he is said to have defeated the Athenian commander Phrynon in single combat, trapping him in a net.

After years of faction fighting, in which he allied with and later turned against Alcaeus' group, the Mytilenaeans chose him as *aisymnetes*, a lawgiver with full authority, for ten years from about 590 BC. He passed laws, including one doubling the penalty for crimes committed while drunk. Alcaeus called him a tyrant and a low-born boor, but later writers saw him as a moderate ruler who surrendered his powers at the end of his term.

Pittacus was counted among the Seven Sages, and his sayings were quoted, among them 'Know your opportunity' and 'It is hard to be good'.
