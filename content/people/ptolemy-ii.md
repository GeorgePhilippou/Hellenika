<!-- field: summary -->
The second Ptolemaic king of Egypt (283–246 BC), under whom Alexandria became the cultural capital of the Greek world.

<!-- field: significance -->
The great patron of the Library and Museum of Alexandria, and the ruler who made the Ptolemaic monarchy a fusion of Greek and Egyptian kingship.

<!-- field: body -->
Ptolemy II (309–246 BC) was born on the island of Kos, the son of Ptolemy I and Berenice I. His father made him co-regent in 285 BC, and he ruled alone from 283/282 until 246.

His reign was the height of Ptolemaic power and splendour. He expanded the Museum and Library founded by his father, bringing scholars and poets such as Callimachus and Theocritus to Alexandria; Theocritus' seventeenth *Idyll* is a poem in his praise. The great lighthouse on Pharos was completed early in his reign. According to the *Letter of Aristeas*, a later and partly legendary text, he commissioned the translation of the Jewish Law into Greek, the Septuagint. The Egyptian priest Manetho wrote a history of Egypt in Greek in this period.

Around 279–273 BC he married his full sister Arsinoe II, earning the title *Philadelphos*, "sibling-loving". He made his dead parents into gods, and later Arsinoe and himself, founding a royal cult. He staged a vast procession at Alexandria, described by a contemporary, with gold, exotic animals and huge floats.

He fought two wars with the Seleucids over Syria and backed Athens and Sparta against Macedon in the Chremonidean War, extending Ptolemaic control across the eastern Mediterranean.
