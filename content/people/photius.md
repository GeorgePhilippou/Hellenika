<!-- field: summary -->
The 9th-century patriarch of Constantinople and scholar whose Bibliotheca preserves summaries of hundreds of ancient books, many now lost.

<!-- field: significance -->
The greatest scholar of Byzantium, at the centre of the revival of classical learning after Iconoclasm.

<!-- field: body -->
Photius (c. 810–893) came from a wealthy iconophile family of Constantinople and became a high court official and teacher. In 858 the emperor Michael III made him patriarch, though he was a layman, replacing the deposed Ignatius. The pope refused to recognise him, and the quarrel, over authority and the western addition of the *filioque* ("and the Son") to the Creed, became the "Photian schism", an early step towards the split of 1054. He was deposed in 867, restored in 877 and deposed again in 886.

As patriarch he sent the brothers Cyril and Methodius on their mission to the Slavs of Moravia in 863; for it they created the first Slavic alphabet.

His most famous work, the *Bibliotheca* or *Myriobiblon*, is a record of 279 books he had read, ranging from Christian theology to pagan history, novels and medicine, with summaries and critical comments. Many of the works it describes are lost, including parts of Diodorus, Ctesias, Arrian's history of Alexander's successors and the novels of Antonius Diogenes, so it is a crucial source for Greek literature. He also compiled a lexicon and wrote letters and sermons. His circle was at the heart of the copying of classical texts in the new minuscule script.
