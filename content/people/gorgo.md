<!-- field: summary -->
A Spartan queen, daughter of Cleomenes I and wife of Leonidas, remembered by Herodotus for her shrewdness.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the few Spartan women whose words and actions are recorded, and an emblem of the outspoken independence of Spartan women.

<!-- field: body -->
Gorgo (born around 518 BC) was the only child of King Cleomenes I of Sparta, and married her uncle Leonidas, who became king and died at Thermopylae in 480 BC. Herodotus mentions her twice, both times for her good sense.

In 499 BC Aristagoras of Miletus came to Sparta seeking help for the Ionian revolt against Persia. When Cleomenes refused, Aristagoras began offering him ever larger bribes. Gorgo, then about eight or nine years old, said: "Father, the stranger will corrupt you if you don't go away." Cleomenes left the room, and Sparta stayed out of the revolt.

Later, before Xerxes' invasion, the exiled Spartan king Demaratus, living at the Persian court, sent a warning to Sparta. He scraped the wax from a writing tablet, wrote his message on the wood beneath, and covered it with fresh wax so it would pass the guards as blank. No one in Sparta could understand it until Gorgo suggested scraping off the wax.

Plutarch preserves a famous saying attributed to her. Asked by a woman from Attica why Spartan women were the only ones who ruled their men, she replied: "Because we are the only ones who give birth to men." Such sayings, whether genuine or not, reflect the reputation of Spartan women for frankness and independence.
