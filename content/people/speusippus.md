<!-- field: summary -->
Plato's nephew and successor as head of the Academy, who replaced the Forms with numbers as first principles.

<!-- field: significance -->
Speusippus shows how quickly the Academy moved away from Plato's own doctrine, and he anticipated the classification of living things that Aristotle carried out.

<!-- field: body -->
Speusippus was the son of Plato's sister Potone, and lived in the philosopher's household. He went with Plato to Sicily and was the obvious choice as successor when Plato died in 347 BC. Diogenes Laertius records that he fell ill with paralysis in old age, and handed the school on to Xenocrates in 339.

He did not simply defend Plato's teaching. According to Aristotle, he rejected the Forms and made numbers and geometrical objects the basic realities, derived from a pair of principles, the One and multiplicity. He also denied that the One is the same as the Good, which he regarded as a later, emergent result. Aristotle complained that Speusippus's universe is "episodic", like a poor tragedy in which the episodes are not connected by any necessity.

Speusippus wrote on a huge range of subjects. His lists of similar and dissimilar things, reported by Athenaeus, mark an early attempt to classify plants and animals by their shared features, a programme that Aristotle and Theophrastus later developed.

He was also known as a defender of the claim that pleasure is not a good, and as a man of ill temper who quarrelled with Aristotle. The reports come largely from hostile anecdote, and only fragments of his work survive.
