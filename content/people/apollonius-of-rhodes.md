<!-- field: summary -->
The Alexandrian scholar-poet of the 3rd century BC, head of the Library, who wrote the Argonautica, the epic of Jason and Medea.

<!-- field: significance -->
Author of the only full Greek epic to survive between Homer and the Roman period, and a major influence on Virgil.

<!-- field: body -->
Apollonius (c. 295–215 BC) was probably born in Alexandria; he was called "of Rhodes" because he later lived on that island. Ancient biographies say he was a pupil of Callimachus, and a list of librarians on a papyrus from Oxyrhynchus confirms that he was head of the Library of Alexandria, succeeding Zenodotus, probably in the reign of Ptolemy II, and tutor to the future Ptolemy III. Later stories of a fierce quarrel with Callimachus, and of his retreat to Rhodes after his poem was poorly received, are probably invented.

His *Argonautica*, in four books, tells the voyage of Jason and the Argonauts to Colchis at the eastern end of the Black Sea to win the Golden Fleece, and their long journey home. Apollonius wrote in Homer's language and metre but in a new spirit: his Jason is an uncertain, unheroic leader, and the centre of the poem is the young Colchian princess Medea, whose love for Jason is described with deep psychological insight. The poem is also full of learned detail on geography, the origins of cults and place-names.

The Roman poet Virgil drew on it closely for the *Aeneid*, modelling Dido's passion for Aeneas on Medea's for Jason.
