<!-- field: summary -->
The Carian ruler of the fourth century BC who governed as a Persian satrap, rebuilt Halicarnassus as his capital, and was buried in the monument that gave us the word 'mausoleum'.

<!-- field: significance -->
A semi-independent ruler on the Greek frontier of Persia, whose tomb was counted among the Seven Wonders of the World.

<!-- field: body -->
Mausolus belonged to the Hecatomnid dynasty of Caria in south-western Asia Minor. As satrap, he governed in the name of the Persian king, but acted largely on his own account. About 365 BC he moved the capital to Halicarnassus on the coast and began to rebuild it with a harbour, walls and a palace, bringing in Greek architects and sculptors. He took part in the revolt of the western satraps against Artaxerxes II, but made peace with the king.

He was ambitious abroad. In 357 BC he helped Rhodes, Cos, Chios and Byzantium to break away from the Second Athenian League, and then dominated them. Mausolus died in 353 BC and was followed in turn by his wife and sister Artemisia, and by his brothers.

Artemisia began the huge tomb in Halicarnassus. Designed by Satyrus and Pytheos and carved by sculptors including Scopas, it stood for centuries, and its sculptures were removed to the British Museum in the nineteenth century.
