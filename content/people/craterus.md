The general the Macedonian soldiers loved best, whose early death removed a likely ruler of Macedon.

<!-- field: body -->
Craterus (c. 370–321 BC) was one of Alexander's most capable generals and often commanded the left wing or independent forces. At the Hydaspes in 326 BC he held the main camp opposite the Indian king Porus while Alexander crossed upstream. On the return from India he led part of the army back by an inland route.

He was known as a traditional Macedonian who disliked Alexander's adoption of Persian dress and customs; Plutarch says Alexander called Hephaestion "friend of Alexander" and Craterus "friend of the king". At the mass weddings at Susa in 324 he married a Persian princess, Amastris. That year Alexander sent him to lead about 10,000 discharged veterans home and to take over Macedon from Antipater.

He was still in Cilicia when Alexander died. He crossed to Europe and helped Antipater crush the Greek revolt in the Lamian War at Crannon in 322, and married Antipater's daughter Phila. In the first war of the Successors he crossed into Asia against Perdiccas' ally Eumenes, hoping the Macedonian troops would desert to him. In the battle, in 321 or 320, his horse fell and he was trampled; Eumenes found him dying and mourned him.
