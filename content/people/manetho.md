<!-- field: summary -->
The Egyptian priest who wrote a history of Egypt in Greek in the early 3rd century BC and divided the pharaohs into dynasties.

<!-- field: significance -->
His thirty dynasties are still the framework of Egyptian history.

<!-- field: body -->
Manetho was a priest from Sebennytos in the Nile Delta, active under Ptolemy I and Ptolemy II. He wrote in Greek, for the new Greek rulers and settlers, a history of Egypt called the *Aegyptiaca*, in three books, based on temple records and king lists. Plutarch says he was one of the advisers of Ptolemy I in establishing the cult of Serapis.

Manetho grouped the rulers of Egypt from the legendary Menes, who united the country, to the Persian conquest into thirty dynasties (a thirty-first was added later). This scheme, though imperfect, is still the basis of Egyptian chronology: Egyptologists speak of the 4th Dynasty of the pyramid-builders or the 18th Dynasty of Tutankhamun because of Manetho.

His work does not survive. The Jewish historian Josephus quoted passages from it in the 1st century AD, including an account of the Hyksos rulers, and Christian chronographers such as Julius Africanus and Eusebius preserved summaries of the king lists, which differ from one another. Manetho also wrote on Egyptian religion and festivals, and criticised Herodotus' account of Egypt.
