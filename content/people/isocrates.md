<!-- field: summary -->
Athenian teacher of rhetoric who urged the Greeks to unite under a strong leader and invade Persia — and looked to Philip II to do it.

<!-- field: significance -->
Formulated the Panhellenic ideology that Philip and Alexander used to justify the conquest of Persia.

<!-- field: body -->
Isocrates had a weak voice and never spoke in the assembly; he taught and published instead, and his school shaped Greek education for centuries. His *Panegyricus* (380 BC) argued that Greek unity could only be achieved against a common enemy.

In the *Address to Philip* (346 BC) he named the man: Philip should reconcile the leading cities and lead a united campaign into Asia. Whether this made him a visionary or a collaborator has been argued ever since — Demosthenes was making exactly the opposite case at exactly the same time.

He reportedly died shortly after Chaeronea, aged 98.
