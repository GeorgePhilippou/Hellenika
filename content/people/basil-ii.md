<!-- field: summary -->
The emperor (976–1025) who conquered Bulgaria and brought the Byzantine Empire to its medieval peak.

<!-- field: significance -->
Under him Byzantium was the strongest power in Europe and the Near East, and the Rus' adopted Orthodox Christianity.

<!-- field: body -->
Basil II (958–1025) of the Macedonian dynasty was crowned co-emperor as a small child and took full power in 976. His early years were spent fighting rebel generals from the great landowning families of Asia Minor, defeating them with the help of 6,000 Varangian warriors sent by Prince Vladimir of Kiev. In return Basil gave Vladimir his sister Anna in marriage, and around 988 Vladimir and his people were baptised — the beginning of Russian Orthodox Christianity. The Varangians became the emperor's elite guard.

Basil then fought a long war against the Bulgarian tsar Samuel, campaigning almost every year. In 1014 he trapped the Bulgarian army at Kleidion; a later chronicler says he blinded 15,000 prisoners, leaving one eye to every hundredth man to lead them home, and that Samuel died of shock when he saw them. The figure is probably exaggerated. By 1018 Bulgaria was annexed. On his way home Basil visited Athens to give thanks in the Parthenon, then a church of the Virgin.

He never married, lived like a soldier, and left the treasury full. He also extended Byzantine rule in Armenia and Syria. He died in 1025 while planning an expedition to Sicily.
