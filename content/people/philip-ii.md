<!-- field: summary -->
The king who reforged Macedon's army, subdued Greece at Chaeronea, and planned the invasion of Persia his son carried out.

<!-- field: significance -->
Built the instrument of conquest. Alexander's campaigns are inconceivable without Philip's army, treasury and diplomatic groundwork.

<!-- field: body -->
Philip took a broken kingdom in 359 BC and made it the strongest power in Europe within two decades. The military reforms were fundamental: the sarissa pike phalanx, the Companion Cavalry as a shock arm, professional year-round soldiers, and a siege train with torsion catapults.

He was also a formidable diplomat and a systematic briber — Demosthenes complained that no fortress was impregnable if a mule loaded with silver could reach the gate. He married at least seven times, each marriage a treaty.

He paid physically: he lost an eye at Methone, and his skeleton would be expected to show a severe leg wound. In 336 BC, at the wedding of his daughter, he was killed by his bodyguard Pausanias. Whether Olympias or Alexander was involved has never been settled.
