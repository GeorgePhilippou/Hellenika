<!-- field: summary -->
Overseer of the Parthenon sculptures and maker of the colossal Zeus at Olympia, one of the Seven Wonders.

<!-- field: significance -->
Defined the visual language of the Classical style — and none of his major works survives except at second hand.

<!-- field: body -->
Pheidias (active c. 465–430 BC) was an Athenian sculptor, the most famous artist of Classical Greece. Pericles put him in charge of the sculpture of the Acropolis building programme.

He made the Athena Parthenos, a statue about 12 m tall of gold and ivory over a wooden core, which stood inside the Parthenon from 438 BC. His workshop produced the Parthenon's frieze, metopes and pediment sculptures, designed under his direction though carved by many hands. Earlier, he made the great bronze Athena Promachos that stood on the Acropolis. At Olympia he made the seated Zeus in gold and ivory, about 12 m high, counted among the Seven Wonders of the ancient world. His workshop there has been excavated, with tools, clay moulds for the statue's drapery and a cup inscribed "I belong to Pheidias".

The great statues are all lost, melted down or destroyed in later antiquity, and are known only from descriptions, Roman copies and coins. His style survives in the Parthenon sculptures, which defined the calm, idealised manner of High Classical art.

Late in his career he was prosecuted in Athens, apparently for stealing gold or ivory meant for the Athena; ancient writers thought the charge was really aimed at Pericles. Accounts disagree on whether he died in prison in Athens or in exile at Olympia.
