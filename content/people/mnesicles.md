<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian architect who designed the Propylaea, the monumental gateway to the Acropolis, in 437–432 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
His ingenious design fitted a grand symmetrical gateway onto an uneven slope, and became a model for gateways in later times.

<!-- field: body -->
Mnesicles is known only from Plutarch, who says that he built the Propylaea in five years, and from the building itself. Work began in 437 BC, immediately after the Parthenon was structurally complete, and stopped in 432, on the eve of the Peloponnesian War.

His design solved a difficult problem: the gateway had to climb the steep west slope of the Acropolis and connect with existing shrines and the bastion of Athena Nike. Mnesicles combined a Doric façade, matching the Parthenon, with taller Ionic columns inside the central passage, an early mix of the two orders in one building. Wings on either side were planned, but only partly built; the north wing housed a picture gallery. Unfinished details, such as the lifting bosses left on some blocks, show where the work stopped.

Because of stylistic links, he has sometimes been credited with the Erechtheion, built from the 420s, but there is no direct evidence.
