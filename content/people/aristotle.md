<!-- field: summary -->
Plato's student, Alexander's tutor, founder of the Lyceum, and the most systematically encyclopaedic mind of antiquity.

<!-- field: significance -->
Effectively founded logic, biology, literary criticism and political science as distinct disciplines, and dominated European and Islamic thought for nearly two thousand years.

<!-- field: body -->
Aristotle studied under Plato for twenty years, then broke with the theory of Forms: for him, form is not in a separate realm but in the thing itself. That shift turns philosophy towards empirical investigation of the world.

He collected and dissected specimens; his marine biology at Lesbos is accurate enough that some of his observations — the hectocotyl arm of the octopus, the placental dogfish — were dismissed for centuries and only confirmed in the nineteenth. He and his school also collected the constitutions of 158 cities, of which the *Constitution of the Athenians* survives on papyrus.

He tutored the young Alexander at Mieza from 343 BC. After Alexander's death, anti-Macedonian feeling forced him to leave Athens — reportedly saying he would not let the city sin twice against philosophy.
