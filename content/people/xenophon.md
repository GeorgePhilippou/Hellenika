<!-- field: summary -->
Soldier, student of Socrates and author of the Anabasis — the first-person account of ten thousand Greek mercenaries fighting their way out of Persia.

<!-- field: significance -->
Wrote the earliest surviving military memoir, and provides the main continuation of Greek history after Thucydides breaks off.

<!-- field: body -->
Xenophon joined a mercenary army hired by Cyrus the Younger to overthrow his brother, the Persian king. Cyrus was killed at Cunaxa in 401 BC and the Greek generals were seized under truce and executed. Stranded a thousand miles inside hostile territory, the survivors elected new commanders — Xenophon among them — and marched north through Armenian winter to the Black Sea. Their shout of "Thalatta! Thalatta!" — the sea, the sea — on first sighting it is among the most famous moments in Greek literature.

He later fought for Sparta, was exiled from Athens, and wrote widely: the *Hellenica*, Socratic memoirs, a treatise on horsemanship and one on estate management. His demonstration that a Greek army could operate deep inside Persia was not lost on Philip and Alexander.
