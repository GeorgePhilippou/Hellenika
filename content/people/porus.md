<!-- field: summary -->
Indian king who fought Alexander at the Hydaspes with war elephants, lost, and was reinstated as a satrap.

<!-- field: significance -->
His resistance marked the effective eastern limit of Alexander's conquest and produced one of the most quoted exchanges in ancient history.

<!-- field: body -->
Porus met Alexander at the Hydaspes in 326 BC with around 200 elephants and a large army, on the far bank of a river swollen by monsoon rain. Alexander crossed upstream at night in a storm, concealing the move with repeated feints. The battle was the hardest of his career; the elephants panicked and trampled troops on both sides.

Captured and brought before Alexander, Porus was asked how he wished to be treated. He answered: as a king. Alexander restored his kingdom and enlarged it.

The practical reason is as important as the gesture — Alexander needed a strong local ruler to hold the region, and Porus was the obvious candidate. The battle also killed Bucephalus, and Alexander founded a city in his horse's name.
