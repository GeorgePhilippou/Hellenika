<!-- field: summary -->
The statesman who built the Achaean League into the leading power in the Peloponnese in the 3rd century BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The architect of the Achaean League, whose memoirs shaped Polybius' history of the age.

<!-- field: body -->
Aratus (271–213 BC) was born at Sicyon in the northern Peloponnese. As a child he escaped when a tyrant killed his father. In 251 BC, aged twenty, he and a band of exiles scaled the walls of Sicyon at night and overthrew the tyrant Nicocles. He then brought the city into the Achaean League, a federation of small cities of the region.

From 245 he was elected the League's general (*stratēgos*) almost every other year, the maximum the law allowed. His greatest exploit came in 243, when he took by surprise the Acrocorinth, the citadel of Corinth, which Macedon had used to dominate the Peloponnese. Corinth and many other cities joined the League, which became the strongest power in the region.

Aratus was a better diplomat than general. When Cleomenes III of Sparta defeated the League repeatedly in the 220s, Aratus reversed his life's policy and called in Antigonus III Doson of Macedon, handing back the Acrocorinth. After the victory at Sellasia in 222 the League became a Macedonian ally. Aratus advised the young Philip V but fell out with him, and died in 213, believed to have been poisoned on Philip's orders. His memoirs were a main source for Polybius.
