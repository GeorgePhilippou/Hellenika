<!-- field: summary -->
The Byzantine monk and scholar of around 1300 who edited the Greek Anthology, recovered Ptolemy's Geography and translated Latin classics into Greek.

<!-- field: significance -->
A leading figure of the Palaiologan renaissance, through whom many Greek texts reached the West.

<!-- field: body -->
Maximos Planoudes (c. 1255–1305) was born Manuel Planoudes in Nicomedia and worked in Constantinople after its recovery from the Latins in 1261, when Byzantine scholars set about restoring the classical heritage damaged in 1204. He became a monk, taking the name Maximos, ran a school at a monastery in the capital, and went on an embassy to Venice in 1296.

He edited and copied many classical authors, including Plutarch's *Moralia*, Aratus and the Greek mathematicians. Around 1300 he compiled an anthology of Greek epigrams, the *Planudean Anthology*; his own autograph manuscript is in the Marciana Library in Venice. It was the form in which the Greek epigram was known in the West until the discovery of the fuller Palatine Anthology in 1606.

He searched for and found a manuscript of Ptolemy's *Geography* without its maps, and had them redrawn from Ptolemy's coordinates; copies reached Italy in the early 15th century and transformed Renaissance mapmaking. Unusually for a Byzantine, he knew Latin well, and translated into Greek works by Ovid, Cicero, Augustine and Boethius. He also wrote a popular *Life of Aesop*.
