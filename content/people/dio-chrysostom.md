<!-- field: summary -->
The Greek orator from Bithynia (c. AD 40–115) who was exiled by Domitian, wandered as a philosopher, and became a friend of Trajan.

<!-- field: significance -->
An early star of the Second Sophistic, whose speeches show how Greek cities and intellectuals lived under Roman rule.

<!-- field: body -->
Dio (c. 40–115) was born to a wealthy family at Prusa in Bithynia, in north-western Asia Minor. He made his name as a sophist in Rome, but around 82 the emperor Domitian banished him from Italy and his homeland, probably because of his connection with a disgraced aristocrat. For some fourteen years he wandered the frontier lands from Greece to the Black Sea, dressed as a poor philosopher in the manner of Diogenes the Cynic, working with his hands and talking about virtue.

When Domitian was murdered in 96, Dio returned and became a friend of the new emperors Nerva and Trajan. In his last years he was involved in the bitter politics of Prusa and its rivalry with neighbouring cities.

About eighty speeches survive. Some advise cities to live in harmony or advise Trajan on good kingship. The *Euboean Oration* describes the simple life of a family of hunters on Euboea. The *Olympic Oration*, given at the Games, discusses Phidias' gold-and-ivory statue of Zeus and how human beings form their idea of god. In the *Borystheniticus* he describes a Greek city on the Black Sea where everyone still knew Homer by heart. Later writers called him *Chrysostomos*, "golden-mouthed".
