<!-- field: summary -->
An Athenian orator and Peripatetic philosopher who governed Athens for Cassander from 317 to 307 BC, then advised Ptolemy I in Alexandria.

<!-- field: significance -->
A link between Aristotle's school and the Library of Alexandria, and the last ruler of Athens in the classical style.

<!-- field: body -->
Demetrius was born in Phalerum, the port district of Athens, and studied with Theophrastus. He became a well-known orator and writer, and a member of the philosophical circle around Aristotle's school.

In 317 BC Cassander, then master of Macedonia, installed Demetrius to govern Athens. For ten years he ran the city as a mild oligarchy, limiting the vote to those with a property qualification and appointing officials to control extravagance. Athenians erected hundreds of statues of him, which were torn down and melted in 307 BC when Demetrius Poliorcetes captured the city and restored the democracy. Demetrius was condemned in absentia, fled and later joined the court of Ptolemy I in Alexandria.

Later sources say he advised Ptolemy on the founding of the Library. Most modern historians doubt this, because the Library probably took shape under Ptolemy II, with whom Demetrius fell out. The story of the translation of the Septuagint, told in the Letter of Aristeas, also names him but is generally thought to be legendary.
