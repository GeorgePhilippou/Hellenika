<!-- field: summary -->
The tyrant of Gela and Syracuse who made Syracuse the greatest Greek city in the west and defeated Carthage at Himera in 480 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
Founder of Syracusan power, whose victory at Himera was seen as the western counterpart of Salamis.

<!-- field: body -->
Gelon was a cavalry commander under Hippocrates, tyrant of Gela in southern Sicily, and seized power there after his death around 491 BC. In 485 the aristocrats of Syracuse, expelled by the common people, called on him for help; he took the city and made it his capital, leaving Gela to his brother Hiero.

He greatly enlarged Syracuse, moving there the populations of several other cities, including half of Gela and the wealthy citizens of Camarina and Megara Hyblaea. He built up a large army and navy.

When Xerxes invaded Greece in 480 BC, Herodotus says, the Greeks asked Gelon for help; he offered a large force but demanded overall command, and was refused. In the same year a great Carthaginian army landed in Sicily and besieged Himera. Gelon and his ally Theron, tyrant of Akragas, destroyed it; the Carthaginian general Hamilcar was killed. Later Greeks claimed the battle was fought on the same day as Salamis. With the spoils Gelon dedicated a golden tripod at Delphi and built temples in Syracuse.

Gelon was popular, and when he died in 478 BC his brother Hiero I succeeded him.
