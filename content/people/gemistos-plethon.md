<!-- field: summary -->
The Byzantine philosopher of Mystras (c. 1355–1452) who revived Plato and inspired the Platonism of Renaissance Florence.

<!-- field: significance -->
The last great original thinker of Byzantium, who carried Plato to the Italian Renaissance and dreamed of a pagan Hellenic revival.

<!-- field: body -->
George Gemistos (c. 1355–1452), who later took the name Plethon — a classical-sounding equivalent of his surname, both meaning "full" — was born in Constantinople. Around 1409 he settled at Mystras, the capital of the Byzantine Peloponnese, where he taught for over forty years.

He urged the emperor Manuel II and the Despot of the Morea to save the Peloponnese by reforming it on the model of ancient Sparta and Plato's *Republic*, with a citizen army and land held in common. He was the first Byzantine to call his people *Hellenes* rather than Romans, in a positive sense.

In 1438–39, already over eighty, he went with the Byzantine delegation to the Council of Ferrara–Florence, which tried to reunite the Greek and Latin churches. In Florence he lectured on the superiority of Plato over Aristotle. Marsilio Ficino later wrote that Cosimo de' Medici, hearing him, conceived the idea of a Platonic Academy in Florence.

In secret Plethon wrote a *Book of Laws* describing a new religion based on the Greek gods, with Zeus at its head. After his death the patriarch Gennadios Scholarios had it burned, though fragments survive. In 1466 the Italian condottiere Sigismondo Malatesta brought his remains from Mystras to Rimini.
