<!-- field: summary -->
An Athenian politician of the late fifth century BC who was active in two oligarchic regimes and was killed by Critias in 404/403 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The central figure in the political upheavals at the end of the Peloponnesian War, and a symbol of moderate oligarchy.

<!-- field: body -->
Theramenes, son of Hagnon, came to prominence in 411 BC, when he helped overthrow the democracy and install the Four Hundred. He broke with the extremists, who fortified Piraeus and negotiated with Sparta, and joined the group that established the Five Thousand, a more moderate government of those who could provide their own arms.

As trierarch at Arginusae in 406 BC he was ordered to pick up survivors after the storm and failed, then shifted blame onto the generals, who were tried and executed. After Aegospotami, he travelled to Lysander to negotiate the terms of surrender, was away for three months and returned with the peace that ended the war.

He was chosen as one of the Thirty. He soon opposed their killings and confiscations, and Critias had him removed from the citizen list and condemned him. According to Xenophon, Theramenes drank the hemlock and flicked the last drops, saying 'This is for the beautiful Critias.' Later writers, among them Aristotle, saw him as a patriot of a moderate constitution, but his critics saw a turncoat.
