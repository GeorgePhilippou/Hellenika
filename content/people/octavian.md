Caesar's adopted heir who defeated Antony and Cleopatra at Actium, annexed Egypt in 30 BC and became Augustus, the first Roman emperor.

<!-- field: significance -->
The victor who ended the last Hellenistic kingdom and brought the entire Greek world under one Roman ruler.

<!-- field: body -->
Gaius Octavius (63 BC–AD 14), known as Octavian, was the great-nephew and adopted son of Julius Caesar. After Caesar's murder in 44 BC he fought to take Caesar's place, joining Mark Antony to defeat the assassins at Philippi in 42 BC, then dividing the Roman world with him, Octavian taking the West and Antony the East.

As their rivalry grew, Octavian turned Roman opinion against Antony and his partner Cleopatra VII of Egypt, presenting the coming war as Rome against a foreign queen. In 31 BC his fleet, commanded by Marcus Agrippa, defeated Antony and Cleopatra at Actium off north-western Greece. The next year he took Alexandria; Antony and Cleopatra died, Caesarion was killed, and Egypt became a Roman province under a governor answerable to Octavian himself, ending the last of the Hellenistic kingdoms.

At Alexandria he visited the tomb of Alexander the Great; asked if he wished to see the tombs of the Ptolemies too, he reportedly replied that he had come to see a king, not corpses. To celebrate Actium he founded the city of Nicopolis, "Victory City", nearby.

In 27 BC the Senate gave him the name Augustus, and he ruled as the first Roman emperor until AD 14. Under him and his successors the Greek East enjoyed two centuries of peace, while Greek culture shaped Rome itself.
