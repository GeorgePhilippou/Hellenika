<!-- field: summary -->
Student of Socrates, founder of the Academy, and author of the dialogues that set the agenda for Western philosophy.

<!-- field: significance -->
Whitehead's remark that European philosophy is footnotes to Plato overstates the case, but not by much: metaphysics, epistemology, ethics and political theory all take their initial shape here.

<!-- field: body -->
Plato founded the Academy around 387 BC in a grove outside Athens — arguably the first institution of higher learning in Europe, and it operated for nearly three centuries.

His central doctrine is the theory of Forms: the changing things we perceive are imperfect instances of perfect, unchanging realities grasped by reason alone. The *Republic* builds a just city as a model for the just soul, argues for rule by philosopher-kings, and contains the Allegory of the Cave. His political proposals are genuinely authoritarian — censorship of poetry, a rigid class structure, state control of reproduction — and reading him as a straightforward democrat requires ignoring the text.

Unusually for an ancient author, his complete works survive.
