<!-- field: summary -->
Achaemenid king who organised the empire into satrapies, built Persepolis and the Royal Road, and lost at Marathon.

<!-- field: significance -->
His administrative system held an empire of perhaps 25 million people together, and his invasion of Greece began the Persian Wars.

<!-- field: body -->
Darius took power in 522 BC after a disputed succession he justified at length in the trilingual Behistun inscription, carved 100 metres up a cliff — the text that gave nineteenth-century scholars the key to cuneiform.

He divided the empire into about twenty satrapies with fixed tribute, standardised coinage with the gold daric, and built the Royal Road from Sardis to Susa, along which relay couriers covered 2,700 km in a week. Herodotus' description of them is the source of the unofficial motto of the US Postal Service.

The Ionian Revolt of 499 BC, supported by Athens and Eretria, provoked his intervention in Greece. His punitive expedition was defeated at Marathon in 490 BC. He died preparing a larger invasion, which his son Xerxes carried out.
