<!-- field: summary -->
The architect of the Parthenon and of the temple of Apollo at Bassae, the most celebrated Greek architect of the Classical age.

<!-- field: significance -->
The designer of the most famous building of antiquity.

<!-- field: body -->
Iktinos was an architect working in the mid-5th century BC. With Kallikrates he designed the Parthenon on the Acropolis of Athens, built from 447 to 432 BC under Pericles, with the sculptor Pheidias in charge of its decoration. The Roman architect Vitruvius says Iktinos wrote a book about the temple with a man named Karpion; it is lost.

Iktinos is credited with the Parthenon's bold design: an unusually wide temple, eight columns across instead of the usual six, so that it could house Pheidias' enormous statue of Athena, combining the Doric order outside with Ionic features such as a continuous frieze, and refined throughout by subtle curves and inclinations.

Pausanias names Iktinos as the architect of the temple of Apollo Epikourios at Bassae in Arcadia, built in the later 5th century BC, which has the earliest known Corinthian column. Plutarch also says he worked on the Telesterion, the great hall of initiation at Eleusis.

Almost nothing else is known about his life.
