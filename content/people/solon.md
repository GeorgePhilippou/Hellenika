<!-- field: summary -->
Athenian lawgiver of 594 BC who cancelled debts, abolished debt-slavery and opened political office by wealth rather than birth.

<!-- field: significance -->
His reforms broke the aristocratic monopoly on Athenian politics and laid the legal groundwork that Cleisthenes later turned into democracy.

<!-- field: body -->
Solon (c. 638–558 BC) was an Athenian statesman and poet, appointed archon in 594 BC with special powers to resolve a crisis. Poor farmers were falling into debt to the rich, losing their land and being enslaved for debt or sold abroad, and Athens was close to civil war.

His *seisachtheia*, "shaking off of burdens", cancelled existing debts secured on land or persons, freed Athenians enslaved for debt and brought back those sold abroad, and banned debt slavery for the future. He divided the citizens into four classes by wealth, measured in farm produce, instead of birth: the highest offices were open only to the richest, but even the poorest, the *thetes*, could attend the Assembly and sit in the popular law court. Any citizen could now bring a prosecution on behalf of someone wronged. He also replaced most of Draco's harsh law code.

Having passed his laws, Solon left Athens for ten years, so that he could not be pressured into changing them. Fragments of his own poetry survive, in which he defends having refused to satisfy either the rich or the poor completely.

His reforms did not end the conflict — Peisistratus later seized power as tyrant — but they broke the aristocracy's monopoly of power and laid the foundations on which Cleisthenes later built Athenian democracy.
