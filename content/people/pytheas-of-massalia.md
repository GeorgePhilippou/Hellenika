<!-- field: summary -->
A Greek navigator from Massalia who sailed to Britain and the far north about 325 BC and wrote an account known today only from quotations.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first Greek to record the Atlantic coasts of north-west Europe and to describe the midnight sun and the effect of the moon on tides.

<!-- field: body -->
Pytheas lived in Massalia (Marseille), a Greek colony in southern Gaul. Around 325 BC, perhaps funded by his city, he took a voyage along the Atlantic coast, circled Britain and reported on its tin trade, its peoples, climate and farming. He measured the sun's height with a gnomon and gave latitudes for several places, and noted the movements of the tides.

He reported that after six days' sail north of Britain there was a land called Thule, where in midsummer the night was very short or absent, and beyond it a sea that was neither land, sea nor air but a mixture, perhaps drift ice. He mentioned the amber coast on the North Sea.

Pytheas' book *On the Ocean* is lost. Polybius and Strabo, who preserve many extracts, doubted him, but Eratosthenes and Hipparchus used his observations. Modern scholars mostly believe the voyage took place, though the identity of Thule is uncertain and has been argued to be Iceland, Norway or the Shetlands.
