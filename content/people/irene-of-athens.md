<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian woman who became empress, restored the icons in 787 and ruled the Byzantine Empire alone from 797 to 802.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first woman to rule the Roman empire as sole sovereign, whose reign helped provoke the crowning of a rival emperor in the west.

<!-- field: body -->
Irene (c. 752–803) came from a noble family of Athens, then a small provincial town. In 769 she was brought to Constantinople to marry Leo, son of the iconoclast emperor Constantine V. When Leo IV died in 780, she became regent for their nine-year-old son Constantine VI.

Irene favoured the veneration of icons. After a first attempt was broken up by iconoclast soldiers, she summoned the Seventh Ecumenical Council at Nicaea in 787, which restored icons to Christian worship.

As Constantine grew up, mother and son struggled for power. In 797 her supporters seized him and blinded him so savagely that he died. Irene then ruled alone, sometimes styling herself *basileus*, "emperor", rather than empress. In the west, the claim that the imperial throne was vacant because it was held by a woman was one argument for Pope Leo III's coronation of Charlemagne as emperor on Christmas Day 800; a later chronicler says Charlemagne even proposed marriage to her. In 802 her finance minister Nikephoros overthrew her, and she died in exile on Lesbos the next year. For restoring the icons, the Orthodox Church honours her as a saint.
