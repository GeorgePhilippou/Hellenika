<!-- field: summary -->
The emperor (610–641) who saved the empire from Persia, only to lose Syria and Egypt to the Arabs, and under whom Byzantium became fully Greek.

<!-- field: significance -->
His reign marks the turn from the late Roman to the medieval Greek empire.

<!-- field: body -->
Heraclius (c. 575–641) was the son of the governor of Africa. In 610 he sailed to Constantinople, overthrew the tyrant Phocas and became emperor. The empire was in crisis: the Persians under Khosrow II had overrun Syria, took Jerusalem in 614 with the relic of the True Cross, and conquered Egypt, while Avars and Slavs ravaged the Balkans.

Heraclius rebuilt the army and in 622 took the war to Persia, campaigning for years in Armenia and Mesopotamia. In 626, while he was away, the Avars and Persians besieged Constantinople together, but the city held. In 627 he won a decisive victory near Nineveh, and the Persian king was overthrown. In 630 Heraclius restored the True Cross to Jerusalem in triumph.

The victory was short-lived. Exhausted by the war, both empires were attacked by Arab armies newly united by Islam. After the battle of the Yarmuk in 636, Syria and Palestine were lost; Egypt, with Alexandria, fell in 641–642. In his reign Greek finally replaced Latin as the language of law and government, and from 629 the emperor used the Greek title *basileus*, "king". He died in 641.
