<!-- field: summary -->
The king of Macedon from 277 to 239 BC who established the Antigonid dynasty firmly on the Macedonian throne.

<!-- field: significance -->
Restored Macedon after the Gallic invasion and made it, for a century, one of the three great Hellenistic kingdoms.

<!-- field: body -->
Antigonus II (c. 319–239 BC) was the son of Demetrius Poliorcetes and grandson of Antigonus the One-Eyed. His nickname Gonatas is of uncertain meaning. When his father was captured by Seleucus in 285, he held on to a few Greek cities, including Corinth and Demetrias, but not Macedon.

In 279 BC Gauls invaded Macedon and Greece, killing the king Ptolemy Keraunos and plundering the country. In 277 Antigonus defeated a Gallic army near Lysimachia in Thrace, and on the strength of this victory became king of Macedon. He was briefly driven out by Pyrrhus in 274–272 but recovered his throne.

From his capital at Pella he controlled Greece through garrisons at Corinth, Chalcis and the Piraeus. In the Chremonidean War (c. 267–261) he defeated an alliance of Athens and Sparta backed by Ptolemy II and took Athens. In 243 Aratus of Sicyon seized the Acrocorinth from his garrison.

Antigonus had studied philosophy in Athens and admired Zeno the Stoic, whom he invited to his court; he invited poets such as Aratus of Soli, author of the *Phaenomena*. He is said to have called kingship "noble servitude". He died in 239 BC aged about eighty.
