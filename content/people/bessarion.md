<!-- field: summary -->
The Byzantine scholar who became a Roman cardinal and gave his great library of Greek manuscripts to Venice in 1468.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most important figure in the transfer of Greek learning to Renaissance Italy; his manuscripts are the core of the Marciana Library.

<!-- field: body -->
Bessarion (c. 1403–1472) was born at Trebizond on the Black Sea and educated in Constantinople. He became a monk, then studied philosophy with Plethon at Mystras. In 1437 he was made metropolitan bishop of Nicaea and went to the Council of Ferrara–Florence, where he became one of the leading supporters of the union of the Greek and Latin churches, agreed in 1439. The union was rejected in Byzantium, but Pope Eugenius IV made Bessarion a cardinal, and he settled in Italy.

In Rome his house became a centre for Greek and Italian scholars. He worked for a crusade to save Byzantium and, after 1453, to recover it. In his book *Against the Slanderer of Plato* (1469) he defended Plato against Aristotelian critics.

After the fall of Constantinople he set out to collect copies of as many Greek works as possible, so that the literature of his people would not perish. In 1468 he gave his library — about a thousand manuscripts, most of them Greek, including the Venetus A of the *Iliad* — to the Republic of Venice, "a second Byzantium" as he called it. They became the foundation of the Biblioteca Marciana. Venice soon became the centre of Greek printing. He died at Ravenna in 1472.
