<!-- field: summary -->
The Greek secretary of Philip and Alexander who became one of the ablest generals of the wars of the Successors, loyal to Alexander's family to the end.

<!-- field: significance -->
The one Successor who fought for the royal house rather than for himself; as a Greek, he could never command Macedonians as an equal.

<!-- field: body -->
Eumenes (c. 362–316 BC) came from Cardia, a Greek city on the Thracian Chersonese. He served as chief secretary to Philip II and then to Alexander, keeping the royal journals, and late in the campaign was given a cavalry command.

At the settlement after Alexander's death in 323 he received Cappadocia and Paphlagonia, still unconquered. He supported Perdiccas, and in 321 or 320 BC defeated and killed the popular Macedonian general Craterus in Asia Minor. After Perdiccas' murder, the other generals condemned Eumenes to death.

For the next four years he fought Antigonus, the strongest of the Successors, in the name of the kings Philip III and Alexander IV and of Olympias. He held out under siege at Nora, then took command of the Silver Shields, Alexander's veteran infantry, in the east. To keep their loyalty he claimed that Alexander's spirit presided over his councils, held before an empty throne. In 317 and 316 he fought two great battles against Antigonus in Iran, at Paraetacene and Gabiene. After Gabiene, the Silver Shields, whose families and baggage had been captured, handed Eumenes over to Antigonus in exchange for them, and he was put to death.
