<!-- field: summary -->
The greatest mathematician and engineer of antiquity — founder of hydrostatics, and the man who came closest to inventing calculus.

<!-- field: significance -->
His method of exhaustion anticipates integration by nearly two millennia, and his mechanics remained unsurpassed until the seventeenth century.

<!-- field: body -->
Archimedes determined the value of π between 3¹⁰/₇₁ and 3¹/₇, found the volume and surface area of the sphere in terms of the circumscribing cylinder — he asked for that diagram on his tomb — and established the law of the lever and the principle of buoyancy.

His siege engines defended Syracuse against the Romans for two years: cranes that lifted ships out of the water, and catapults ranged for every distance. The "burning mirrors" story is late and physically doubtful.

He was killed in 212 BC by a Roman soldier during the sack, reportedly while working on a diagram. In 1906 a medieval prayer book was found to be a palimpsest over erased Archimedes texts, including *The Method*, in which he explains how he used mechanical reasoning and indivisibles to discover results before proving them rigorously.
