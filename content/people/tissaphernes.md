<!-- field: summary -->
The Persian satrap who played Athens and Sparta against each other in the last decade of the Peloponnesian War and was killed on the king's orders in 395 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The key Persian figure in Greek affairs at the end of the fifth century BC.

<!-- field: body -->
Tissaphernes governed Lydia and Caria from Sardis from about 413 BC. When the Spartans asked for Persian money, he made a succession of treaties with them in 412 and 411 BC, but the terms conceded Persian rights over the Asian Greek cities, and Sparta found the funds slow to arrive. Thucydides records that he took the advice of the exiled Alcibiades to keep the Greeks fighting without giving either side a decisive advantage.

His position was challenged in 407 BC when Cyrus the Younger was sent to take over command of the western satrapies. In 401 BC he supported Artaxerxes II at Cunaxa, and afterward he invited the Greek generals to a parley and seized and killed them, leaving the Ten Thousand leaderless.

In 396–395 BC the Spartan king Agesilaus invaded Asia Minor and defeated him near Sardis. Queen mother Parysatis, who hated him, saw to it that he was replaced and put to death by Tithraustes in 395 BC.
