<!-- field: summary -->
The Roman general who ruled the eastern Mediterranean, allied with Cleopatra VII, and lost the struggle for Rome to Octavian at Actium.

<!-- field: significance -->
His alliance with Cleopatra was the last attempt to build a power centred on the Greek East, and its defeat ended the Hellenistic age.

<!-- field: body -->
Marcus Antonius (83–30 BC) was a Roman general and a close ally of Julius Caesar. After Caesar's murder in 44 BC he joined Octavian and Lepidus in the Second Triumvirate, and in 42 BC he and Octavian defeated Caesar's assassins, Brutus and Cassius, at Philippi in Macedonia.

Antony took charge of Rome's eastern provinces. He lived for a time in Athens, where he was honoured as the "New Dionysus" and took part in Greek festivals. In 41 BC he summoned Cleopatra VII to Tarsus; she arrived on a gilded barge dressed as Aphrodite, and they became political and personal partners. They had three children. Antony's invasion of Parthia in 36 BC failed badly. In 34 BC, in the "Donations of Alexandria", he granted Roman eastern territories to Cleopatra and her children, which Octavian used to portray him in Rome as a traitor under the spell of a foreign queen.

Octavian declared war on Cleopatra in 32 BC. In 31 BC his admiral Agrippa defeated Antony and Cleopatra's fleet at Actium off north-western Greece. They escaped to Egypt, but as Octavian took Alexandria in 30 BC, Antony fell on his sword on a false report that Cleopatra was dead.

Shakespeare's *Antony and Cleopatra*, based on Plutarch, made their story famous.
