<!-- field: summary -->
The Hellenistic poet from Syracuse, active around 270 BC, who invented pastoral poetry.

<!-- field: significance -->
The founder of the pastoral tradition, from Virgil's Eclogues to Renaissance and modern poetry of the countryside.

<!-- field: body -->
Theocritus came from Syracuse and was active in the first half of the 3rd century BC. He seems to have spent time on the island of Kos and at Alexandria. Two of his poems are bids for royal patronage: *Idyll* 16 appeals, apparently without success, to Hiero II of Syracuse, while *Idyll* 17 is a poem of praise for Ptolemy II.

About thirty poems are attributed to him, called *Idylls* ("little pieces"), though some are not his. The most famous are the bucolic poems, set among the herdsmen of Sicily and southern Italy. Shepherds and goatherds compete in songs, mourn the dead herdsman Daphnis, or suffer unrequited love; the Cyclops Polyphemus woos the sea-nymph Galatea. Written in a literary Doric dialect and in the hexameter verse of epic, they combine scholarly refinement with rustic detail, and invented a new kind of poetry: the pastoral.

Other poems are scenes of city life, such as *Idyll* 15, in which two Syracusan housewives in Alexandria push through the crowds to see the festival of Adonis in the royal palace, or the story of a woman who uses magic to win back her lover (*Idyll* 2). The Roman poet Virgil modelled his *Eclogues* on Theocritus.
