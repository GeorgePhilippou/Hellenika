<!-- field: summary -->
The actress who became Justinian's empress and partner in power, famous for her courage in the Nika riots of 532.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the most powerful women in Byzantine history, whose portrait in mosaic at Ravenna is among the best-known images of the age.

<!-- field: body -->
Theodora (c. 497–548) was the daughter of a bear-keeper of the Hippodrome in Constantinople, and as a girl became an actress and dancer — a profession then considered close to prostitution. The historian Procopius, in his *Secret History*, a work of venomous gossip written in secret, describes her youth in scandalous detail; how much is true cannot be known. Later she converted to the Monophysite form of Christianity, which she supported throughout her life.

The heir to the throne, Justinian, fell in love with her, and a law was changed so that he could marry her. When he became emperor in 527 she was crowned empress and took an active part in government, receiving ambassadors, supporting laws to protect women, and protecting Monophysite clergy.

In 532, during the Nika riots, when the city was in flames and Justinian's advisers urged him to flee, Procopius says Theodora rose and declared that she would rather die an empress than live as a fugitive, saying that royalty makes a fine burial shroud. The emperor stayed, and the rising was crushed. She died in 548, probably of cancer. The mosaics of San Vitale in Ravenna show her with her court, robed in imperial purple.
