Greek archaeologist who proposed that the Theran eruption ended Minoan civilisation, then excavated the buried town of Akrotiri.

Uncovered the best-preserved Bronze Age settlement in the Aegean — the Aegean's Pompeii, with frescoes intact to ceiling height.

<!-- field: body -->
Spyridon Marinatos (1901–1974) was a Greek archaeologist, born on Kefalonia, who became one of the leading figures of Greek archaeology. He served as Inspector General of Antiquities and excavated at many Bronze Age sites, including early work in Messenia near Pylos, and at Thermopylae, where in 1939 he found the Persian arrowheads on the hill of the Spartans' last stand.

In 1939 he also proposed that the eruption of the volcano of Thera (Santorini) had destroyed Minoan civilisation on Crete, after noticing volcanic pumice in Cretan excavations. In 1967 he began excavating at Akrotiri on Thera itself, and found a whole Bronze Age town buried under metres of ash: houses of two and three storeys, drains, furniture, storage jars and wall paintings still in place, including the Flotilla fresco, the Boxing Boys and the Saffron Gatherers. No bodies and few valuables were found, suggesting the people left before the final eruption.

His theory in its strongest form has not survived. Radiocarbon dating and other evidence now put the eruption around 1600 BC, more than a century before the destructions across Crete around 1450 BC that ended the Minoan palaces, so it cannot have caused them directly, though it may have done longer-term damage.

Marinatos died in 1974 after a fall at Akrotiri, and is buried at the site. Christos Doumas continued the excavation.
