<!-- field: summary -->
Greek archaeologist who proposed that the Theran eruption ended Minoan civilisation, then excavated the buried town of Akrotiri.

<!-- field: significance -->
Uncovered the best-preserved Bronze Age settlement in the Aegean — the Aegean's Pompeii, with frescoes intact to ceiling height.

<!-- field: body -->
Marinatos built a long and prominent career in Greek archaeology well before Akrotiri made his name internationally, serving at various points as Greece's Inspector General of Antiquities and directing excavations at several major Bronze Age sites, including early work at Pylos that helped lay groundwork for Carl Blegen's later, more famous excavation of the Palace of Nestor there.

In 1939 Marinatos published the hypothesis that the eruption of Thera had destroyed Minoan civilisation on Crete, an idea prompted by volcanic pumice he had noticed turning up in Cretan archaeological contexts. He began digging at Akrotiri on Thera itself in 1967 and found an entire Bronze Age town preserved under metres of volcanic ash: multi-storey houses, a functioning drainage system, and wall paintings depicting ships in procession, boxing boys, wild antelopes and a ceremonial saffron harvest.

Crucially, no unburied human bodies and almost no valuable portable goods were found among the ruins, strongly suggesting the inhabitants had evacuated the settlement in good order after a sequence of warning earthquakes gave them time to leave.

His original hypothesis, in its strongest form, has not held up under later scientific scrutiny. Radiocarbon dating and corroborating evidence from ice cores now place the Theran eruption around 1600 BC, more than a century before the widespread destructions across Crete itself around 1450 BC that mark the effective end of Minoan palatial civilisation, meaning the eruption cannot have caused those particular destructions directly, whatever longer-term economic or social damage it may still have done. Marinatos died at Akrotiri itself in 1974 after a fall on the excavation site he had devoted his final years to, and he is buried there, close to the town he spent so long uncovering.
