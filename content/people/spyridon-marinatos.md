<!-- field: summary -->
Greek archaeologist who proposed that the Theran eruption ended Minoan civilisation, then excavated the buried town of Akrotiri.

<!-- field: significance -->
Uncovered the best-preserved Bronze Age settlement in the Aegean — the Aegean's Pompeii, with frescoes intact to ceiling height.

<!-- field: body -->
In 1939 Marinatos published the hypothesis that the eruption of Thera destroyed Minoan Crete, prompted by pumice found in Cretan contexts. He began digging at Akrotiri on Thera in 1967 and found a town preserved under metres of volcanic ash: multi-storey houses, drainage, wall paintings of ships, boxers, antelopes and a saffron harvest.

Crucially, no unburied bodies and almost no valuables were found — the inhabitants appear to have evacuated after warning earthquakes.

His original hypothesis has not held up in its strong form. Radiocarbon and ice-core dating place the eruption around 1600 BC, more than a century before the widespread Cretan destructions of 1450 BC, so the eruption cannot have caused them directly. He died at Akrotiri in 1974 after a fall on the site, and is buried there.
