<!-- field: summary -->
The great preacher of Antioch who became archbishop of Constantinople in 398 and died in exile in 407.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most widely read of the Greek Church Fathers, whose sermons and liturgy remain at the heart of Orthodox Christianity.

<!-- field: body -->
John (c. 347–407) was born in Antioch, the great Greek city of Syria, and trained in rhetoric under the famous pagan teacher Libanius. Turning to an ascetic life, he lived for some years as a hermit in the hills before returning to Antioch, where he was ordained priest in 386. Over the next twelve years his sermons, mostly on books of the Bible, made him the most celebrated preacher in the east. Over 600 survive. A century after his death he was called *Chrysostomos*, "golden-mouthed".

In 398 he was made archbishop of Constantinople. He attacked the luxury of the rich and the court, reformed the clergy, and spent church money on hospitals. He made powerful enemies, including the empress Eudoxia, who took his sermons against vanity personally, and Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria. In 403 a synod packed with his opponents deposed him; he was briefly restored after riots, but in 404 he was exiled to Armenia. In 407 he was ordered to a more remote place and died on the forced march at Comana in Pontus.

He is honoured as one of the Three Holy Hierarchs of the Orthodox Church. His sermons *Against the Jews*, violent even by the standards of the time, have damaged his reputation in modern times.
