<!-- field: summary -->
An austere Athenian general and statesman of the fourth century BC who urged peace with Macedon and was executed in 318 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The leading Athenian voice of accommodation with Macedon, set against Demosthenes' resistance.

<!-- field: body -->
Phocion was born about 402 BC and studied with Plato and Xenocrates. He first made his name as a soldier under Chabrias and served as strategos repeatedly, despite never seeking office. He campaigned in Euboea in 348 and 341, and in the Hellespont against Philip II.

Plutarch gives him as an example of honesty. He lived simply, turned down gifts from Alexander, and wore the same plain cloak. He opposed Demosthenes, arguing that Athens could not beat Macedon and should avoid pointless confrontation. After Chaeronea in 338 BC he accepted terms from Philip, and later again from Antipater after the Lamian War.

In 318 BC Athens' democracy was restored and, with the Macedonian regent Polyperchon, he was put on trial for treason. He was condemned and drank hemlock, reportedly asking his son to forgive the Athenians. A few years later the Athenians regretted the sentence and put up a bronze statue in his honour.
