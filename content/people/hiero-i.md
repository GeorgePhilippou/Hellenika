<!-- field: summary -->
The tyrant of Syracuse from 478 to 467 BC, victor over the Etruscans at Cumae and the most celebrated patron of poets of his age.

<!-- field: significance -->
His court made Syracuse a centre of Greek poetry; Pindar's grandest odes were written for his victories.

<!-- field: body -->
Hiero I succeeded his brother Gelon as tyrant of Syracuse in 478 BC. In 474 the Greek city of Cumae near Naples asked for help against the Etruscans, and Hiero's fleet won a decisive victory off its coast, ending Etruscan sea power in southern Italy. He dedicated captured Etruscan helmets at Olympia; one, now in the British Museum, is inscribed "Hiero son of Deinomenes and the Syracusans, to Zeus: Etruscan spoils from Cumae".

Like Gelon, he moved populations by force. In 476 he expelled the people of Catana and refounded it as Aetna, with new settlers; Aeschylus wrote a play, *The Women of Aetna*, for the foundation, and later staged the *Persians* at Syracuse.

Hiero spent lavishly on the panhellenic games. His horse won the single-horse race at Olympia in 476, celebrated in Pindar's First Olympian Ode, and his chariot won at Delphi in 470 and at Olympia in 468; Pindar and his rival Bacchylides both wrote victory odes for him. The poet Simonides also lived at his court. Ancient tradition, however, remembered him as a harsher ruler than Gelon, with a network of informers. He died at Aetna around 467 BC, and the tyranny fell soon after.
