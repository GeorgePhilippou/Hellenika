<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian admiral who commanded a Persian fleet to destroy Spartan sea power at Cnidus in 394 BC and so helped Athens rebuild its walls.

<!-- field: significance -->
The man who brought about the first partial recovery of Athenian power after the Peloponnesian War.

<!-- field: body -->
Conon was one of the Athenian generals at Aegospotami in 405 BC. When Lysander's attack destroyed the fleet, he escaped with eight ships and took refuge with Evagoras of Salamis in Cyprus, rather than return to Athens in disgrace. For some years he lived there, and in 398 BC Evagoras helped him to obtain the support of the Persian king.

Artaxerxes II appointed him admiral together with the satrap Pharnabazus, and in 394 BC their fleet of triremes defeated the Spartans under Peisander off Cnidus. The Greek cities of Asia expelled their Spartan garrisons, and Conon sailed to the Greek mainland, raiding the Peloponnese. In 393 BC he reached Athens and paid for the rebuilding of the Long Walls, which Sparta had demolished in 404 BC.

Sparta, anxious about his influence, persuaded the Persian governor Tiribazus to arrest him in 392 BC. He died in Cyprus not long afterwards, though the circumstances are uncertain. The Athenians set up a statue of Conon in the Agora.
