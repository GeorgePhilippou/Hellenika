<!-- field: summary -->
Athenian philosopher who wrote nothing, questioned everyone, and was executed by the democracy in 399 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
Turned philosophy from cosmology to ethics — how one ought to live — and made cross-examination the method of philosophical enquiry.

<!-- field: body -->
Socrates spent his life in the Athenian agora asking people to define what they claimed to know: courage, piety, justice. The *elenchus* — a sequence of questions exposing contradiction in the respondent's own beliefs — is his signature method, and it made him enemies steadily.

He was tried in 399 BC for impiety and corrupting the young. The charge is inseparable from politics: Athens had just lost the war and endured the murderous rule of the Thirty Tyrants, several of whom had been in his circle. Under an amnesty they could not be prosecuted directly. He was convicted by a narrow margin, proposed free meals for life as his counter-penalty, and drank hemlock.

Everything known about him comes through others — Plato, Xenophon, Aristophanes — who disagree substantially. This is the "Socratic problem".
