<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian general who overthrew the Thirty Tyrants in 403 BC and restored the democracy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The hero of the democratic restoration, whose amnesty of 403 BC became a model of reconciliation after civil war.

<!-- field: body -->
Thrasybulus (c. 440–388 BC) first appears in 411 BC, when an oligarchy seized power at Athens. With the fleet at Samos he led the sailors in remaining loyal to democracy, and persuaded them to recall the exiled Alcibiades. He then commanded in the naval victories of the last years of the Peloponnesian War, including Cyzicus in 410.

After Athens surrendered in 404, the Spartan-backed Thirty Tyrants under Critias took power and Thrasybulus fled to Thebes. That winter he crossed the border with about seventy exiles and seized the mountain fort of Phyle. His force grew, and in spring 403 it took the Piraeus and defeated the Thirty at Munychia, where Critias was killed. With the agreement of the Spartan king Pausanias the democracy was restored, and an amnesty forbade prosecution for most acts committed during the civil war.

In the 390s Thrasybulus led Athenian recovery in the Corinthian War, and in 389 sailed to the Aegean and Asia Minor to win back allies and revenue. In 388 his soldiers' plundering provoked the people of Aspendus in Pamphylia, who killed him in his tent.
