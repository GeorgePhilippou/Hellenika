<!-- field: summary -->
The Greek historian and Roman governor of the 2nd century AD whose Anabasis is the best surviving account of Alexander's campaigns.

<!-- field: significance -->
The historian through whom the eyewitness accounts of Alexander's officers reach us.

<!-- field: body -->
Arrian (c. AD 86–160) was born at Nicomedia in Bithynia, north-western Asia Minor. He studied with the Stoic philosopher Epictetus, whose teachings he wrote down in the *Discourses* and *Handbook*. He entered Roman service, became consul and, under the emperor Hadrian, governed the frontier province of Cappadocia, where he commanded legions against the Alans. Later he settled in Athens and held office there.

He saw himself as a new Xenophon, and named his history of Alexander the *Anabasis* after Xenophon's work. Writing more than four centuries after Alexander, he chose as his main sources the lost memoirs of Ptolemy, Alexander's general, and Aristobulus, an engineer on the campaign, explaining that both had been there. His knowledge of war makes his account of battles and sieges the most credible of all ancient Alexander histories, though he admired Alexander and treated his crimes as lapses.

He also wrote the *Indica*, on India and the voyage of Alexander's admiral Nearchus, and works on tactics, hunting and the Black Sea.
