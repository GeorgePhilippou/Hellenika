<!-- field: summary -->
The Byzantine scholar and envoy who taught Greek in Florence from 1397, launching the study of Greek in Renaissance Italy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The teacher who made Greek part of Italian humanism; after him Italian scholars could read Plato, Homer and Thucydides in the original.

<!-- field: body -->
Manuel Chrysoloras (c. 1350–1415) was a nobleman of Constantinople and a friend of the emperor Manuel II. In the 1390s he went to Venice as an ambassador seeking help against the Ottomans, and met Italian humanists eager to learn Greek, which almost no one in Italy could then read.

In 1397 Coluccio Salutati, chancellor of Florence, invited him to teach Greek in the city's university. In three years he trained a generation of humanists, including Leonardo Bruni, who later translated Plato and Aristotle into Latin, the patron Palla Strozzi, and the educator Guarino of Verona. He taught them to translate for meaning rather than word for word, and with Uberto Decembrio made the first Latin translation of Plato's *Republic*. He brought Greek manuscripts with him, and his pupils began to collect more.

His grammar, the *Erotemata* ("Questions"), written in question-and-answer form, was copied widely and was among the first Greek books printed in Italy. Chrysoloras later taught in Pavia and Milan, travelled as an envoy for the emperor and the pope to France, England and Spain, and died at the Council of Constance in 1415.
