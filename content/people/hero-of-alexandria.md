<!-- field: summary -->
A Greek engineer and mathematician of Roman Alexandria, who described steam-driven devices, automata and a formula for the area of a triangle.

<!-- field: significance -->
The best-known ancient writer on mechanical devices, whose works preserved Hellenistic engineering.

<!-- field: body -->
Hero of Alexandria worked in the first century AD, probably around AD 62, which is the date of a lunar eclipse he mentions. Little is known about his life. He wrote a series of practical treatises, among them *Pneumatica*, on devices powered by air, water and steam; *Automata*, on self-moving theatres; *Mechanica*; *Dioptra*, on surveying; and *Metrica*, on measurement.

His devices include the aeolipile, a hollow sphere that spun when steam escaped from bent tubes, a wind-powered organ, a fire engine and a temple door opened by a fire on an altar. He also described a dispenser that released a measure of holy water when a coin dropped on a tray. Most of these were demonstration pieces that exploited hydraulics and air pressure rather than useful machines. In geometry he is known for the formula for the area of a triangle from its three sides, which may have been earlier than him.

His works were copied in Byzantine and Arabic scholarship and, when translated in the sixteenth century, influenced Renaissance engineers and the design of automata.
