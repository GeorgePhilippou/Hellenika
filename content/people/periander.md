<!-- field: summary -->
The second tyrant of Corinth, who ruled from about 627 to 587 BC, expanded the city's colonies and became a figure of tyranny and wisdom in Greek tradition.

<!-- field: significance -->
Under him Corinth reached the peak of its power in the archaic period.

<!-- field: body -->
Periander took over from his father Cypselus, who had overthrown the Bacchiad oligarchs. His rule is described as harsh. Herodotus has him send a messenger to Thrasybulus of Miletus, who showed him the tyrant's secret of cutting down the tallest ears of corn, and Diogenes Laertius lists him among the Seven Sages. Later stories say he killed his wife Melissa in anger and was haunted by it.

Trade and colonial expansion were his main achievements. Corinth extended its trading network in the Adriatic and northern Aegean, founded Potidaea and Apollonia, and brought Corcyra under Corinthian control. The city's pottery and bronze reached markets across the Mediterranean. He gave his son Lycophron the rule of Corcyra, where the Corcyraeans killed him.

He was succeeded by a nephew, Psammetichus, who was soon overthrown, ending the Cypselid dynasty after about seventy years. The Diolkos, a paved track across the Isthmus, has been connected with him, but modern dating puts it a little later.
