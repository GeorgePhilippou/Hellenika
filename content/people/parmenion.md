<!-- field: summary -->
Philip II's most senior general, who served Alexander as second-in-command until Alexander had him killed.

<!-- field: significance -->
Represents the older Macedonian establishment whose loyalty Alexander eventually found incompatible with his own transformation.

<!-- field: body -->
Parmenion commanded the left wing at Granicus, Issus and Gaugamela — the defensive anchor while Alexander struck from the right. Arrian repeatedly has him counsel caution and Alexander overrule him, a pattern probably shaped by later sources wanting a foil for Alexander's boldness.

In 330 BC his son Philotas was accused of failing to report a plot, tortured, and executed. Alexander then sent riders ahead to Ecbatana to kill Parmenion before news could reach him. There was no evidence against him. It is the clearest case of Alexander eliminating a potential rival on grounds of position rather than proof.
