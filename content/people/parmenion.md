Parmenion (c. 400–330 BC) was a Macedonian general, the most senior commander of Philip II and second-in-command to Alexander the Great. He won major victories for Philip, including over the Illyrians in 356 BC, and in 336 BC led the advance force into Asia that prepared the way for Alexander's invasion.

In Alexander's great battles — the Granicus, Issus and Gaugamela — Parmenion commanded the left wing, holding the line while Alexander attacked from the right. Ancient writers such as Arrian repeatedly show him advising caution and Alexander overruling him; this pattern is probably exaggerated to make Alexander look bolder.

In 330 BC his son Philotas, commander of the Companion Cavalry, was accused of failing to report a plot against Alexander, and was tortured and executed. Alexander then sent agents to Ecbatana in Media, where Parmenion was stationed, to kill him before he heard the news. There was no evidence of his guilt. The killing removed the most powerful representative of Philip's old guard, men whose loyalty was to Macedonian tradition rather than to Alexander's new, partly Persian style of kingship, and it shocked the army.
