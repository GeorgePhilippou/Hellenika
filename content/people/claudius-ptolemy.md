<!-- field: summary -->
The Alexandrian astronomer and geographer of the 2nd century AD whose Almagest and Geography shaped science for 1,400 years.

<!-- field: significance -->
His model of an Earth-centred universe was the accepted picture of the cosmos until the 16th century.

<!-- field: body -->
Almost nothing is known of Ptolemy's life except that he worked at Alexandria in the 2nd century AD; the astronomical observations he reports date from AD 127 to 141. His name, Claudius Ptolemaeus, shows he was a Roman citizen of Greek culture in Egypt. He is not related to the Ptolemaic kings.

His greatest work, the *Mathematical Syntaxis*, known from its Arabic title as the *Almagest* ("the greatest"), is a complete treatise on astronomy in thirteen books. Building on Hipparchus, he explained the movements of the Sun, Moon and planets around a stationary Earth with combinations of circles — epicycles, eccentrics and a new device, the equant — that predicted their positions well. It includes a catalogue of 1,022 stars in 48 constellations, the basis of most constellations still recognised.

His *Geography* explained how to draw a map of the world using latitude and longitude, with coordinates for about 8,000 places from Ireland to China. He also wrote the *Tetrabiblos*, the most influential ancient book on astrology, and works on optics and musical harmony.

His system was taken up by Arab and then European astronomers, and was not overthrown until Copernicus published his Sun-centred model in 1543.
