<!-- field: summary -->
The last Byzantine emperor (1449–1453), who died defending Constantinople against Mehmed II.

<!-- field: significance -->
His death in the final assault made him a lasting symbol of Greek resistance and of the end of Byzantium.

<!-- field: body -->
Constantine Palaiologos (1404–1453) was a son of the emperor Manuel II and a Serbian princess, Helena Dragaš, from whom he took the name Dragases. He spent much of his life in the Peloponnese as Despot of the Morea, where he rebuilt the Hexamilion wall across the Isthmus of Corinth and won back territory from the Latins, until an Ottoman army broke through in 1446.

When his brother John VIII died in 1448, Constantine was proclaimed emperor at Mystras in January 1449 and sailed to Constantinople. He inherited little more than the city itself, impoverished and depopulated, and divided over the church union agreed at Florence, which he accepted in the hope of western help. Help came only in small numbers, notably 700 Genoese under Giovanni Giustiniani.

When Mehmed II besieged the city in April 1453, Constantine refused offers to surrender in return for his life. On 29 May, as the Ottomans broke through the land walls, he is said to have thrown off his imperial insignia and charged into the fighting; he was never seen again. Greek folklore told that an angel turned him to marble and hid him in a cave beneath the Golden Gate, from which he would one day return to reclaim the city.
