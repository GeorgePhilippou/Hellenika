<!-- field: summary -->
The last ruler of Ptolemaic Egypt, whose alliances with Caesar and Antony were a strategy for Egyptian independence — and who lost.

<!-- field: significance -->
Her death in 30 BC ends the Hellenistic age and the last Greek-ruled successor kingdom of Alexander.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleopatra VII (69–30 BC) was the last ruler of the Ptolemaic kingdom of Egypt, founded by Alexander's general Ptolemy three centuries earlier. She was Macedonian Greek by descent, but was reportedly the first of her dynasty to learn Egyptian, and Plutarch says she spoke many languages.

She came to the throne in 51 BC as joint ruler with her younger brother Ptolemy XIII, who drove her out. Julius Caesar, arriving in Egypt in 48 BC, restored her; Ptolemy XIII died in the war that followed, and she bore Caesar a son, Caesarion. After Caesar's murder in 44 BC she allied with Mark Antony, whom she met at Tarsus in 41 BC, arriving on a gilded barge dressed as Aphrodite. They had three children. In 34 BC, in the "Donations of Alexandria", Antony gave Roman eastern territories to Cleopatra and her children — a gift Octavian used in Rome to portray Antony as the tool of a foreign queen.

Her relationships were also policy: Egypt was rich but militarily weak, and her best hope of keeping it independent was to make herself indispensable to Rome's most powerful men. It worked for twenty years. In 31 BC Octavian defeated Antony and Cleopatra at Actium. When he took Alexandria in 30 BC, Antony killed himself, and Cleopatra died soon after — by tradition from the bite of an asp, though Plutarch admits no one knew how. Egypt became a Roman province, ending the last Hellenistic kingdom.
