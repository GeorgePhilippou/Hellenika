<!-- field: summary -->
Brilliant, treacherous Athenian aristocrat who commanded for Athens, defected to Sparta, advised Persia, and returned to Athens again.

<!-- field: significance -->
His career is the clearest single illustration of how personal ambition destabilised Athenian democracy during the Peloponnesian War.

<!-- field: body -->
Alcibiades pushed Athens into the Sicilian Expedition in 415 BC. On the eve of departure the city's herms were mutilated overnight; he was implicated, recalled for trial, and instead defected to Sparta — where he advised them to fortify Decelea in Attica permanently, which did Athens enormous harm.

He then fell out with the Spartans, moved to the Persian satrap Tissaphernes, and negotiated his own return to Athens, winning naval victories before a subordinate's defeat gave his enemies the pretext to remove him. He was assassinated in Phrygia in 404 BC.

He was also a close associate of Socrates, who saved his life at Potidaea. Plato's *Symposium* gives him a drunken speech of genuine love for Socrates, and his association with the philosopher counted against Socrates at trial.
