<!-- field: body -->
Ptolemy I Soter (c. 367–282 BC) was a Macedonian general and boyhood friend of Alexander the Great, one of his seven personal bodyguards, who founded the Ptolemaic dynasty that ruled Egypt until Cleopatra VII.

After Alexander's death in 323 BC he took Egypt as his province. In 321 BC he seized Alexander's body on its way to Macedon and brought it to Egypt, a great source of prestige, and that year defeated the regent Perdiccas, whose invasion collapsed in a disastrous crossing of the Nile. Over the following decades he fought the other Successors for Cyprus, Palestine and the Aegean. He took the title of king in 305 BC; the name Soter, "Saviour", was given him by the Rhodians for his help during the siege of their city in 305–304 BC.

Ptolemy made Alexandria his capital and founded the Museum and Library, which made the city the centre of Greek learning. He promoted the new god Serapis, combining Greek and Egyptian features, as a cult for both his Greek and Egyptian subjects, while presenting himself to Egyptians as a traditional pharaoh.

He wrote a history of Alexander's campaigns, now lost but used as a main source by Arrian, so much of what is known about Alexander comes through his memory. Unusually among the Successors, he died peacefully, having handed power to his son Ptolemy II in 285 BC.
