<!-- field: summary -->
The Roman emperor (AD 117–138) who loved Greek culture, rebuilt Athens and tried to unite the Greek cities in the Panhellenion.

<!-- field: significance -->
The emperor who did most for Greece, whose buildings still shape the centre of Athens.

<!-- field: body -->
Hadrian (AD 76–138) came from a Roman family settled at Italica in Spain. As a young man he was so devoted to Greek learning that he was nicknamed *Graeculus*, "the little Greek". In AD 112, before he became emperor, the Athenians elected him their chief magistrate (archon). He succeeded his relative Trajan in 117 and spent much of his reign touring the provinces. He was the first emperor to wear a full beard, perhaps in the style of a Greek philosopher.

He visited Athens at least three times, was initiated into the Mysteries at Eleusis, and gave the city a new quarter. He finished the vast temple of Olympian Zeus, begun by the tyrant Peisistratus' sons around 520 BC and continued by Antiochus IV, and dedicated it in 131/2; the Athenians set up an arch marking the boundary between the old city and "the city of Hadrian". He also built a great library with a colonnaded court. In 131/2 he founded the Panhellenion, a council of Greek cities that met at Athens.

His favourite, the Bithynian youth Antinous, drowned in the Nile in 130; Hadrian founded a city, Antinoopolis, in his memory and promoted his worship as a god across the Greek east. He appointed the Greek historian Arrian governor of Cappadocia.
