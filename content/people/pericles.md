<!-- field: summary -->
The dominant Athenian politician for three decades, who rebuilt the Acropolis, extended democratic participation and led Athens into the Peloponnesian War.

<!-- field: significance -->
His name marks the height of Athenian power, and the buildings he commissioned remain the visual signature of Greek civilisation.

<!-- field: body -->
Pericles was elected *strategos* — general — repeatedly from 461 BC until shortly before his death. That office, unlike almost every other, was elective and re-electable, and it was the base of his authority; he held no permanent power and had to win the assembly's vote again and again.

He introduced pay for jury service, which made participation possible for poor citizens, and moved the Delian League treasury to Athens, spending allied tribute on the Parthenon, Propylaea and Odeon. Thucydides gives him the Funeral Oration, the classic statement of Athenian democratic self-understanding, and also judges his regime "in name a democracy, but in fact rule by the first man".

His strategy at the outbreak of war — abandon the countryside, rely on the fleet, wait Sparta out — was sound and was destroyed by an unforeseeable variable. Plague broke out in the crowded city in 430 BC and killed him in 429.
