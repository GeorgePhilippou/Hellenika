<!-- field: summary -->
Founder of the Seleucid Empire, the largest of Alexander's successor states, stretching from Anatolia to Central Asia.

<!-- field: significance -->
Ruled the greatest territorial share of Alexander's conquests and planted Greek cities across Mesopotamia, Iran and beyond.

<!-- field: body -->
Seleucus was made satrap of Babylonia in 321 BC, driven out, and returned in 312 — a date the Seleucid Era counts from, making it the first continuous numbered year-count in history and the ancestor of all such systems.

He pushed east to the Indus and met Chandragupta Maurya. Rather than fight, he ceded the easternmost provinces in exchange for 500 war elephants — which proved decisive at Ipsus in 301 BC. He founded Antioch and Seleucia-on-the-Tigris and married the Sogdian noblewoman Apama, whose son Antiochus I succeeded him. He was assassinated in 281 BC while attempting to take Macedon, the one prize he never got.
