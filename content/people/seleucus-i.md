<!-- field: body -->
Seleucus I Nicator (c. 358–281 BC), "the Victor", was a Macedonian officer of Alexander the Great who founded the largest of the kingdoms that succeeded him. At the mass weddings at Susa in 324 BC he married Apama, daughter of a Sogdian noble, and unlike most of Alexander's officers he kept his Iranian wife; their son Antiochus I succeeded him.

After Alexander's death he became governor of Babylonia in 321 BC, was driven out by Antigonus in 316 BC, and returned in 312 BC — the starting year of the Seleucid era, used for dating for centuries. He then took control of Iran and Central Asia. Reaching the Indus, he made peace with the Indian king Chandragupta Maurya, giving up his easternmost provinces in exchange for 500 war elephants. At Ipsus in 301 BC those elephants helped defeat Antigonus, and Seleucus won Syria. In 281 BC he defeated and killed Lysimachus at Corupedium, gaining Asia Minor.

He founded many new cities settled by Greeks and Macedonians, including Antioch in Syria, named after his father, and Seleucia on the Tigris, his eastern capital. These cities spread Greek culture across the Near East and Iran.

On the verge of taking Macedon itself, Seleucus was murdered in 281 BC by Ptolemy Keraunos, an exiled son of Ptolemy I whom he had sheltered. His dynasty ruled in Syria until Rome ended it in 63 BC.
