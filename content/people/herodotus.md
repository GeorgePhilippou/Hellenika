<!-- field: summary -->
Author of the Histories, the first sustained prose enquiry into the human past — an account of the Persian Wars that ranges across the known world.

<!-- field: significance -->
Invented history as a genre: a named investigator, weighing evidence and reporting conflicting versions rather than reciting tradition.

<!-- field: body -->
His opening sentence sets out the programme: this is the *historie* — enquiry — of Herodotus, so that human achievements are not forgotten and the reason Greeks and barbarians fought is preserved. The word *historia* means investigation; it acquires its modern sense from this book.

He travelled widely, describing Egypt, Scythia, Babylon and Libya with an ethnographic curiosity that has no precedent. His method is to report what he was told and mark his own doubt: he repeatedly says he must record what is said but is not obliged to believe it. Ancient critics called him a liar; modern archaeology has vindicated him on numerous specifics, including details of Egyptian practice and Scythian burial that once seemed fanciful.
