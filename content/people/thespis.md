<!-- field: summary -->
The semi-legendary Athenian credited with inventing tragic acting in the sixth century BC, from whom actors take the name 'thespians'.

<!-- field: significance -->
The traditional founder of European drama.

<!-- field: body -->
Thespis is the earliest name connected with Athenian tragedy. He came from Icaria, a deme in Attica associated with Dionysus, and is said to have been the first to step out of the chorus and speak as an individual character, answering the chorus. With this innovation tragedy became a performance between actor and chorus rather than a purely choral song.

A tradition recorded on the Parian Marble puts an early Athenian dramatic victory in the 530s BC, and later sources give 534 BC as the date of the first tragedy at the City Dionysia, in the time of Peisistratus. Both the date and the story have been questioned, and Aristotle himself says only that tragedy grew from the leaders of the dithyramb.

No play of Thespis survives, and in antiquity some works circulated under his name were considered forgeries. Aeschylus is said to have added a second actor, giving the form its familiar shape. The word 'thespian' still means an actor.
