<!-- field: summary -->
The king of Pergamon from 197 to 159 BC who, as Rome's ally, made Pergamon a great power and a rival of Alexandria in culture.

<!-- field: significance -->
The king under whom Pergamon reached its height, with the Great Altar and a library second only to Alexandria's.

<!-- field: body -->
Eumenes II succeeded his father Attalus I in 197 BC and continued his alliance with Rome. When the Seleucid king Antiochus III invaded Greece, Eumenes fought with the Romans, and his cavalry played a key part in their victory at Magnesia in 190 BC. At the Peace of Apamea in 188, Rome rewarded him with most of Seleucid Asia Minor, making Pergamon one of the largest kingdoms of the Greek world.

He spent his wealth on his capital. The upper city was rebuilt with terraces, a theatre built into the steep hillside, and a library said to have held 200,000 scrolls; Pliny, citing Varro, tells a doubtful story that Egypt banned the export of papyrus to stop it rivalling Alexandria, and that Pergamon then developed parchment (Latin *pergamena*). The Great Altar, with its frieze of gods fighting giants, is usually dated to his reign, perhaps in celebration of victories over the Galatians.

In 172 BC he travelled to Rome to denounce Perseus of Macedon, leading to the Third Macedonian War; on his way back he was injured at Delphi by rocks rolled down on him, an attack blamed on Perseus. Rome later grew suspicious of him and he lost favour. He died in 159 or 158 BC.
