<!-- field: summary -->
The Roman emperor who toured Greece in AD 66–67 as a competitor in its great festivals and proclaimed the freedom of the province.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first emperor to treat Greece as a stage rather than a subject territory, and a precedent for Hadrian's philhellenism.

<!-- field: body -->
Nero was passionately fond of Greek music, theatre and athletics, and thought of himself as a performer. In autumn AD 66 he sailed to Greece with a large retinue and ended up spending about a year there. The festival calendar was rearranged so that Olympic, Pythian, Isthmian and Nemean games all fell within his visit, and Nero entered the contests for singing, lyre-playing, tragic acting and chariot racing, and was awarded more than a thousand crowns.

At Olympia he drove a ten-horse chariot, was thrown, and did not finish; the judges nonetheless proclaimed him the victor. At Delphi he is said to have taken hundreds of statues and quarrelled with the oracle. In November AD 67, at the Isthmian Games, he announced that Greece was to be free and exempt from taxes, a status that Vespasian soon reversed. A ceremonial first dig on a canal across the Isthmus followed.

A revolt in the west forced him home in AD 68, and he committed suicide that year. Greek attitudes to him were mixed: many cities set up honours, but the tax exemption did not last.
