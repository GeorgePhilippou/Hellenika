<!-- field: summary -->
The Spartan princess who became the first woman to win at the Olympic Games, as owner of the victorious chariot team in 396 and 392 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first recorded woman Olympic champion, whose victory monument at Olympia declared her the only woman in Greece to have won the crown.

<!-- field: body -->
Cynisca (born around 440 BC) was a Spartan princess, the daughter of King Archidamus II and sister of King Agesilaus II. Women could not compete at the Olympic Games, and married women were not even allowed to watch. But in the equestrian events the victor was the owner of the horses, not the driver, so a wealthy woman could win.

Cynisca entered a four-horse chariot team and won the race at Olympia in 396 BC and again in 392 BC. She set up a bronze monument of her chariot and horses in the sanctuary, and a statue of herself, made by the sculptor Apelleas. Its inscribed base was found at Olympia. The inscription reads in part: "Kings of Sparta were my fathers and brothers. Cynisca, victorious with her chariot of swift-footed horses, erected this statue. I declare that I am the only woman in all Greece to have won this crown."

Xenophon and Plutarch claimed that her brother Agesilaus encouraged her to compete to show that chariot victories depended only on wealth, not on manly virtue. Modern historians suspect this underplays Cynisca's own ambition, and the wealth and independence of Spartan women.

After her, other women, mostly from Sparta and Macedon, won equestrian victories at Olympia. Cynisca was honoured in Sparta with a hero shrine.
