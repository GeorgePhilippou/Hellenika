<!-- field: summary -->
The tyrant of Samos in the sixth century BC, who built a great fleet and monuments and whose proverbial luck ended in his death at Persian hands.

<!-- field: significance -->
The model of an archaic tyrant who ruled the sea, and a patron whose court shaped Greek poetry and engineering.

<!-- field: body -->
Polycrates seized control of Samos, with his brothers, about 538 BC, and soon took sole power. He built up a fleet of one hundred penteconters, with which he plundered neighbours and made Samos the leading naval power of the Aegean. For a time he was allied with Amasis of Egypt and then with Persia.

He spent heavily on public building. Under him the Samians built the tunnel of Eupalinos, a water conduit more than a kilometre long through a hill, a breakwater and the vast temple of Hera. At court he gathered poets, among them Anacreon, and doctors. Herodotus regards him as exceptionally fortunate and tells how he threw a prized ring into the sea, only to have it returned inside a fish; the story marks the point at which his luck was due to turn.

Around 522 BC the Persian governor Oroetes tempted Polycrates to the mainland with an offer of money and had him killed and his body hung up. Samos then passed to a puppet ruler.
