<!-- field: summary -->
Theban general who broke Spartan military supremacy at Leuctra in 371 BC and freed the Messenian helots.

<!-- field: significance -->
Ended Sparta as a great power permanently, and pioneered the tactical asymmetry that Philip II — a hostage in Thebes as a youth — would later develop.

<!-- field: body -->
At Leuctra, Epaminondas massed his left wing fifty ranks deep against the Spartan right, held back the rest of his line, and shattered the elite before the weaker Theban centre and right were engaged. It was the first clear use of a deliberately unequal battle line, and roughly 400 of the 700 Spartiates present were killed — a demographic wound Sparta could not absorb.

He then invaded the Peloponnese and did the decisive thing: he liberated Messenia, whose helots had farmed the land that fed the Spartan military system for three centuries. He refounded Messene as a fortified city. Sparta never recovered.

He was killed at Mantinea in 362 BC, and Theban hegemony died with him.
