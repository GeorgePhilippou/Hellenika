<!-- field: summary -->
The former slave who became the most influential Stoic teacher of the Roman empire, teaching at Nicopolis in Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
His teaching on what is and is not in our power has influenced thinkers from Marcus Aurelius to modern cognitive therapy.

<!-- field: body -->
Epictetus (c. 50–135) was born a slave at Hierapolis in Phrygia, in Asia Minor; his name simply means "acquired". In Rome he belonged to Epaphroditus, a powerful freedman and secretary of Nero, who allowed him to study with the Stoic philosopher Musonius Rufus. He was lame, perhaps from mistreatment. After he was freed he taught philosophy in Rome, until around AD 93 the emperor Domitian expelled the philosophers from Italy.

Epictetus moved to Nicopolis in north-western Greece, the city Augustus had founded after Actium, and opened a school that drew students from across the empire, including the young Arrian. He wrote nothing himself; Arrian took down his lectures as the *Discourses*, four of whose eight books survive, and made a short summary, the *Handbook* (*Enchiridion*).

His teaching begins with a distinction: some things are up to us — our judgements, desires and choices — and others are not, such as our body, property and reputation. Unhappiness comes from wanting what is not in our power. Freedom, even for a slave, lies in governing one's own mind. The emperor Marcus Aurelius quotes him in the *Meditations*, and his *Handbook* has been read ever since.
