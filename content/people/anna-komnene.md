<!-- field: summary -->
The Byzantine princess (1083–c. 1153) who wrote the Alexiad, the history of her father Alexios I and the First Crusade.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first woman historian known in Europe, and our chief Byzantine witness to the Crusades.

<!-- field: body -->
Anna Komnene was born in 1083 in the Porphyra, the purple chamber of the imperial palace, the eldest child of the emperor Alexios I Komnenos. She received an outstanding education in Greek literature, philosophy, medicine and astronomy. She was betrothed as a child to the heir of the previous dynasty and expected to become empress, but the birth of her brother John ended that hope. She married the general and historian Nikephoros Bryennios.

When Alexios died in 1118, Anna is said to have plotted with her mother to put her husband on the throne instead of John. The plot failed, and she spent the rest of her life in or near a convent in Constantinople, where she wrote.

Her *Alexiad*, in fifteen books, written in a learned Attic Greek modelled on Thucydides and Polybius, tells how her father saved the empire after the defeat at Manzikert, fighting the Normans, the Pechenegs and the Turks. It includes a vivid portrait of the knights of the First Crusade who passed through Constantinople in 1096–1097, especially the Norman Bohemond, whom she describes with fascination and distrust. It is partisan in her father's favour but full of detail. She died around 1153.
