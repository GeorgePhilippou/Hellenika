<!-- field: summary -->
Founder of the Achaemenid Empire, who conquered Media, Lydia and Babylon and established a template of tolerant imperial rule.

<!-- field: significance -->
Created the empire that would dominate Greek foreign policy for two centuries and that Alexander eventually destroyed.

<!-- field: body -->
Cyrus built the largest empire the world had yet seen in about twenty years. His conquest of Lydia in 546 BC brought Persian rule to the Ionian Greek cities, making conflict with the Greek mainland a structural certainty.

He governed by conciliating rather than erasing. The Cyrus Cylinder, a Babylonian foundation text, presents him as restoring temples and returning displaced peoples — including, in the biblical account, permitting Jewish exiles to return and rebuild the Temple. It is a royal inscription and therefore propaganda, but it matches an evident practical policy of local accommodation.

Greeks regarded him with respect: Xenophon wrote an idealised education of Cyrus as a manual of ideal kingship, and Alexander made a point of restoring his tomb at Pasargadae after finding it plundered.
