Founder of the Achaemenid Empire, who conquered Media, Lydia and Babylon and established a template of tolerant imperial rule.

<!-- field: significance -->
Created the empire that would dominate Greek foreign policy for two centuries and that Alexander eventually destroyed.

<!-- field: body -->
Cyrus II, the Great (c. 600–530 BC), was a Persian king from Anshan in south-western Iran who founded the Achaemenid Empire. In about twenty years he built the largest empire the world had yet seen. He overthrew the Median king Astyages around 550 BC, conquered Lydia and its king Croesus in 546 BC, and took Babylon in 539 BC.

The conquest of Lydia brought the Greek cities of Ionia under Persian rule, beginning the long relationship between Persia and the Greeks that led to the Persian Wars.

Cyrus generally ruled through existing local customs and elites rather than destroying them. The Cyrus Cylinder, a clay inscription from Babylon, presents him as restoring temples and returning displaced peoples and their gods to their homes; the Hebrew Bible credits him with allowing the Jews exiled in Babylon to return to Jerusalem and rebuild their Temple. The Cylinder is royal propaganda, but it matches a real policy of tolerance.

He was killed in 530 BC fighting nomads on the north-eastern frontier, probably the Massagetae, though ancient accounts differ. His tomb survives at Pasargadae, the capital he built. The Greeks admired him even as the founder of an enemy empire: Xenophon's *Cyropaedia* made him a model of ideal kingship, and Alexander had his tomb restored after finding it plundered.
