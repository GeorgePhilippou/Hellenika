<!-- field: summary -->
Persian general and son-in-law of Darius I who led the first invasion of Greece in 492 BC and died commanding the second at Plataea in 479 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
He was the architect of Persia's direct military involvement in Greece on both attempts, and his death at Plataea, with the destruction of the army he commanded, ended the Persian invasion of the Greek mainland for good.

<!-- field: body -->
Mardonius' first command, in 492 BC, marched an army through Thrace and forced Macedon into vassalage, extending Persian authority to the edge of Greece proper — but the accompanying fleet was wrecked rounding the promontory of Mount Athos, and the campaign was called off before reaching Athens or Eretria.

He was among the advisors who pushed Xerxes toward the much larger invasion of 480 BC, reportedly out of personal ambition to govern a conquered Greece, and served through the campaign that took Athens and won at Thermopylae before being checked at Salamis. When Xerxes withdrew the bulk of the army to Asia after the naval defeat, Mardonius stayed behind with a large force — Persia's best infantry — to finish the conquest the following year.

At Plataea in 479 BC his army faced a large combined Greek force under the Spartan regent Pausanias. Mardonius was killed in the fighting and his army broke, ending the invasion outright; the same summer's Greek naval victory at Mycale finished off Persian power in the Aegean.
