<!-- field: summary -->
The emperor (527–565) who reconquered much of the west, codified Roman law and built Hagia Sophia.

<!-- field: significance -->
The greatest of the early Byzantine emperors; his law code underlies European law and his church still dominates Istanbul.

<!-- field: body -->
Justinian (482–565) was born to a peasant family in the Balkans, a Latin speaker. His uncle Justin rose from the ranks to become emperor in 518, and Justinian, who had been educated in Constantinople, was his chief adviser and succeeded him in 527. He married Theodora, a former actress, who became his closest partner in government.

He dreamed of restoring the Roman empire. His general Belisarius destroyed the Vandal kingdom in North Africa in 533–534 and began the reconquest of Italy from the Ostrogoths, completed only after twenty years of war in 554; part of southern Spain was also taken. These wars, and long struggles with Persia, exhausted the empire, and from 541 the first great outbreak of bubonic plague killed perhaps a quarter of its people.

At home he had Roman law collected and simplified in the *Code*, *Digest* and *Institutes* (529–534), later called the *Corpus Juris Civilis*. In 532 the Nika riots, a rising of the circus factions, nearly overthrew him and burned much of the city; he crushed them and rebuilt on a grand scale, above all Hagia Sophia. He persecuted pagans and heretics, and in 529, according to the chronicler Malalas, forbade the teaching of philosophy at Athens.
