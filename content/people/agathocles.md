<!-- field: summary -->
The ruthless tyrant and then king of Syracuse (317–289 BC) who carried the war against Carthage to Africa itself.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first Greek to invade Carthaginian Africa, and the model of the self-made Hellenistic strongman.

<!-- field: body -->
Agathocles (361–289 BC) was said to have been the son of a potter. He rose as a soldier, and in 317 BC seized power in Syracuse in a bloody coup against the oligarchs, in which thousands were killed or exiled, overturning the settlement of Timoleon.

He extended his power over much of Greek Sicily, provoking war with Carthage. In 311 he was defeated and besieged in Syracuse. In 310 he made a bold decision: slipping out with about 14,000 men, he sailed to Africa, burned his ships, and ravaged Carthaginian territory for three years, coming close to Carthage itself. The campaign failed — he abandoned his army and fled back to Sicily in 307 — but he made peace with Carthage on equal terms in 306.

Around 304, following the example of Alexander's successors, he took the title of king. He later fought in southern Italy and Corcyra, and married his daughter Lanassa to Pyrrhus of Epirus.

He died in 289 BC, possibly poisoned. Diodorus says that on his deathbed he restored freedom to the Syracusans. Ancient writers, especially the hostile historian Timaeus, whom he had exiled, portrayed him as a monster of cruelty.
