<!-- field: summary -->
German businessman who excavated Troy and Mycenae, proved the Bronze Age Aegean was real, and destroyed a great deal of it doing so.

<!-- field: significance -->
Founded Aegean prehistory as a field, and stands as its permanent cautionary tale about method and self-promotion.

<!-- field: body -->
Schliemann made a fortune in indigo and Californian gold, then went looking for Homer's cities. At Hisarlik from 1871 he dug a vast trench straight down through the mound, destroying much of the layer that was actually Homeric-era Troy in order to reach a much older one he called "Priam's Treasure".

At Mycenae in 1876 he found the shaft graves with their gold masks, and telegraphed that he had gazed on the face of Agamemnon. He had not — the burials are roughly three centuries too early — but the discovery was genuinely epochal: it proved a rich Bronze Age civilisation had existed on the Greek mainland.

His published accounts are unreliable. He embellished, backdated discoveries, and by his own account had his Greek wife Sophia present when she was elsewhere. The dig, the find and the story have to be assessed separately.
