<!-- field: summary -->
The emperor (379–395) who made Christianity the state religion and banned pagan sacrifice, ending the public worship of the Greek gods.

<!-- field: significance -->
His laws marked the official end of ancient Greek religion, and he was the last emperor to rule both halves of the Roman empire.

<!-- field: body -->
Theodosius (347–395) was a Spanish general appointed emperor of the east in 379, after the Goths had destroyed a Roman army at Adrianople. He made peace with the Goths by settling them within the empire as allies.

A committed Nicene Christian, he issued the Edict of Thessalonica in 380, declaring the faith of the bishops of Rome and Alexandria the only legitimate form of Christianity, and in 381 summoned the Council of Constantinople, which completed the Nicene Creed. In 390, after a riot at Thessalonica, his troops massacred thousands in the hippodrome; Bishop Ambrose of Milan made him do public penance.

In 391–392 he issued laws banning all pagan sacrifice, public and private, and closing the temples. In Alexandria, Christians destroyed the Serapeum in 391. A late Byzantine source says the Olympic Games ended in 393, and the date is widely quoted, but there is evidence of games at Olympia into the 5th century; they probably faded rather than ending by decree.

In 394 Theodosius defeated a western usurper and briefly ruled the whole empire. When he died in 395 it was divided between his sons, Arcadius in the east and Honorius in the west, and was never united again.
