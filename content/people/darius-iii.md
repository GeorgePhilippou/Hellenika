<!-- field: summary -->
The last Achaemenid king, defeated by Alexander at Issus and Gaugamela and murdered by his own satrap.

<!-- field: significance -->
His defeat ended the two-century Achaemenid Empire and transferred the Near East to Macedonian rule.

<!-- field: body -->
Darius III (c. 380–330 BC), originally named Artashata, was the last king of the Achaemenid Persian Empire. He came to the throne in 336 BC, the same year as Alexander, after the powerful court official Bagoas had poisoned two previous kings; when Bagoas turned on him too, Darius forced him to drink his own poison.

Two years later Alexander invaded. At Issus in 333 BC Darius' larger army was trapped on a narrow coastal plain, and he fled the field, leaving his mother, wife and children to be captured; Alexander treated them with great respect. Darius offered Alexander large territories and money for peace and was refused. At Gaugamela in 331 BC he chose a wide plain, levelled it for his scythed chariots, and still lost when Alexander charged through a gap in the Persian line. He fled again.

Darius retreated into eastern Iran, where his own kinsman Bessus, governor of Bactria, arrested him. As Alexander's forces closed in during 330 BC, Bessus and his allies stabbed Darius and left him dying in a wagon. Alexander gave him a royal burial and later had Bessus executed, presenting himself as the avenger of a murdered king and the rightful heir to the Persian throne.

Greek writers portrayed Darius as a coward; modern historians see a capable ruler facing an exceptional opponent. With his death the Achaemenid Empire, two centuries old, came to an end.
