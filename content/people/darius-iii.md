<!-- field: summary -->
The last Achaemenid king, defeated by Alexander at Issus and Gaugamela and murdered by his own satrap.

<!-- field: significance -->
His defeat ended the two-century Achaemenid Empire and transferred the Near East to Macedonian rule.

<!-- field: body -->
Darius III came to the throne in 336 BC, the same year as Alexander, after a court poisoning cleared the way. He faced an invasion two years later.

At Issus in 333 BC his larger army was caught in terrain too narrow to deploy in; he fled the field, leaving his mother, wife and children to be captured — Alexander treated them with conspicuous respect. He offered enormous territorial concessions twice and was refused both times. At Gaugamela in 331 he chose open ground and levelled it for his scythed chariots, and still lost when Alexander drove a wedge into a gap opened in the Persian line.

He fled east and was arrested and stabbed by his satrap Bessus. Alexander found him dying or dead, and had him buried at Persepolis with royal honours, then hunted Bessus down and executed him — establishing himself as the legitimate avenger rather than merely the conqueror.
