<!-- field: summary -->
Son of Antipater who seized Macedon against his father's wishes, executed Alexander's mother, and secretly killed Alexander's son and heir.

<!-- field: significance -->
His murder of Alexander IV removed the last direct heir to Alexander's throne, formally ending any prospect the empire could be reunited under Alexander's own bloodline rather than divided among the generals who had served him.

<!-- field: body -->
Passed over by his own father, who named the general Polyperchon regent instead of him in 319 BC, Cassander refused to accept the arrangement and fought his way to control of Macedon over the following years, allying at different points with Antigonus, Ptolemy and Lysimachus against whichever successor threatened him most.

His hold on power required removing Alexander's surviving family. Olympias, Alexander's formidable mother, had massacred Cassander's own relatives during the fighting; he besieged her at Pydna, had her condemned by an assembly of Macedonians too frightened to acquit her, and allowed her to be killed by the relatives of her victims rather than execute her himself. Around 309 BC he moved against the last real threat to his rule: he had Alexander's posthumous son, Alexander IV, and the boy's mother Roxana quietly murdered, ending the Argead royal line that had ruled Macedon for centuries.

He founded two cities that outlasted him by a very wide margin — Cassandreia and Thessalonica, the latter named for his wife and surviving today as Greece's second city. He took the royal title in 305/4 BC along with the other leading successors, and died of illness in 297 BC; his own sons did not hold Macedon for long after him.
