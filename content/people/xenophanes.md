<!-- field: summary -->
Ionian poet-philosopher who ridiculed the human-shaped gods of Homer and argued for a single, unlike-mortal divinity.

<!-- field: significance -->
Xenophanes is the earliest surviving critic of Greek religion from inside the tradition, and a pioneer of the argument that human beliefs about the gods reflect the people who hold them.

<!-- field: body -->
Xenophanes came from Colophon, in Ionia, and by his own account spent sixty-seven years wandering the Greek world after leaving his home. Tradition connects the departure with the Persian conquest of Ionia in the 540s BC. He is later found in Sicily and southern Italy, and one ancient story places him at Elea, where he is said to have taught.

He wrote in verse, including elegies for symposia and satirical poems. The surviving lines are among the earliest sustained criticism of religion in the Greek world. He objected that Homer and Hesiod had ascribed to the gods everything that is shameful among men, and observed that people picture gods like themselves: Ethiopians with snub noses, Thracians with red hair, and, if horses or oxen could draw, gods in the shape of horses and oxen. He proposed instead a single greatest god, unlike mortals in body and in thought, who shakes all things without moving by the thinking of his mind.

The fragments also touch on knowledge. He wrote that no one has seen or will know the truth about the gods and about all he speaks of, and that even one who spoke the truth by chance would not know it. Scholars debate whether this is a sceptical claim or an admission of the limits of human inquiry into what cannot be observed.

Later writers say he noted fossils of shells and fish in quarries, and reasoned that the land had once been under water. The evidence is a report in a Christian author, so it should be treated with care. Aristotle already ranked him with the Eleatics, but modern scholars are divided on how far he influenced Parmenides. He is best understood as an independent critic whose questions later thinkers were able to use.
