<!-- field: summary -->
Alexander's Cretan-born admiral, who led the fleet from the Indus to the Persian Gulf in 325–324 BC and wrote an account of the voyage.

<!-- field: significance -->
His voyage opened the sea route between India and Mesopotamia to Greek knowledge.

<!-- field: body -->
Nearchus (c. 360–300 BC) was born in Crete and grew up at Amphipolis in Macedon, where he became one of Alexander's boyhood friends. In 337 BC Philip II exiled him with other companions for supporting Alexander in a quarrel over a marriage alliance with the Carian ruler Pixodarus. After Alexander became king he recalled them, and Nearchus served as governor of Lycia and Pamphylia.

In India, Alexander built a fleet on the Hydaspes and sailed down the Indus. In 325 he chose Nearchus to command the fleet on a voyage of exploration along the unknown coast from the mouth of the Indus to the Persian Gulf, while he himself led the army through the Gedrosian desert. The voyage, of about 2,000 kilometres, was hard: the sailors met monsoon winds, whales, and the "Fish-Eaters" of the coast, and ran short of food. Nearchus rejoined Alexander in Carmania, and at Susa in 324 was honoured with a golden crown.

He later wrote an account of the voyage, which Arrian used for his *Indica*. After Alexander's death he served Antigonus I and disappears from history around 312 BC.
