<!-- field: summary -->
The Jewish priest and general who changed sides in the revolt against Rome and wrote its history in Greek.

<!-- field: significance -->
Our chief source for Jewish history in the Greek and Roman periods, and for the destruction of the Temple in AD 70.

<!-- field: body -->
Josephus (37–c. 100) was born in Jerusalem to a priestly family, and was given a Greek education as well as a Jewish one. When the great revolt against Rome broke out in 66, he was made commander in Galilee. In 67 the Roman general Vespasian besieged him in the fortress of Jotapata; when it fell, Josephus survived a suicide pact among his companions and surrendered. He told Vespasian that he would become emperor; when this came true in 69, he was freed and took the family name Flavius.

He accompanied Titus at the siege of Jerusalem in 70, trying to persuade the defenders to surrender, and witnessed the burning of the Temple. He spent the rest of his life in Rome on an imperial pension.

His *Jewish War*, first written in Aramaic and then reworked in Greek in the 70s, tells the story of the revolt, including the fall of Masada. His *Jewish Antiquities* (93/94), in twenty books, retells Jewish history from the Creation to the revolt for Greek readers. In *Against Apion* he defended the antiquity of the Jews against Greek critics, quoting many lost authors, including Manetho. He also wrote an autobiography defending his conduct.
