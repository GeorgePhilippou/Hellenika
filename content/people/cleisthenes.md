<!-- field: summary -->
Architect of the Athenian democratic constitution in 508/7 BC, who rebuilt the citizen body from scratch to break the power of regional clans.

<!-- field: significance -->
Created the institutional machinery — ten artificial tribes, the Council of 500, the deme registry — that made mass self-government workable.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleisthenes' central move was ingenious social engineering. He divided Attica into three zones — city, coast and inland — split each into thirty units called *trittyes*, and built ten new tribes by combining one trittys from each zone. Every tribe therefore contained citizens from across Attica with no shared local loyalty, which severed the link between aristocratic families and their regional power bases.

Citizenship was registered through the *deme*, the local village or ward, and a man's demotic replaced his father's name in official use — replacing lineage with residence as the mark of identity. The Council of 500, fifty from each tribe chosen by lot, set the assembly's agenda.
