<!-- field: summary -->
Architect of the Athenian democratic constitution in 508/7 BC, who rebuilt the citizen body from scratch to break the power of regional clans.

<!-- field: significance -->
Created the institutional machinery — ten artificial tribes, the Council of 500, the deme registry — that made mass self-government workable.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleisthenes (active c. 525–507 BC) was an Athenian aristocrat of the powerful Alcmaeonid family who created the democratic constitution of Athens in 508/7 BC. After the tyrant Hippias was expelled in 510 BC, with Spartan help that the Alcmaeonids had encouraged through the Delphic oracle, Cleisthenes lost a power struggle with his rival Isagoras. He then turned to the ordinary people for support, promising reform. When Isagoras called in Spartan troops, the Athenian people rose and drove them out, and Cleisthenes carried his reforms.

He reorganised the whole citizen body. Attica was divided into about 140 local units called demes, grouped into thirty *trittyes* in three zones — city, coast and inland. Ten new tribes were formed, each combining one trittys from each zone, so that every tribe mixed men from all over Attica and no aristocratic family could dominate a tribe through local influence. Citizenship was registered in the deme, and men were officially identified by their deme rather than their father's name.

A new Council of 500, with fifty men from each tribe chosen by lot, prepared business for the Assembly of all citizens. The ten tribes also provided the army's regiments and, from 501 BC, the ten generals. Ostracism, the vote to exile a citizen for ten years, is traditionally credited to him too.

Cleisthenes then disappears from the record, but his system remained the framework of Athenian democracy for nearly two centuries.
