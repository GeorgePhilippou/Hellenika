<!-- field: summary -->
The third Ptolemaic king (246–222 BC), whose victories over the Seleucids and patronage of the Library mark the peak of Ptolemaic Egypt.

<!-- field: significance -->
Under him the Ptolemaic empire reached its greatest extent and Alexandria's Library its greatest ambition.

<!-- field: body -->
Ptolemy III (c. 284–222 BC) was the son of Ptolemy II. On his accession in 246 BC he married Berenice II, heiress of Cyrene, reuniting that region with Egypt. In the same year his sister, the widow of the Seleucid king Antiochus II, was murdered with her young son in Antioch, and Ptolemy invaded the Seleucid empire in the Third Syrian War (246–241 BC). His own inscription claims that he reached Mesopotamia and brought home sacred statues the Persians had carried off from Egypt, earning him the title *Euergetes*, "benefactor".

At home he built temples in the Egyptian manner, including the great temple of Horus at Edfu, begun in 237 BC, and in Alexandria the Serapeum, the main temple of Serapis, whose foundation plaques bear his name. In 238 BC the Egyptian priests honoured him and Berenice in the Canopus Decree.

He was a keen collector for the Library. According to Galen, ships arriving at Alexandria were searched for books, which were copied and the copies returned to their owners, and Ptolemy borrowed Athens' official texts of Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides against a deposit of fifteen talents, then kept the originals and let the deposit go. He died in 222 BC.
