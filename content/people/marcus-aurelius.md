<!-- field: summary -->
The Stoic Roman emperor (AD 161–180) whose private notebook, the Meditations, was written in Greek, and who endowed philosophy teaching at Athens.

<!-- field: significance -->
The best-known philosopher-emperor, and a patron of Greek learning.

<!-- field: body -->
Marcus Aurelius was adopted by Antoninus Pius in AD 138 and became emperor in 161. As a boy he was trained in Greek and Latin rhetoric and was drawn to philosophy, studying Stoicism under Junius Rusticus, who gave him the works of Epictetus. His reign was dominated by war on the Danube frontier and against Parthia, and by the Antonine plague, which was brought back by the army and killed a significant part of the population.

He wrote the *Meditations* in Greek during his campaigns, as private exercises in Stoic self-discipline, titled *To Himself*. In AD 176 he visited Athens, was initiated into the Eleusinian Mysteries and set up salaried chairs of philosophy for the four main schools, giving the city's teaching a new official footing, with Herodes Atticus as a prominent local patron.

He was succeeded by his son Commodus. The *Meditations* has remained among the most widely read of ancient works, and his reign has long been seen as the high point of Roman rule.
