<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian architect of the 5th century BC who designed the temple of Athena Nike and, with Iktinos, the Parthenon.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the architects of Periclean Athens; the Nike temple is the jewel of the Ionic order on the Acropolis.

<!-- field: body -->
Callicrates was one of the leading architects of Athens in the age of Pericles. Plutarch names him with Iktinos as the builder of the Parthenon (447–432 BC), though the architect Vitruvius mentions only Iktinos, and how the work was divided between them is unknown.

An Athenian decree, usually dated around 448 BC, orders a temple to be built for Athena Nike, "as Callicrates shall specify", on the bastion at the entrance to the Acropolis. The small Ionic temple was not actually built until the 420s, perhaps to Callicrates' revised design. Another inscription records him as architect of repairs or a new wall on the Acropolis, and Plutarch says he took the contract for the middle of the Long Walls linking Athens to the Piraeus.

Some scholars attribute to him other temples of the period built in a similar style, such as the temple of Athena at Sounion or the Ionic temple by the Ilissos river (now lost), but the evidence is circumstantial.
