<!-- field: summary -->
Athenian general turned historian of the Peloponnesian War, and the founder of critical, evidence-based historical method.

<!-- field: significance -->
Stripped the gods and the marvellous out of historical explanation, replacing them with power, interest and human nature — the model for political history ever since.

<!-- field: body -->
Thucydides commanded an Athenian fleet in 424 BC, failed to relieve Amphipolis, and was exiled for twenty years. He used the time to interview participants on both sides, which is precisely why the work is not a partisan Athenian account.

He states his method explicitly: he rejects poetic embellishment, cross-checks eyewitness reports against each other, and warns his book will be less entertaining as a result. He intends it as "a possession for all time". His analysis of how war corrodes language and morality — the account of civil war at Corcyra — is still routinely cited by political scientists.

The history breaks off mid-sentence in 411 BC, seven years before the war ended.
