<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian politician whose reforms of 462/1 BC took power from the aristocratic Areopagus and completed the democracy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The reformer who turned Cleisthenes' system into radical democracy — and was murdered for it.

<!-- field: body -->
Ephialtes (died 461 BC) was a leading Athenian democratic politician of the 460s BC, known for his honesty and his prosecutions of corrupt officials. Little else is known of his life; he appears mainly in Aristotle's *Constitution of the Athenians* and in Plutarch.

The main remaining stronghold of aristocratic power in Athens was the Areopagus, a council of former archons who served for life and had broad powers to supervise officials and protect the laws. In 462/1 BC, while the conservative leader Cimon was away helping Sparta against a helot revolt, Ephialtes persuaded the Assembly to strip the Areopagus of these powers, leaving it chiefly a court for homicide. Its political functions passed to the Council of 500, the Assembly and the popular law courts — bodies drawn from the whole citizen body.

The reforms were bitterly contested. When Cimon returned, humiliated after Sparta sent his army home, he was ostracised. Soon afterwards Ephialtes was assassinated, one of the very few political murders in Classical Athens. His younger ally Pericles took over leadership of the democrats and extended the reforms, introducing pay for jurors.

Aeschylus' *Eumenides*, staged in 458 BC, which presents the Areopagus as a court founded by Athena to try homicide, has often been read as a response to these divisive events.
