<!-- field: summary -->
The king of Syracuse from about 270 to 215 BC, ally of Rome and patron of Archimedes, under whom Syracuse enjoyed its last golden age.

<!-- field: significance -->
His long, stable reign kept Syracuse independent and prosperous between Rome and Carthage.

<!-- field: body -->
Hiero II (c. 308–215 BC) was an officer under Pyrrhus during his Sicilian campaign, and was chosen general by the Syracusan army around 275 BC. After defeating the Mamertines — Italian mercenaries who had seized Messana — around 269, he was hailed as king.

When the First Punic War broke out in 264 BC over Messana, Hiero first sided with Carthage, but in 263 he made peace with Rome and became its ally. He kept that alliance for the rest of his life, supplying grain and money to Roman armies, and Syracuse kept its independence in eastern Sicily while the rest of the island became Rome's first province.

His reign was peaceful and prosperous. His grain-tax law, the *lex Hieronica*, was later kept by the Romans. He enlarged the great theatre of Syracuse, built a huge altar for public sacrifices, and had a giant ship, the *Syracusia*, built and sent as a gift to Ptolemy III in Egypt.

The mathematician Archimedes, said to be his relative, worked for him: on the story told by Vitruvius, Hiero asked him to find out whether a goldsmith had cheated him over a crown. Hiero died in 215 BC aged about ninety; his grandson joined Carthage, and Rome sacked Syracuse in 212.
