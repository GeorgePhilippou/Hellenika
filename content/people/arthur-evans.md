<!-- field: summary -->
Excavator of Knossos, who named the Minoan civilisation and reconstructed its palace in painted concrete.

<!-- field: significance -->
Revealed Europe's earliest civilisation — and shaped how it is seen through reconstructions that are substantially his own interpretation.

<!-- field: body -->
Evans bought the Kephala mound and began digging in 1900. Within weeks he had uncovered frescoes, storage magazines and clay tablets in two unknown scripts. He named the civilisation "Minoan" after the legendary king Minos, and identified the complex with the labyrinth of the Minotaur myth.

His "reconstitutions" used reinforced concrete to rebuild columns, staircases and upper storeys, and commissioned Émile Gilliéron and his son to restore frescoes from fragments — sometimes from very few fragments. The famous "Prince of the Lilies" combines pieces that may not belong to one figure, or even to one sex. Visitors to Knossos are looking at an Edwardian interpretation as much as a Bronze Age building.

Against that: he excavated carefully by the standards of his day, published in detail, and preserved the site. He also insisted, correctly, that the Linear B tablets were an administrative archive — while wrongly refusing to accept that the language could be Greek.
