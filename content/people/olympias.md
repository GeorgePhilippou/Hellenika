Molossian princess, wife of Philip II, mother of Alexander, and a ruthless political operator in the succession wars after his death.

<!-- field: body -->
Olympias (c. 375–316 BC) was a princess of the Molossians, the royal house of Epirus in north-western Greece, which claimed descent from Achilles. Around 357 BC she married Philip II of Macedon, and in 356 BC gave birth to Alexander the Great.

Ancient writers, mostly hostile to her, describe her devotion to ecstatic Dionysiac rites, including handling snakes, and she encouraged the story that Alexander's real father was Zeus — a claim that suited his later ideas about his own divinity. When Philip married a young Macedonian noblewoman, Cleopatra, in 337 BC, threatening Alexander's succession, Olympias withdrew to Epirus. After Philip's murder in 336 BC she was widely suspected of involvement, and she had Cleopatra and her infant child killed.

She stayed influential through Alexander's reign, in constant conflict with his regent in Macedon, Antipater. In the wars after Alexander's death she backed his young son Alexander IV, and in 317 BC invaded Macedon with an Epirote army. The Macedonian troops went over to her; she had Alexander's half-brother King Philip III Arrhidaeus killed and forced his wife Eurydice to take her own life.

Cassander, Antipater's son, then besieged her at Pydna. She surrendered in 316 BC on a promise that her life would be spared, but was condemned by an assembly and killed by relatives of those she had executed. She is one of the few women of the Greek world known to have wielded open political and military power.
