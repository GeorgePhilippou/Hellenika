<!-- field: summary -->
Molossian princess, wife of Philip II, mother of Alexander, and a ruthless political operator in the succession wars after his death.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the few women in Greek history who exercised open political and military power, and was executed for it.

<!-- field: body -->
Olympias came from the royal house of Epirus and claimed descent from Achilles — a lineage Alexander took seriously. Ancient sources describe her involvement in ecstatic Dionysiac religion, including handling snakes, though these accounts come from writers hostile to her.

After Philip took another wife she was sidelined, and after his murder she was widely suspected of involvement. During the wars of the Diadochi she invaded Macedon, captured and executed Philip III Arrhidaeus and his wife Eurydice, and was besieged by Cassander at Pydna. She surrendered on terms and was killed anyway in 316 BC, reportedly by relatives of those she had executed, since Cassander's soldiers refused to do it.
