<!-- field: summary -->
King of Macedon who destroyed the Achaemenid Empire in a decade of campaigning, reached the Indus, and died at 32 without an heir.

<!-- field: significance -->
Redrew the political map from the Adriatic to the Punjab and made Greek the language of government and learning across the Near East for a thousand years.

<!-- field: body -->
Alexander became king at twenty, immediately suppressed revolts in Greece — destroying Thebes as an example — and crossed into Asia in 334 BC. He won at the Granicus, Issus and Gaugamela, took Tyre by building a causeway to an island city, was crowned in Egypt, and burned Persepolis.

He kept going: through Bactria and Sogdiana, over the Hindu Kush, into the Punjab, where he beat Porus and his elephants at the Hydaspes in monsoon rain. At the Hyphasis his men refused to march further. The return through Gedrosia killed thousands.

He was not simply a conqueror on the Greek model. He kept Persian administrators, adopted elements of Persian ceremonial, and married Roxana and then Darius' daughter. He also killed his friend Cleitus in a drunken quarrel and had Parmenion murdered. He died in Babylon in 323 BC after eleven days of fever, and his empire was fought over within hours.
