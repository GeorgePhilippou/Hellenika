<!-- field: summary -->
The forceful Spartan king of c. 520–490 BC who intervened in Athens, crushed Argos and made Sparta leader of the Peloponnese.

<!-- field: significance -->
The king who made Sparta the strongest power in Greece on the eve of the Persian Wars.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleomenes I was king of Sparta from the Agiad royal house from about 520 to about 490 BC. Our knowledge of him comes almost entirely from Herodotus, who portrays him as able but unstable.

In 510 BC he led the Spartan army that drove the tyrant Hippias out of Athens. Two years later he returned to support the aristocrat Isagoras against Cleisthenes, expelling hundreds of families, but the Athenian people rose up and besieged him on the Acropolis, forcing him to leave — the moment that made Cleisthenes' democratic reforms possible. An attempt to restore Hippias failed when Sparta's allies refused to follow him.

In 499 BC Aristagoras of Miletus asked him to help the Ionian Revolt against Persia; Herodotus says that Cleomenes' young daughter Gorgo warned him against the bribes offered. Around 494 he crushed Argos at Sepeia, then burned a sacred grove in which Argive survivors had taken refuge. He later had his fellow king Demaratus deposed by bribing the Delphic oracle. When this was discovered he fled, was brought back, and died around 490 BC; Herodotus says he went mad and mutilated himself. His half-brother Leonidas succeeded him and married Gorgo.
