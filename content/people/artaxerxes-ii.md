<!-- field: summary -->
The Persian king from 404 to 358 BC who defeated his brother at Cunaxa and made Persia the arbiter of Greece through the King's Peace.

<!-- field: significance -->
The longest-reigning Achaemenid, and the king who restored Persian influence over the Greek cities of Asia.

<!-- field: body -->
Artaxerxes II succeeded his father Darius II in 404 BC. His younger brother Cyrus, satrap in Anatolia, raised an army that included thousands of Greek mercenaries and marched on Babylon, but was killed at Cunaxa in 401 BC. The Greek force, Xenophon among them, withdrew to the Black Sea.

When Sparta turned on Persia, Artaxerxes subsidised its enemies. His admiral Conon with Pharnabazus destroyed the Spartan fleet at Cnidus in 394 BC. Later the king dictated the peace of 387/386 BC, the King's Peace, by which the Greeks of Asia were again his subjects while the cities of Greece itself were declared autonomous, and Sparta was left to enforce it.

His later years were troubled by rebellions of the satraps in western Asia and the loss of Egypt, which stayed independent. He rebuilt buildings at Susa and Persepolis and was succeeded by his son Artaxerxes III after a dispute about the succession.
