<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian orator who favoured peace with Philip II, and the lifelong rival of Demosthenes.

<!-- field: significance -->
The other side of the great debate over Macedon, whose surviving speeches show Athenian democracy at work.

<!-- field: body -->
Aeschines (c. 389–314 BC) came from a modest Athenian family and worked as an actor and a clerk before entering politics. He served on the embassies to Philip II of Macedon that negotiated the Peace of Philocrates in 346 BC, and from then on he argued that Athens should make peace and come to terms with Macedon.

This made him the great opponent of Demosthenes, who accused him of taking bribes from Philip and of betraying Athens. Their rivalry produced three pairs of speeches, the only such pairs to survive from Athens. In 343 BC Demosthenes prosecuted him for his conduct on the embassy; Aeschines was narrowly acquitted. In 330 BC Aeschines attacked a proposal to award Demosthenes a gold crown for his services, in the speech *Against Ctesiphon*. Demosthenes replied with *On the Crown*, his masterpiece. Aeschines won less than a fifth of the jury's votes, was fined, and left Athens.

He settled on Rhodes, where, by tradition, he taught rhetoric. His three surviving speeches are vivid sources for Athenian politics and law in the age of Philip.
