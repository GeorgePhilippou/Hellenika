<!-- field: summary -->
Sicilian philosopher, poet and self-styled holy man who explained the world as four elements moved by Love and Strife.

<!-- field: significance -->
His four roots became the standard Greek model of matter for two thousand years, and his career shows how blurred the lines between philosopher, healer and prophet could be.

<!-- field: body -->
Empedocles lived in Akragas, in Sicily, in the fifth century BC. Parmenides had argued that nothing can come to be or pass away; Empedocles answered by keeping being intact and locating change in mixture. There are four unchanging roots, and everything else is their combination and separation, driven by Love (which unites) and Strife (which divides). The cosmos passes endlessly between the dominance of each.

Two poems are attributed to him, and their relationship is a long-running puzzle. On Nature is a physics; Purifications is a religious work about the fall of divine spirits into mortal bodies and the path back, with a ban on animal sacrifice and meat. Some scholars see one system in two registers; others see two moods. The Strasbourg papyrus, published in 1999, suggested that the two themes were closer than was thought, since its cosmological passages seem to speak of divine spirits and their cycle of rebirth.

The biography is mostly legend. He is said to have stopped a plague, revived a woman, and declined a crown. The best-known tale, that he threw himself into the crater of Etna to prove he was a god, is likely a later invention, and Diogenes Laertius reports it alongside less flattering versions.

The fragments show a poet as well as a thinker: images of the elements as painters' pigments, and of the eye as a lantern, make abstract argument vivid.
