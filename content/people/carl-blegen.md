<!-- field: summary -->
American archaeologist who excavated the Palace of Nestor at Pylos and re-excavated Troy with modern stratigraphic method.

<!-- field: significance -->
His Pylos tablets made the Linear B decipherment testable, and his Troy sequence remains the framework for the site.

<!-- field: body -->
Carl Blegen (1887–1971) was an American archaeologist, a professor at the University of Cincinnati who worked for most of his life with the American School of Classical Studies at Athens. In the 1920s he excavated smaller sites in the Corinthia such as Korakou and Zygouries, developing careful methods of digging layer by layer and dating each layer by its pottery. With Alan Wace he worked out the basic sequence of the Greek Bronze Age.

From 1932 to 1938 he re-excavated Troy for the University of Cincinnati. He divided the mound into its main cities and dozens of sub-phases, the framework still used. He argued that Troy VI fell to an earthquake, and that Troy VIIa, burned around 1180 BC with storage jars sunk into its floors as if for a siege, was the best candidate for Homer's Troy.

In 1939 he began excavating at Ano Englianos in Messenia, and on the first day found the Palace of Nestor at Pylos and its archive of Linear B tablets, the largest found on the Greek mainland. The war halted work until 1952, the year Michael Ventris announced that Linear B was Greek. A tablet found that season listing tripods beside drawings of them confirmed the decipherment.

Blegen published both sites in full and spent his last years in Athens. His work turned Aegean archaeology into a precise, stratigraphic discipline.
