<!-- field: summary -->
The tyrant of Pherae who united Thessaly in the 370s BC and seemed poised to dominate Greece before his murder in 370 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
His brief career anticipated Philip II: a northern ruler using a professional army to unite a region and dominate the Greek cities.

<!-- field: body -->
Jason ruled the Thessalian city of Pherae in the 370s BC. Xenophon, our main source, gives a vivid picture of him through the speech of a Thessalian, Polydamas of Pharsalus, who warned Sparta that Jason was the most powerful man of his time. He kept a professional army of about 6,000 mercenaries, whom he trained hard and shared hardships with, and by about 374 had brought the cities of Thessaly together under him as *tagos*, the federal war-leader, controlling large forces of cavalry and infantry.

He was an ally of Thebes and of Athens. After the Theban victory over Sparta at Leuctra in 371 BC he marched south, and rather than help Thebes finish off the Spartans he arranged a truce that let them withdraw — keeping the balance of power in his own favour. He was said to be planning a campaign against Persia.

In 370 BC he announced that he would preside over the Pythian Games at Delphi and ordered a vast sacrifice of cattle, alarming the Greeks. Before the festival he was stabbed to death by seven young men while reviewing his cavalry. Thessaly fell into disorder, and twenty years later Philip II of Macedon took his place.
