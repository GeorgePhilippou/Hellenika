The Athenian orator who led opposition to Macedon after Demosthenes, inspired the Lamian War and was executed by Antipater.

<!-- field: significance -->
A brilliant, witty orator of the last years of Athenian independence, whose speeches have been recovered from papyri.

<!-- field: body -->
Hyperides (c. 390–322 BC) was a wealthy Athenian, a pupil of Isocrates, who became one of the leading orators of the 4th century BC. He worked as a speechwriter and prosecutor, and was famous for his wit and elegance; ancient critics ranked him second only to Demosthenes.

Politically he was a firm opponent of Macedon and an ally of Demosthenes, though he later prosecuted Demosthenes in the Harpalus affair of 324 BC. He is remembered for a celebrated defence of the courtesan Phryne, charged with impiety, in which, by later legend, he won acquittal by revealing her beauty to the jury.

After Alexander's death in 323 BC, Hyperides was a leader of the revolt that became the Lamian War, and delivered the funeral oration for the Athenian dead in its first year, part of which survives. When the war was lost in 322 BC, Antipater demanded the surrender of the anti-Macedonian leaders. Hyperides fled, was captured and executed.

His speeches were lost after antiquity. From 1847 onwards, papyri bought in Egypt restored several of them, and in 2002 parts of two more were identified in the Archimedes Palimpsest.
