<!-- field: summary -->
The queen of Ptolemy III, heiress of Cyrene, whose dedicated lock of hair became a constellation.

<!-- field: significance -->
A powerful Ptolemaic queen celebrated in poetry and the stars, remembered through Callimachus' poem and Catullus' Latin version of it.

<!-- field: body -->
Berenice II (c. 267–221 BC) was the daughter of Magas, king of Cyrene in Libya. She was promised to the heir of Egypt, but after her father's death her mother arranged instead a marriage to a Macedonian prince, Demetrius the Fair. Ancient writers say Berenice herself had him killed when he became her mother's lover. In 246 BC she married Ptolemy III, bringing Cyrene back under Egyptian rule.

When her husband went to war against the Seleucids, she vowed a lock of her hair to the gods for his safe return and placed it in a temple. The lock disappeared, and the court astronomer Conon of Samos announced that it had been placed among the stars — the constellation still called Coma Berenices, "Berenice's Hair". Callimachus wrote a poem, *The Lock of Berenice*, which survives mainly through Catullus' Latin translation. Poets also celebrated her victories in chariot races at the Greek games.

Berenice appears on coins and was honoured with her husband in the Canopus Decree of 238 BC. After Ptolemy III died in 222 BC, her son Ptolemy IV's chief minister Sosibius had her murdered.
