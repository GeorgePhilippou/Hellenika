<!-- field: summary -->
The Bactrian noblewoman who married Alexander the Great in 327 BC and bore his only legitimate heir.

<!-- field: significance -->
Mother of Alexander IV, the last of the Argead royal house, whose murder ended Alexander's dynasty.

<!-- field: body -->
Roxana was the daughter of Oxyartes, a nobleman of Bactria or Sogdia in modern Afghanistan and Uzbekistan, who resisted Alexander during the hard campaign in Central Asia. When Alexander captured the mountain stronghold known as the Sogdian Rock around 327 BC, Roxana was among the captives, and he married her the same year. Ancient writers said it was a love match, but it also helped win over the local aristocracy.

She travelled with Alexander to India and back. When he died at Babylon in 323 BC she was pregnant, and a few months later bore a son, Alexander IV, who was proclaimed joint king with Alexander's half-brother Philip III. Plutarch says Roxana had Alexander's Persian wife Stateira murdered soon after his death.

Mother and son became pawns in the wars of the Successors. They were taken to Macedon and placed under the protection of Alexander's mother Olympias, but in 316 Cassander captured them at Pydna and kept them under guard at Amphipolis. Around 310 BC, as Alexander IV approached manhood, Cassander had them both secretly killed. The young king may be the boy buried in Tomb III at Vergina.
