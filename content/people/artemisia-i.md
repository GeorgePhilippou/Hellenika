<!-- field: summary -->
The queen of Halicarnassus who commanded ships for Xerxes at Salamis in 480 BC — the only woman commander in Herodotus' account of the war.

<!-- field: significance -->
A rare woman commander in a Greek war, praised by Herodotus, a fellow citizen of Halicarnassus, for her judgement and courage.

<!-- field: body -->
Artemisia (active around 480 BC) ruled the Greek city of Halicarnassus in Caria, south-western Asia Minor, and nearby islands as a vassal of the Persian king. After her husband's death she governed on behalf of her young son. When Xerxes invaded Greece in 480 BC she joined his fleet with five ships, which Herodotus calls among the best in the navy.

Herodotus, himself from Halicarnassus, gives her a prominent role. Before the battle of Salamis, she alone among Xerxes' commanders advised him not to fight at sea, warning that the Greeks were stronger on water and that he could win by waiting. Xerxes praised her advice but did not take it.

In the battle, chased by an Athenian ship, she escaped by ramming and sinking another ship of the Persian side. The Athenian captain, thinking she must be an ally, broke off the pursuit, while Xerxes, watching from the shore and thinking she had sunk an enemy, is said to have remarked: "My men have become women, and my women men." After the defeat she advised Xerxes to return to Asia, and he entrusted his children to her care.

The Athenians, Herodotus says, offered a large reward for her capture, angry that a woman had fought against them. Later legend added a romantic death, which has no basis.
