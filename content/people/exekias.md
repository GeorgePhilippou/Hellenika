<!-- field: summary -->
The greatest Athenian black-figure vase painter, active around 545–525 BC, famous for his Achilles and Ajax amphora.

<!-- field: significance -->
The master of black-figure painting, who brought drama and psychological depth to Greek vase painting just before the invention of red-figure.

<!-- field: body -->
Exekias worked in Athens around 545–525 BC as both potter and painter. His name is known because he signed his vases — some as potter (*Exekias epoiēsen*, "Exekias made me"), a few as both potter and painter. Scholars attribute a few dozen vases to his hand.

He worked in the black-figure technique, painting figures in black slip and incising fine details through it. He brought to it a new seriousness and refinement. His most famous work, an amphora in the Vatican, shows Achilles and Ajax absorbed in a board game during a pause in the Trojan War. Another amphora, in Boulogne, shows Ajax alone, carefully fixing his sword in the ground before he kills himself — the moment before the act, not the act itself. A cup in Munich shows Dionysus reclining in a ship whose mast has sprouted vines, sailing among dolphins over a coral-red sea, one of the first cups painted across its whole interior.

Exekias' figures are often still and thoughtful, with intricately detailed armour and cloaks. His work marks the height of black-figure painting; within a few years, painters in his workshop tradition took up the new red-figure technique.
