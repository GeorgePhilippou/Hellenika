<!-- field: summary -->
The revolutionary Spartan king of 235–222 BC who carried out land reform and briefly made Sparta a great power again.

<!-- field: significance -->
The last attempt to revive classical Sparta, ended by the Macedonian victory at Sellasia in 222 BC.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleomenes III became king of Sparta, from the Agiad house, around 235 BC. He was the son of Leonidas II, the enemy of Agis IV, but he married Agis' widow Agiatis and adopted Agis' programme.

In 227 BC, after early successes against the Achaean League, he seized power in a coup. He killed four of the five ephors, abolished their office, cancelled debts and redivided the land, and raised the citizen body to about 4,000 by enfranchising perioikoi. He revived the old Spartan training (*agōgē*) and common messes, and reorganised the army in the Macedonian style. With this new army he defeated the Achaean League repeatedly and won over much of the Peloponnese.

In alarm, Aratus, leader of the Achaean League, turned to Sparta's old enemy Macedon. King Antigonus III Doson invaded, and in 222 BC crushed Cleomenes at Sellasia, north of Sparta. Cleomenes fled to Ptolemaic Egypt. In 219, held under guard in Alexandria by Ptolemy IV, he tried to raise the city in revolt; when it failed, he and his companions killed themselves. Polybius, from the Achaean side, is hostile; Plutarch's *Life* is admiring.
