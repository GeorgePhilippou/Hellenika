<!-- field: summary -->
The Persian king from 465 to 424 BC, who saw off Athenian intervention in Egypt and presided over the end of open war between Persia and the Greeks.

<!-- field: significance -->
His long reign coincides with the first phase of Athenian imperial power, and the end of the Persian Wars.

<!-- field: body -->
Artaxerxes came to the throne in 465 BC after the assassination of his father Xerxes by Artabanus, the captain of the guard. After suppressing his rivals, he faced a revolt in Egypt led by Inaros, supported by about two hundred Athenian ships. The Persian army defeated the rebels about 454 BC, and the Athenian fleet was largely lost.

Greek sources record that Themistocles fled to his court in 465 BC or later, and was honoured with the revenues of three cities. After Athens' attack on Cyprus ended in 450 BC with the death of Cimon, a peace may have been agreed, later called the Peace of Callias. Whether a formal treaty existed has been debated since the fourth century BC.

In the Hebrew Bible, Artaxerxes also sends Ezra and Nehemiah to Jerusalem. He was followed by his son Xerxes II, who was murdered within weeks, and finally by Darius II. The empire stayed strong while Greeks fought each other.
