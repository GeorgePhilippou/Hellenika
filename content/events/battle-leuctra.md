<!-- field: summary -->
Epaminondas destroyed the myth of Spartan invincibility with a massed left wing, killing 400 of 700 Spartiates present.

<!-- field: significance -->
The battle that ended Spartan supremacy in Greece and showed how tactics could beat a reputation.

<!-- field: body -->
Fought in July 371 BC at Leuctra in southern Boeotia, between Thebes and its Boeotian allies under Epaminondas and a Spartan army under King Cleombrotus. Sparta was trying to break up the Boeotian League that Thebes led.

Greek armies normally put their best troops on the right wing, so the strongest units of two armies rarely met. Epaminondas reversed this. He massed his left wing fifty ranks deep, directly against the Spartan king and his best troops on the Spartan right, and held his weaker right wing back. The Theban cavalry drove off the Spartan horse, and the deep Theban column, led by the elite Sacred Band, smashed the Spartan right before the rest of the lines had properly engaged.

Cleombrotus was killed, along with about 400 of the 700 full Spartan citizens (Spartiates) present. With perhaps only 1,500 Spartiates left in all, this was a loss Sparta could not replace. The myth of Spartan invincibility, which had lasted for centuries, was broken in an afternoon.

Thebes followed up immediately. In 370–369 BC Epaminondas invaded Laconia, the first enemy army to do so in living memory, and freed Messenia, whose helots had farmed the land on which the Spartan system depended. Thebes became the leading power in Greece until Epaminondas' death at Mantinea in 362 BC. The young Philip II of Macedon, then a hostage in Thebes, learned from his methods.
