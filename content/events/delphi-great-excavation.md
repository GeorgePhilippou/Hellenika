<!-- field: summary -->
The French excavation of the sanctuary of Delphi in 1892–1903, for which a whole village was moved.

<!-- field: significance -->
It uncovered the most famous oracle of antiquity, with the Charioteer and the treasuries of the Sacred Way.

<!-- field: body -->
For centuries the site of the Delphic sanctuary lay buried under the village of Kastri, whose houses stood on top of the ruins. Travellers from the 17th century on recognised inscriptions and walls, but systematic excavation was impossible while the village stood.

In 1891, after long negotiations — linked, it was said, to a trade agreement on Greek currants — the Greek government granted France the right to excavate, and the villagers were rehoused at a new site to the west, the modern village of Delphi. From 1892 to 1903 the French School at Athens under Théophile Homolle carried out what became known as the "Great Excavation", employing hundreds of workers and a light railway to remove the earth.

They uncovered the temple of Apollo, the theatre, the stadium, the Sacred Way with its treasuries, and thousands of inscriptions, using Pausanias' description as their guide. Among the finds were the twin kouroi known as Kleobis and Biton, the bronze Charioteer, found in 1896, and two hymns to Apollo of the 2nd century BC with musical notation, which were performed again in Paris. The Athenian Treasury was rebuilt from its own blocks in 1903–06. Excavation and study have continued ever since.
