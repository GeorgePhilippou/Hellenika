<!-- field: summary -->
The Roman legion defeated the Macedonian phalanx for the first time, on broken ground in Thessaly.

<!-- field: body -->
The two armies collided unexpectedly in fog on the "Dog's Heads" ridge. Philip's right formed up and pushed the Roman left back downhill. His left, still in march column on rough ground, was caught before it could deploy.

An unnamed Roman tribune then took twenty maniples out of the victorious Roman right and marched them into the rear of Philip's successful wing. A phalanx cannot turn. It was destroyed from behind.

Polybius drew the conclusion that became doctrine: the phalanx is unbeatable frontally on level ground and helpless if it cannot get that ground or maintain its line. Flamininus then proclaimed at the Isthmian Games that the Greek cities were free — to enormous acclaim, and under Roman supervision.
