<!-- field: summary -->
Athenian and Plataean hoplites defeated a larger Persian landing force in 490 BC — the first Greek victory over Persia.

<!-- field: body -->
The Persians landed at Marathon intending to draw the Athenian army out and take the city. Athens marched to meet them with about 10,000 hoplites and 1,000 Plataeans against a Persian force of perhaps 25,000.

Miltiades thinned the Greek centre to extend the line and avoid being outflanked, and strengthened the wings. The Greeks advanced at a run over the last stretch to minimise time under archery. The centre gave ground as expected; the wings won and turned inward, enveloping the Persian centre.

Herodotus gives 6,400 Persian dead against 192 Athenians. The Athenians then marched roughly 40 km back to Athens the same day to face the Persian fleet, which sailed away.

The result mattered beyond the numbers: Persian infantry, in the open, could be beaten by hoplites.
