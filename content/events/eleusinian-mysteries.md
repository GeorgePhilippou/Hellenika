<!-- field: summary -->
The most important mystery cult of ancient Greece, in which initiates at Eleusis were promised a happier afterlife through the myth of Demeter and Persephone.

<!-- field: significance -->
A cult open to all who spoke Greek, which lasted for about two thousand years and whose secrets remain undisclosed.

<!-- field: body -->
The Mysteries of Eleusis were celebrated each year for the goddess Demeter and her daughter Persephone. The story in the *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* tells how Persephone was abducted by Hades, how her mother, grieving, brought famine, and how Zeus arranged for the girl to return for part of each year. Demeter taught the Eleusinians her rites.

The Lesser Mysteries, in spring, prepared candidates. The Greater Mysteries took place in the autumn month of Boedromion and lasted about nine days. Initiates bathed in the sea, fasted and drank a barley drink called *kykeon*. A procession carrying sacred objects went along the Sacred Way from Athens to Eleusis, where rites were held at night in a great hall called the Telesterion. Initiates were sworn not to reveal what they saw, and the secret has been kept.

Participation, open to free men, women and even slaves, was thought to bring a better lot after death. Roman emperors including Hadrian and Marcus Aurelius were initiated. In 415 BC Alcibiades was accused of mocking the Mysteries in private houses, and the charge helped bring about his downfall. The cult ended in the late fourth century AD, when Theodosius I banned pagan rites.
