<!-- field: summary -->
An alliance against Persia founded in 478 BC that Athens converted within a generation into an empire.

<!-- field: significance -->
The alliance that won the Aegean back from Persia and became the Athenian empire whose tribute paid for the Parthenon.

<!-- field: body -->
An alliance of Greek states, mostly islands and coastal cities of the Aegean, formed under Athenian leadership in 478 BC, after the Persian invasion of Greece had been defeated. Its purpose was to continue the war against Persia and free the Greek cities still under Persian rule. It lasted until Athens' defeat in 404 BC.

Members swore to have the same friends and enemies, and contributed either ships or money to a common treasury kept on the sacred island of Delos, where the League's council met. Under the Athenian general Cimon it drove Persian forces out of the Aegean, winning a great victory at the Eurymedon around 466 BC.

As the Persian threat faded, the League became an Athenian empire. Members that tried to leave — Naxos around 470 BC, Thasos in 465 BC — were besieged and forced back. Most allies chose to pay money rather than supply ships, so only Athens kept a fleet. In 454 BC the treasury was moved from Delos to Athens, and tribute soon paid for Pericles' buildings on the Acropolis. Athens imposed its coinage and measures on its allies, settled cleruchies (colonies of Athenian citizens) on their land, and moved important court cases to Athens.

The Athenian Tribute Lists, inscriptions recording the share of each ally's payment given to Athena, survive on stone and show who paid what year by year. Resentment of Athenian rule was one of the causes of the Peloponnesian War, in which Sparta claimed to be fighting to free the Greeks. With Athens' surrender in 404 BC the League was dissolved.
