<!-- field: summary -->
The largest battle of the Diadochi wars, where Seleucus' 400 elephants decided the fate of Alexander's empire.

<!-- field: body -->
Antigonus the One-Eyed came closest to reuniting Alexander's empire, which is why the others allied against him. At Ipsus in Phrygia perhaps 150,000 men met.

Demetrius drove off the opposing cavalry and pursued too far. Seleucus then interposed his 400 war elephants — obtained from Chandragupta Maurya in exchange for eastern territory — as a screen between Demetrius and the battlefield. Horses will not approach elephants, and Demetrius could not get back.

Antigonus, aged around 80, was left unsupported and killed by javelins, reportedly still expecting his son to return. With him died the last realistic prospect of a single empire.

The victors carved up his territory among themselves rather than reuniting it under one ruler: Lysimachus took most of Anatolia, Seleucus gained Syria, and Cassander and Ptolemy consolidated their existing holdings. This partition, more than any single treaty, fixed the basic territorial pattern of Macedon, the Seleucid empire, Egypt and the smaller Anatolian kingdoms that would define the Hellenistic world for the next hundred years.
