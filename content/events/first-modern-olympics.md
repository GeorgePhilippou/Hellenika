<!-- field: summary -->
The first modern Olympic Games, held in Athens in April 1896 in the rebuilt Panathenaic Stadium.

<!-- field: significance -->
The revival of the ancient Games as the world's greatest sporting festival, staged in Greece as a tribute to its classical past.

<!-- field: body -->
The excavation of Olympia by German archaeologists from 1875 revived interest in the ancient Games. In Greece a wealthy patriot, Evangelos Zappas, had already funded national "Olympics" in Athens in 1859, 1870 and 1875. In 1894 the French educator Pierre de Coubertin, who believed in sport as a moral education, held a congress at the Sorbonne that founded the International Olympic Committee, with the Greek writer Demetrios Vikelas as its first president, and chose Athens to host the first Games.

The Panathenaic Stadium, built in marble by Herodes Atticus in the 2nd century AD, was excavated and rebuilt in white marble with money from the merchant Georgios Averoff. The Games were held on 6–15 April 1896, with about 240 athletes, all men, from some fourteen countries, competing in athletics, wrestling, gymnastics, cycling, fencing, shooting, swimming and tennis. The American James Connolly won the first event, the triple jump.

The French scholar Michel Bréal proposed a new race from Marathon to Athens, inspired by the legend of the messenger who announced the victory of 490 BC. It was won by the Greek water-carrier Spyridon Louis, to the delight of the home crowd, and became the symbolic event of the modern Games.
