<!-- field: summary -->
Alexander defeated Darius III in person on a narrow coastal plain, capturing the Persian royal family.

<!-- field: body -->
Darius manoeuvred behind Alexander and cut his supply lines — a good move that placed his own much larger army on a coastal strip too narrow to deploy in.

Alexander led the Companions in an oblique charge across the Pinarus river directly at Darius' chariot. The phalanx struggled crossing the riverbed against Greek mercenaries, but once Darius turned and fled the Persian line dissolved.

The royal camp fell with Darius' mother, wife and two daughters. Alexander guaranteed their status and treated them, the sources agree, with conspicuous respect. Darius offered ransom, territory west of the Euphrates and a daughter in marriage. Parmenion said he would accept if he were Alexander; Alexander replied that he would too, if he were Parmenion.
