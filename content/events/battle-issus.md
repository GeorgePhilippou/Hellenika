Alexander defeated Darius III in person on a narrow coastal plain, capturing the Persian royal family.

<!-- field: significance -->
Alexander's first defeat of Darius in person, which opened the Levant and Egypt to his conquest.

<!-- field: body -->
Fought in November 333 BC on the narrow coastal plain at Issus, near modern Iskenderun in south-eastern Turkey, between Alexander the Great and the Persian king Darius III, the first time the two kings met in battle.

Darius made a clever move, marching his army round behind Alexander and cutting his supply line. But in doing so he brought his much larger army onto a narrow strip of land between the mountains and the sea, where it could not use its numbers.

Alexander led the Companion Cavalry across the Pinarus river in a charge aimed straight at Darius himself. Further along the line the Macedonian phalanx struggled to cross the riverbed against Darius' Greek mercenaries and suffered heavy losses. But when Darius turned and fled, the Persian army lost heart and collapsed.

The Persian camp was captured with Darius' mother, wife and daughters. Alexander kept their royal rank and treated them with marked respect. Darius later offered a great ransom, all lands west of the Euphrates and a daughter in marriage. Plutarch says Parmenion remarked that he would accept if he were Alexander, and Alexander replied that so would he, if he were Parmenion.

The victory opened the coast of Syria and Phoenicia, and then Egypt, to Alexander. The battle is shown in the famous Alexander Mosaic from Pompeii.
