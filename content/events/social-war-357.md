<!-- field: summary -->
The revolt of Athens' leading allies in 357–355 BC that broke up the Second Athenian League.

<!-- field: significance -->
A defeat that reduced Athens to a power with limited means just as Philip II was growing strong in the north.

<!-- field: body -->
The Second Athenian League was founded in 378/377 BC with promises not to repeat the abuses of the fifth-century empire. By the 350s, however, Athenian generals, short of cash, again exacted money from allies and stationed garrisons. Chios, Cos, Rhodes and Byzantium rebelled in 357 BC, encouraged by Mausolus of Caria, who wished to see them detached from Athens.

Athens sent a fleet under Chares and Chabrias, who was killed attacking Chios. In 356 BC, a naval battle at Embata ended without success after Chares ignored the advice of Iphicrates and Timotheus to avoid fighting in a storm; Chares blamed them and they were tried. Timotheus was fined heavily. The Persian king threatened Athens with his own fleet if the war continued.

Athens made peace in 355 BC and recognised the independence of the rebel states. The League kept few members and poor finances, which left Athens weak in 352 BC, when Philip II began his advance into central Greece, a fact Demosthenes' early speeches repeatedly stress.
