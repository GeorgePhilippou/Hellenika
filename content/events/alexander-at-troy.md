<!-- field: summary -->
Alexander opens his Persian campaign by crossing into Asia and performing rites at Troy, casting the invasion as a second Trojan War.

<!-- field: body -->
In spring 334 BC, Alexander ferried his invasion force across the Hellespont from Sestos to Abydos while the fleet made an unopposed crossing nearby. He is said to have thrown a spear into Asian soil from his ship and been first to land in full armour, staking a symbolic claim to the continent he had come to conquer.

He then turned aside to Troy itself, sacrificing to Athena at the citadel and laying a wreath at the tomb attributed to Achilles, the hero from whom the Argead royal house claimed descent. Ancient writers report he exchanged his own armour for relics displayed at the site as relics of the Trojan War, and ran a ceremonial race naked with his companions around the tomb in the old heroic manner.

The stop was not strategically necessary — no army opposed him here — but it did real propaganda work, framing the coming war against Persia as a second Trojan War and Alexander himself as a new Achilles, a self-comparison he is said to have cultivated for the rest of the campaign.
