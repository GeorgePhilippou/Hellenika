<!-- field: summary -->
The Ptolemies founded a Museum and Library that salaried scholars and attempted to collect every book in the world.

<!-- field: body -->
The Museum was a research institution: resident scholars received a salary, meals, lodging and exemption from taxes, and were expected only to work. It is the earliest state-funded research institute known.

Acquisition was aggressive. Ships entering the harbour were searched for books, which were copied — with the copies returned and the originals kept. Ptolemy III borrowed the official Athenian texts of Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides against an enormous deposit, had them copied, kept the originals and forfeited the money.

The scholarship was serious: Zenodotus and Aristarchus produced critical editions of Homer and invented the textual apparatus — obelus and asterisk — still used today. Callimachus catalogued the collection in 120 volumes, effectively founding library science.

The Library declined gradually through fire, funding cuts and the expulsion of scholars, rather than in one dramatic destruction.
