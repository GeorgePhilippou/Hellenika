<!-- field: summary -->
The treaty of winter 446/445 BC that ended the First Peloponnesian War and set the terms for coexistence between Athens and Sparta.

<!-- field: significance -->
The peace that held for fourteen years and whose violation Thucydides thought brought on the great war.

<!-- field: body -->
After the Athenian defeats in Boeotia and the rebellion of Euboea and Megara in 446 BC, Athens agreed to settle with Sparta. The treaty, whose terms are reported by Thucydides, was to last thirty years. Athens surrendered its bases in the Peloponnese, Nisaea, Pegae, Troezen and Achaea, which had given it a hold on the Isthmus.

The agreement recognised two blocs, the Athenian empire and the Peloponnesian League, with their respective allies. Each side agreed not to attack the other's members, and any state not in either bloc was free to join either side. If a dispute arose it was to be submitted to arbitration. Argos, a neutral state, was explicitly allowed to have friendly dealings with both.

The terms were tested in 433 BC, when Athens made a defensive alliance with Corcyra, then in conflict with Corinth, and again over Potidaea and Megara. Thucydides treats the Spartan decision in 432 BC that Athens had broken the peace as a cause of the war of 431 BC.
