<!-- field: summary -->
The decade-long war of 356–346 BC over control of Delphi, in which Philip II of Macedon gained a foothold in central Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
The conflict that brought Macedon into the affairs of Greece and into the Amphictyonic Council.

<!-- field: body -->
In 356 BC the Amphictyonic Council, dominated by Thebes, fined the Phocians for cultivating land sacred to Apollo. Under Philomelus, they seized Delphi in defiance and used the sacred treasures to hire a large mercenary army. Thebes and her allies the Locrians and Thessalians declared a sacred war, while Athens and Sparta sided with Phocis.

For several years the Phocians held the upper hand, led by Onomarchus after Philomelus' death. Philip II was asked by the Thessalians to intervene, and after an initial defeat he crushed Onomarchus at the Crocus Field in 352 BC, drowning many of the Phocian dead. The Phocian commander was hanged. Philip stopped at Thermopylae, held by Athens.

The war dragged on until 346 BC, when Phocis, short of money, surrendered to Philip, who passed through Thermopylae. The Phocian cities were destroyed, Phocis lost its place in the Amphictyony and the Macedonian king took it and presided at the Pythian Games. Athens, which had made the Peace of Philocrates that year, was now neighbour to Macedonian power.
