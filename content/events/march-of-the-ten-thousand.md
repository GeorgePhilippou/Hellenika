<!-- field: summary -->
The expedition of Greek mercenaries who fought for a Persian prince at Cunaxa in 401 BC and then fought their way home from Mesopotamia to the Black Sea.

<!-- field: significance -->
An epic of survival told by Xenophon in the Anabasis, which showed the Greeks that a Greek army could march through the heart of the Persian Empire.

<!-- field: body -->
In 401 BC Cyrus the Younger, brother of the Persian king Artaxerxes II, rebelled to seize the throne. He hired about 10,000 Greek mercenaries, many of them veterans of the Peloponnesian War, and marched from Sardis deep into Mesopotamia. At Cunaxa, near Babylon, the Greeks routed the royal troops facing them, but Cyrus charged at his brother and was killed, leaving them stranded without a cause.

The Persian satrap Tissaphernes invited the Greek generals to a parley and had them murdered. The army elected new leaders, among them the young Athenian Xenophon, a former pupil of Socrates. They decided to march north rather than back the way they had come, through the mountains of Kurdistan and Armenia in winter, fighting off the Carduchi and other peoples, suffering snow, hunger and frostbite. When the vanguard reached a mountain above the coast, Xenophon heard them shouting "Thalatta! Thalatta!" — "The sea! The sea!" They had reached the Black Sea near Trapezus (Trabzon). When the army was counted at Cerasus on the Black Sea coast, about 8,600 hoplites remained.

Xenophon told the story in his *Anabasis*, "The March Up-Country", one of the great adventure narratives of antiquity. The expedition showed that Greek hoplites could defeat Persian armies and cross the empire, an idea that encouraged later Greek plans to invade Persia — finally carried out by Alexander.
