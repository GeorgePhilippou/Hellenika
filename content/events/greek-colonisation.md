<!-- field: summary -->
Between roughly 750 and 550 BC Greeks founded hundreds of settlements from Spain to the Black Sea.

<!-- field: significance -->
The movement that spread Greek cities and culture from Spain to the Black Sea and created the wider Greek world.

<!-- field: body -->
Between about 750 and 550 BC Greek cities founded hundreds of new settlements around the Mediterranean and the Black Sea, from Spain to the Crimea. The main causes were shortage of land at home, political conflict that pushed losing factions abroad, and trade.

A colony (*apoikia*, "settlement away from home") was sent out by a mother city under a founder (*oikistes*), who was often honoured as a hero after his death. Founders usually consulted the oracle at Delphi first. The new city was independent from the start, keeping religious and sentimental ties to its mother city but not obeying it.

The Euboean cities of Chalcis and Eretria led the way, trading at Al Mina in Syria and settling Pithekoussai and then Cumae in Italy by the mid-8th century BC. Corinth founded Syracuse and Corcyra; Achaean cities founded Sybaris and Croton in southern Italy, which became known as Magna Graecia ("Great Greece"); Miletus founded dozens of settlements around the Black Sea, from Sinope to Olbia; Megara founded Byzantion; Thera founded Cyrene in Libya; and Phocaea founded Massalia (Marseille) in Gaul. In Egypt, Greeks traded at Naukratis under the pharaoh's control rather than founding an independent city.

The results shaped the whole ancient world. Greek cities came to ring the Mediterranean and Black Sea "like frogs around a pond", as Plato put it. The Greek alphabet passed to the Etruscans and from them to the Romans, and Greek wine, olive oil, pottery, coinage and religion spread among the peoples the colonists met.
