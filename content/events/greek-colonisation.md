<!-- field: summary -->
Between roughly 750 and 550 BC Greeks founded hundreds of settlements from Spain to the Black Sea.

<!-- field: body -->
Colonies were founded by a mother city, led by an appointed *oikistes*, usually after consulting Delphi — which accumulated remarkable geographic knowledge as a result. The new city was politically independent from the start, keeping cultic and sentimental ties but not obedience.

Drivers included land shortage, political conflict at home, and trade. Chalcis and Eretria settled Sicily and Italy; Miletus dominated the Black Sea; Phocaea reached Massalia and Spain; Corinth founded Syracuse and Corcyra.

The consequences were enormous: the Greek language and alphabet spread around the Mediterranean, the alphabet reached the Etruscans and through them the Romans, and the wine grape and olive were carried to France and Italy.
