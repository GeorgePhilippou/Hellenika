<!-- field: summary -->
A failed attempt by the Olympic victor Cylon to seize power in Athens around 632 BC, whose bloody end left a curse on the city.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the earliest datable events of Athenian history, and the origin of a hereditary curse that shaped Athenian politics for generations.

<!-- field: body -->
Cylon, an Athenian noble and winner at the Olympics, married the daughter of Theagenes, tyrant of Megara. Encouraged by an oracle from Delphi, which told him to take the Acropolis during a festival of Zeus, he seized the citadel with a small force and some Megarian troops. The Athenians gathered in the country to besiege it.

Cylon and his brother escaped, while the rest, starving, took refuge at the altar of Athena. The chief magistrate, Megacles of the Alcmaeonid family, persuaded them to leave under a promise of safety, but some of them were killed on the way, including some at the altar of the Eumenides. Thucydides says the guilty were thereafter regarded as cursed.

The curse was revived in political quarrels. The Alcmaeonids were tried and exiled about 600 BC, and the Cretan holy man Epimenides came to purify the city. Their descendants, including Cleisthenes and Pericles, were taunted with the old stain by their enemies, down to Sparta's demand in 432 BC.
