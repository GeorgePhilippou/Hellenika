<!-- field: summary -->
The war of 395–387 BC in which Thebes, Athens, Corinth and Argos, funded by Persia, fought to break Sparta's dominance of Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first great challenge to Spartan supremacy after the Peloponnesian War, which ended with Persia dictating peace to the Greeks.

<!-- field: body -->
After defeating Athens in 404 BC, Sparta dominated Greece and began to fight Persia in Asia Minor under King Agesilaus II. Persia responded by sending money to Sparta's discontented former allies. In 395 BC Thebes, Athens, Corinth and Argos formed an alliance against Sparta; much of the fighting took place around Corinth and its Isthmus, which gave the war its name.

In 394 BC Agesilaus was recalled from Asia. The Spartans won the land battles of Nemea and Coronea that year, but at sea the exiled Athenian admiral Conon, commanding a Persian fleet, destroyed the Spartan navy off Cnidus, ending Sparta's brief naval empire. With Persian money Conon rebuilt the Long Walls of Athens. In 390 BC the Athenian general Iphicrates, using light-armed troops (peltasts), destroyed a regiment of Spartan hoplites near Corinth, a famous demonstration of what light infantry could do. Corinth and Argos briefly merged into a single state.

The war dragged on until Persia, worried by the revival of Athens, switched its support back to Sparta. In 387 BC the Spartan envoy Antalcidas and the Persian king agreed terms that all the Greeks were forced to accept — the King's Peace — which left Sparta as the dominant power in Greece.
