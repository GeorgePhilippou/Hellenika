<!-- field: summary -->
Rome destroyed Corinth in 146 BC, killing or enslaving its population as a warning to the rest of Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
The Achaean League, provoked by Roman demands that it dismantle itself, went to war and lost quickly. Lucius Mummius took Corinth, killed the men, sold the women and children into slavery, looted the art and burned the city.

It was policy. Rome destroyed Carthage the same year. The message to the Greek world was that resistance would not be punished proportionally.

Polybius, a Greek who had spent seventeen years in Rome, arrived afterwards and had the unenviable task of helping organise the settlement of his own conquered homeland. He records Mummius' famous ignorance about the art he was shipping — warning the contractors that if they lost any of it they would have to replace it.

Corinth lay largely empty until Caesar refounded it as a Roman colony in 44 BC.
