<!-- field: summary -->
The largest hoplite battle ever fought ended the Persian invasion of Greece in 479 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The battle that ended the Persian invasion of Greece for good.

<!-- field: body -->
Fought in August 479 BC near Plataea in southern Boeotia, between the Greek allies under the Spartan regent Pausanias and the Persian army of Mardonius, which had stayed in Greece after Xerxes went home following Salamis.

About 40,000 Greek hoplites, with many lighter troops — the largest Greek army yet assembled — faced the Persians across the Asopus river. For days the two sides manoeuvred over water and supplies, while Persian cavalry harassed the Greeks. A confused Greek withdrawal by night left the allied army scattered, and Mardonius, thinking it was retreating, attacked.

The Spartans and the Tegeans beside them held firm under Persian arrows and then charged. In close fighting the lightly armoured Persian infantry could not stand against the hoplites. Mardonius was killed, and the Persian army broke and fled; its fortified camp was stormed. On the same day, tradition says, the Greek fleet destroyed the Persian navy's base at Mycale in Asia Minor.

The victory ended the Persian invasion of Greece. From the spoils, the Greeks dedicated at Delphi the Serpent Column, a bronze pillar inscribed with the names of the 31 allied states; its base survives in Istanbul, where Constantine moved it. Pausanias himself later fell under suspicion of treason with Persia and was starved to death in a temple in Sparta. The initiative passed to the Greeks, and soon after to Athens and its Delian League.
