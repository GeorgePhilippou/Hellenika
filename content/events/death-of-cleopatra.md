<!-- field: summary -->
Cleopatra VII died in August 30 BC; Egypt became a Roman province, ending the last Hellenistic kingdom.

<!-- field: significance -->
The end of the last Hellenistic kingdom and of three centuries of Greek rule in Egypt.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleopatra VII, last ruler of Ptolemaic Egypt, died at Alexandria in August 30 BC, a year after her and Mark Antony's defeat at Actium, as Octavian's army took the city.

Antony killed himself after a false report that Cleopatra was already dead. Cleopatra was captured alive and negotiated with Octavian's agents, but learned that he meant to parade her in his triumph in Rome. She died soon afterwards in her chambers, with her two attendants, Iras and Charmion.

Plutarch tells the famous story of an asp smuggled in a basket of figs, but admits that no one knew what really happened. Other accounts spoke of poison hidden in a hairpin; the only marks found were two faint punctures on her arm, and no snake was found in the room.

Octavian made Egypt a Roman province under a prefect of equestrian rank answerable to him, and barred senators from entering it without his permission. Egypt's grain was vital to feeding Rome, and he would not let any rival control it. Caesarion, Cleopatra's son by Julius Caesar, was hunted down and killed.

The Ptolemaic dynasty, founded by Alexander's general Ptolemy almost three centuries earlier, came to an end, and with it the last of the Hellenistic kingdoms. Historians often date the end of the Hellenistic age to this moment.
