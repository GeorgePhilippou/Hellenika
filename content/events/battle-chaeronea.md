<!-- field: summary -->
Philip II defeated the Athenian-Theban alliance in 338 BC, ending the independence of the Greek city-states.

<!-- field: significance -->
The battle that ended the independence of the Greek city-states and made Macedon master of Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
Fought in August 338 BC at Chaeronea in western Boeotia, between Philip II of Macedon and an alliance of Greek states led by Athens and Thebes, brought together by the Athenian orator Demosthenes.

Each side had around 30,000 men. Philip commanded the Macedonian right; his eighteen-year-old son Alexander led the Companion Cavalry on the left, facing the Thebans. Philip's wing made a controlled withdrawal, drawing the Athenians forward. As they pressed on, the allied line stretched and a gap opened between the Athenians and the Thebans. Alexander charged into it, Philip turned and attacked, and the Greek army collapsed.

The Theban Sacred Band held its ground and was destroyed almost to a man. A mass grave of 254 men found beneath the Lion of Chaeronea, the monument raised over the Theban dead, is almost certainly theirs. Demosthenes, fighting in the ranks, fled with the rest; about a thousand Athenians were killed and two thousand captured.

Philip treated Athens leniently, valuing its fleet and prestige, but dealt harshly with Thebes, placing a garrison in its citadel. In 337 BC he united the Greek states in the League of Corinth under his leadership and announced a war against Persia. Chaeronea ended the independence of the Greek city-states. Philip's murder in 336 BC left the war with Persia to Alexander.
