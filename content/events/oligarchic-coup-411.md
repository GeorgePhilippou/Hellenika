<!-- field: summary -->
The brief overthrow of Athenian democracy by the Four Hundred in 411 BC, which collapsed after four months.

<!-- field: significance -->
The first major challenge to Athenian democracy from within, and a rehearsal for the Thirty.

<!-- field: body -->
The defeat in Sicily left Athens bankrupt and divided. At Samos, the officers of the fleet, in contact with Alcibiades, who hoped to return to Athens in return for gaining Persian support, tried to change the constitution. The conspiracy was led in Athens by Peisander, Antiphon and Theramenes, who used terror and political clubs to push through a vote in the Assembly at Colonus to abolish pay and offices and to put power in the hands of Four Hundred.

The new government did not obtain support. The fleet at Samos, led by Thrasybulus, took an oath to defend democracy and recalled Alcibiades as its leader. Theramenes and others, fearing the extremists, turned against the Four Hundred, who were building a fort at Eetioneia to admit a Spartan force. When the Spartans defeated an Athenian fleet and Euboea rebelled, the Four Hundred were overthrown, and Antiphon was put on trial and executed.

In their place a more moderate regime of the Five Thousand governed for a time, and the democracy returned by 410 BC after the Athenians won at Cyzicus. Thucydides, whose history breaks off in mid-411 BC, thought this regime the best Athens had had in his lifetime.
