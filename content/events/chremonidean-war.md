<!-- field: summary -->
The war of about 267–261 BC in which Athens and Sparta, backed by Ptolemy II, failed to free Greece from the Macedonian king Antigonus Gonatas.

<!-- field: significance -->
The last attempt of the old city-states to expel Macedonian power by themselves, which ended in the subjection of Athens.

<!-- field: body -->
By the 260s BC, Antigonus II Gonatas had secured the Macedonian throne and held garrisons in Corinth, Piraeus and other key points in Greece. Ptolemy II of Egypt, who wanted to weaken Macedon, encouraged resistance. In 268/267 BC the Athenian Chremonides proposed a decree, which survives on stone, for an alliance of Athens, Sparta, Elis, Achaea and other states, and calling for the freedom of the Greeks, in the manner of their predecessors in the Persian Wars.

The campaign went badly. Spartan king Areus I tried to force a way to join the Athenians but was killed in front of Corinth in 265 BC. The Ptolemaic admiral Patroclus established a base in Attica, but did not engage the Macedonians in force, while Antigonus placed Athens under siege. The city, starved, gave in around 262/261 BC. Antigonus did not destroy the city, but he placed a garrison and Macedonian officials in it.

Sparta withdrew from active politics in the north and Ptolemaic influence in the Aegean declined. Athens remained under Macedonian control until 229 BC.
