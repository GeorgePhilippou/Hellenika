<!-- field: summary -->
Athens sent its largest ever expedition against Syracuse; it was annihilated, and the empire never recovered.

<!-- field: body -->
Alcibiades persuaded the assembly to attack Syracuse — a city as large as Athens, on the far side of the Mediterranean, while at war with Sparta. Nicias opposed it, then tried to deter the assembly by describing the enormous force required. They voted him the enormous force and put him in command of an operation he thought was a mistake.

Alcibiades was recalled for trial almost immediately and defected. The siege stalled; Sparta sent a commander who broke it. Reinforcements arrived and failed too.

When the fleet was destroyed in the Great Harbour, the survivors retreated overland and were cut down or captured. Around 40,000 men were lost. Prisoners were held in the stone quarries; Plutarch says some earned better treatment by reciting Euripides, whose poetry the Syracusans admired.
