<!-- field: summary -->
The battle of 222 BC in which Antigonus Doson and the Achaean League crushed Cleomenes III's reformed Spartan army.

<!-- field: significance -->
It ended Sparta's last bid for leadership in the Peloponnese and Sparta's reform movement, and let Macedon restore its power in southern Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
Cleomenes III had made himself master of Sparta in 227 BC by abolishing the ephors, cancelling debts and redistributing land, in order to expand the citizen body and raise a new army. He defeated the Achaean League, which was led by Aratus of Sicyon, and brought the League near collapse. Aratus then invited in Macedon's king Antigonus III Doson, whose aid cost the League its freedom to deal with Macedon's enemies.

In 222 BC Cleomenes took up a strong position in the hills of Sellasia, about 12 kilometres north of Sparta, on two ridges, Euas and Olympus. Antigonus attacked both. On the Euas ridge the Achaeans and Illyrians, helped by Philopoemen's cavalry charge, broke the Spartan allies under Cleomenes' brother Eucleidas. On the Olympus ridge the Macedonian phalanx fought the Spartan phalanx in a long, fierce struggle, which Polybius describes, before it gave way. Cleomenes escaped with a few horsemen to Gytheion and Egypt.

Antigonus entered Sparta, the first foreign conqueror in its history, but left it its customs and returned north soon after. Cleomenes' reforms were undone, and the victory restored Macedonian influence in the Peloponnese.
