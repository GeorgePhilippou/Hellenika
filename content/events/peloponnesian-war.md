<!-- field: summary -->
Twenty-seven years of war between Athens and Sparta that exhausted Greece and ended the Athenian empire.

<!-- field: significance -->
The war that ended Athens' empire and its Golden Age, and exhausted the Greek city-states.

<!-- field: body -->
The war fought from 431 to 404 BC between Athens and its Delian League empire on one side and Sparta and its Peloponnesian League allies on the other. Thucydides, who fought in it and wrote its history, saw the real cause in the growth of Athenian power and the fear it caused in Sparta.

The first phase, the Archidamian War (431–421 BC), set a land power against a sea power. Pericles brought the people of Attica inside the city walls and relied on the fleet and the Long Walls to the port of Piraeus, while Spartan armies ravaged the countryside. The overcrowded city was struck by plague in 430 BC, which killed perhaps a quarter of its people, Pericles among them. Athenian success at Sphacteria in 425 BC and Spartan success under Brasidas in the north led to the Peace of Nicias in 421 BC.

The peace never really held. Sparta won a major land battle at Mantinea in 418 BC, and in 416 BC Athens conquered the neutral island of Melos, killing its men and enslaving its women and children. Then in 415–413 BC Athens sent a huge expedition against Syracuse in Sicily, which ended with its entire army and fleet destroyed.

In the final phase (413–404 BC) Sparta fortified a base in Attica and, with Persian money, built a fleet. Athens fought on and won at Arginusae in 406 BC, but in 405 BC the Spartan admiral Lysander destroyed its last fleet at Aegospotami, cutting off its grain supply from the Black Sea. Athens surrendered in 404 BC; its empire was dissolved and its Long Walls pulled down, and the oligarchy of the Thirty briefly took power.

Both sides came out exhausted. The real winner was Persia, which by the King's Peace of 387 BC regained control of the Greek cities of Asia Minor.
