<!-- field: summary -->
Twenty-seven years of war between Athens and Sparta that exhausted Greece and ended the Athenian empire.

<!-- field: body -->
Thucydides identified the underlying cause plainly: the growth of Athenian power and the fear it caused in Sparta.

The first decade set a land power against a sea power with no way for either to reach the other decisively. Pericles brought Attica's population inside the walls and relied on the fleet — sound strategy ruined when plague broke out in the crowded city in 430 BC and killed perhaps a quarter of the population, including Pericles.

The Sicilian Expedition of 415–413 BC destroyed an entire Athenian army and fleet. Sparta then took Persian money, built a fleet, and under Lysander destroyed the Athenian navy at Aegospotami in 405. Athens, cut off from Black Sea grain, surrendered in 404.

Both sides were exhausted, and Persia — which had funded the winner — recovered the Greek cities of Asia within a decade.
