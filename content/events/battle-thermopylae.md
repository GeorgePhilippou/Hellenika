<!-- field: summary -->
A Greek force under Leonidas held the pass for three days against Xerxes' army before being outflanked and destroyed.

<!-- field: body -->
Around 7,000 Greeks blocked a pass a few metres wide, where Persian numbers counted for nothing. For two days assaults failed, including attacks by the elite Immortals.

A local man, Ephialtes, showed the Persians the Anopaia path over the mountain. Learning he was surrounded, Leonidas dismissed most of the army and stayed with 300 Spartans, 700 Thespians and 400 Thebans. The Thespians' choice to stay is often forgotten and was entirely voluntary.

They were pushed back to a hillock and killed by missiles — confirmed by the Persian arrowheads Marinatos excavated there. The delay let the Greek fleet withdraw in order and gave the southern states time to prepare.
