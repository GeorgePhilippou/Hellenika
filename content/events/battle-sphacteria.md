<!-- field: summary -->
An Athenian force trapped a Spartan garrison on Sphacteria in 425 BC and forced roughly 120 Spartan citizens to surrender — an unprecedented shock to Sparta's military prestige.

<!-- field: body -->
A chance Athenian landing at Pylos, on the Spartan mainland's own coast, left a Spartan force stranded on the offshore island of Sphacteria when the Athenian fleet cut off their escape. The siege dragged on until Cleon, an Athenian politician with no military reputation who had rashly promised the assembly a quick result, was sent out with the general Demosthenes and, against most expectations, actually delivered one: the Spartan position was stormed and the survivors surrendered.

The surrender itself was the real shock. Spartan citizen-soldiers were expected to die rather than yield, and the roughly 120 Spartiates taken prisoner represented a significant fraction of Sparta's entire full-citizen fighting body — Sparta's citizen population was already in long-term decline, and it could not afford this loss lightly. Held hostage at Athens, the prisoners gave Athens real leverage: Sparta avoided invading Attica for years afterward for fear of what might happen to them.

Thucydides frames the episode partly as an argument about Athenian democratic politics — Cleon's success was resented as much as celebrated, since it vindicated a demagogue's gamble over the caution of more established leaders.
