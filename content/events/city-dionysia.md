<!-- field: summary -->
The Athenian spring festival of Dionysus at which the great tragedies and comedies were performed in competition.

<!-- field: significance -->
The occasion for which Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides and Aristophanes wrote their plays.

<!-- field: body -->
The City Dionysia was celebrated in the month of Elaphebolion, around March, when ships could sail again and visitors from the allied cities came to Athens to pay tribute. It began when the image of Dionysus was brought from a shrine on the road to Eleutherae to a temple near his sanctuary on the south slope of the Acropolis, and was followed by a large procession and sacrifice of bulls.

The competition lasted several days. Dithyrambs by choruses of men and boys opened it, and a day of comedies followed. Three tragedians then each offered a set of three tragedies and a satyr play on successive days. Ten judges chosen by lot decided the prizes, and wealthy citizens (*choregoi*) paid for the choruses. Aeschylus won the contest many times, Sophocles at least eighteen, and Euripides far fewer.

Tradition says the contest was organised in the time of Peisistratus, with the first winner Thespis, and that comedy was added in 486 BC. Inscribed lists of victors, set up in the fourth century BC, record winning poets and plays.
