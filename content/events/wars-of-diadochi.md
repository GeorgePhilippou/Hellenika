<!-- field: summary -->
Forty years of war among Alexander's generals that carved the empire into the Hellenistic kingdoms.

<!-- field: body -->
The generals first claimed to govern for Alexander's heirs, then stopped pretending. From 306 BC they took royal titles outright.

The wars were fought on an enormous scale — Ipsus in 301 BC involved perhaps 150,000 men and 475 elephants — and were settled by the near-total elimination of Alexander's family. Roxana and her son Alexander IV, Arrhidaeus, Olympias, and Alexander's sister Cleopatra were all killed.

By 281 BC the map was set: Ptolemies in Egypt, Seleucids across Asia, Antigonids in Macedon, with Pergamon and Bactria emerging later. This division held, roughly, until Rome dismantled it.
