<!-- field: summary -->
The intermittent conflict of about 460–446 BC between Athens and a coalition led by Sparta and Corinth, which ended in the Thirty Years' Peace.

<!-- field: significance -->
A prelude to the great Peloponnesian War, which established the rival blocs and the tensions that later exploded.

<!-- field: body -->
The origin of the war lay in Athens' expansion. After Megara left the Spartan alliance for Athens around 460 BC and Athens built the Long Walls to link the city to the Piraeus, Corinth and Aegina were alarmed. At the same time a large Athenian force was occupied in Egypt, supporting the revolt of Inaros against Persia, and was lost around 454 BC.

Fighting was inconclusive. Athens conquered Aegina in 457 BC and defeated the Boeotians at Oenophyta, taking control of most of Boeotia, after Sparta won at Tanagra. In 447 BC the Athenians were defeated at Coronea and abandoned Boeotia. Then in 446 BC Euboea revolted from Athens and Megara massacred its Athenian garrison. A Spartan army entered Attica under King Pleistoanax but withdrew without battle, probably after bribery, according to later tradition, and Pericles reconquered Euboea.

The Thirty Years' Peace made the position stable, with Athens abandoning its holdings in the Peloponnese. It lasted about fourteen years.
