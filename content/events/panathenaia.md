<!-- field: summary -->
Athens' great annual festival of Athena, with a procession to the Acropolis, sacrifices and every four years athletic and musical games.

<!-- field: significance -->
The festival that defined the civic identity of Athens, and the subject of the Parthenon frieze.

<!-- field: body -->
The Panathenaia was held each summer on Athena's birthday, with a procession from the Kerameikos through the Agora to the Acropolis. Citizens, resident foreigners, young women with offerings, cattle for sacrifice and cavalry took part. The high point was the presentation of a new woven robe, the *peplos*, for the old wooden statue of Athena. Meat from the sacrificed cattle was distributed to the people.

In 566/565 BC the festival was enlarged with competitions in the manner of the Olympic Games, held every four years as the Great Panathenaia. There were races, wrestling and boxing, contests in music, horse and chariot racing, and a boat race. Victors received amphorae of olive oil from the sacred trees, decorated with a figure of Athena on one side and the event on the other; these vases provide a long record of Athenian vase painting.

The Parthenon frieze is generally taken to show the procession. Later tradition said Peisistratus or his son Hipparchus ordered rhapsodes to recite Homer in order at the festival, and recitals of Homer were a regular feature in the classical period.
