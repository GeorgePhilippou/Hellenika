<!-- field: summary -->
The naval battle off Miletus in 494 BC where the Persian fleet crushed the Ionian Greeks, ending the Ionian Revolt.

<!-- field: significance -->
The defeat that ended the Ionian Revolt and doomed Miletus, setting the stage for Persia's invasion of mainland Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
Fought in 494 BC off the island of Lade, then a small island just offshore from Miletus in Ionia, between the fleet of the Ionian Greek cities in revolt against Persia and the Persian navy, drawn mainly from Phoenicia, Egypt, Cilicia and Cyprus.

By 494 BC the Ionian Revolt had lasted five years, and Persia was closing in on Miletus, where it had begun. The rebels decided to fight at sea. According to Herodotus they assembled 353 triremes, from Chios, Miletus, Samos, Lesbos and other cities, against some 600 Persian ships. Dionysius of Phocaea drilled the fleet hard, but after a week the crews refused to train any longer.

Meanwhile the exiled tyrants of the Ionian cities, now with the Persians, sent messages to their own peoples promising mercy if they deserted. When the battle began, most of the Samian ships sailed away, followed by the Lesbians and others. The Chians fought bravely and lost heavily, but the Ionian fleet was destroyed.

Persia then besieged and captured Miletus, killing many of its men and deporting women and children to the Persian Gulf. The revolt collapsed. Within four years Darius sent his expedition against Athens and Eretria, which had aided the rebels — the campaign that ended at Marathon.
