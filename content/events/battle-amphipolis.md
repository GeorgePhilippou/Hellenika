<!-- field: summary -->
The battle of 422 BC outside Amphipolis in which Brasidas defeated Athens' army and both he and Cleon were killed.

<!-- field: significance -->
The last major action of the first phase of the Peloponnesian War, which cleared the way for the Peace of Nicias.

<!-- field: body -->
In summer 422 BC, Cleon, the leading Athenian demagogue, led thirty ships and 1,200 hoplites to northern Greece to recover Amphipolis, which Brasidas had captured two years before. Cleon camped at Eion and reconnoitred the city, which he found less well guarded than he expected, and began to withdraw. This movement exposed his flank.

Brasidas, who was watching from the walls with about 2,000 hoplites and with Edonian cavalry, saw the opportunity and attacked from the gates. His own men struck the Athenian centre and Clearidas, another Spartan, struck the right. The Athenian left fled at once, and Cleon was killed by a javelin as he ran. Brasidas was fatally wounded but lived long enough to be told of the victory.

The Amphipolitans buried Brasidas in the city and honoured him as a founder. Thucydides reports that the two men had been the strongest opponents of peace, and the way was now clear for Nicias and the Spartan king Pleistoanax to agree a peace in 421 BC.
