<!-- field: summary -->
The age of the first farmers in Greece, from about 7000 BC, when villages of mudbrick houses spread across Thessaly, the Peloponnese and Crete.

<!-- field: significance -->
The beginning of settled life in Europe: farming, villages, pottery and the first sea-borne trade, the foundations on which the Bronze Age was built.

<!-- field: overview -->
Around 7000 BC farming arrived in Greece, the first part of Europe to adopt it. Wheat, barley, lentils, sheep and goats were all domesticated in the Near East, and ancient DNA shows that the first farmers were largely descended from migrants from Anatolia, who mixed with the local hunter-gatherers known from sites such as Franchthi Cave. The richest farming plains, in Thessaly and Macedonia, filled with villages of mudbrick houses — Sesklo, Dimini and Nea Nikomedeia among them — whose rebuilding over centuries created mounds (*magoules*). Knossos on Crete began as a Neolithic village around 7000 BC.

Over the next four thousand years villages grew larger and pottery more elaborate. In the Late and Final Neolithic, after about 4500 BC, copper and gold began to be worked, settlement spread to the Cyclades and to caves and uplands, and long-distance exchange of obsidian, marble and shell increased. These changes led into the Early Bronze Age around 3200 BC.

<!-- field: politics -->
No states or rulers. Villages of a few hundred people seem to have been organised by households and kin groups; larger central houses at Sesklo and Dimini may reflect village leaders, but there is no clear evidence of chiefs.

<!-- field: warfare -->
Evidence for warfare is slight. Ditches and walls surround some villages, but the concentric walls of Dimini are now usually read as enclosures rather than fortifications.

<!-- field: culture -->
Painted pottery — red-on-white in the Sesklo culture, dark spirals and meanders in the Dimini culture — and clay and stone figurines, many of them female. Their meaning is unknown; the idea that they show a Great Mother goddess is a modern speculation.

<!-- field: boundaryNote -->
Farming appears around 7000 BC; a few sites, such as Franchthi Cave, record earlier hunter-gatherers. The 3200 BC end is the conventional start of the Early Bronze Age and varies by region.
