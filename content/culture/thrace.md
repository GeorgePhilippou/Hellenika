<!-- field: summary -->
The region north-east of Greece, home of the Thracian tribes and their Odrysian kingdom, rich in gold, horses and mercenaries.

<!-- field: significance -->
A neighbouring culture which supplied Greece with soldiers, slaves and metal, and gave Greek myth the figures of Orpheus and Dionysus.

<!-- field: body -->
Thrace extends from the river Strymon to the Black Sea and the Danube, a broad area of plains and hills occupied by many peoples without a single government. Greek colonists settled its coasts from the seventh century BC, among them Thasos and Byzantion, and drew on its timber, gold and silver.

In the fifth century BC the Odrysian kingdom, under Teres and Sitalces, united most of the tribes and grew rich on tribute and Greek trade. Sitalces allied with Athens and invaded Macedon in 429 BC with a large army. Thracian horsemen and peltasts, who carried light shields and javelins, were employed by many Greek states.

Philip II brought much of the Thracian coast under Macedonian rule in the 340s BC, and after Alexander's death Lysimachus held it. The region was later absorbed by Rome as a province in AD 46. Its tombs and treasures, such as the Panagyurishte hoard and the painted tomb at Kazanlak, show a distinctive aristocratic culture that adopted Greek styles.
