<!-- field: summary -->
The south-eastern part of the Peloponnese, the territory of Sparta and its dependent perioikic towns, and home of the helots.

<!-- field: significance -->
The land that supported the Spartan system, and the base of one of the most distinctive societies in Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
Laconia is the Eurotas valley and its surroundings in the south-eastern Peloponnese, bounded by the mountains of Taygetus and Parnon. In the Bronze Age it contained a Mycenaean palace society linked to the legend of Menelaus and Helen, and the Menelaion near Sparta is thought to be a cult site for the pair. Rich gold cups were found in a tomb at Vapheio.

Sparta, founded by Dorians, conquered the region and took Messenia in the 8th–7th centuries BC. Laconia's population was divided into Spartiates, the full citizens, perioikoi, free men in about eighty communities who handled trade and crafts and fought in the army, and helots, state serfs who farmed the land. The Spartan system depended on this labour.

In 370/369 BC Epaminondas led a Theban-Arcadian army down the valley, and the liberation of Messenia soon deprived Sparta of half its estates. Laconia kept its identity under Roman rule, when the perioikic towns were organised as the League of Free Laconians.
