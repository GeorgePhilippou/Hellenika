<!-- field: summary -->
The broad farming plain of northern Greece, known for early farming villages, its horsemen and its great landowning families.

<!-- field: significance -->
The earliest farming region of Greece, and later a key to Macedonian control of the south.

<!-- field: body -->
Thessaly is a large basin of central and northern Greece, ringed by Olympus, Ossa, Pelion and Pindus. Its rich plain was the first part of Greece where farming villages took root, as at Sesklo and Dimini, from about 7000 BC.

In the archaic and classical periods wealthy families, among them the Aleuadae of Larissa and the Scopadae of Crannon, ruled the plain through serfs called penestai and raised the best cavalry in Greece. The cities occasionally came together under a single commander, the tagos. Jason of Pherae did so in the 370s BC and nearly established a Thessalian empire. The Aleuadae sided with Xerxes in 480 BC.

Philip II won the Thessalian leadership in 352 BC, and Macedon held the region for most of the next century and a half. After the Romans defeated Philip V at Cynoscephalae in 197 BC, Thessaly was declared free, and in 48 BC Caesar defeated Pompey at Pharsalus on its plain.
