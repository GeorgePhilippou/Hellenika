<!-- field: summary -->
The largest Greek island, home of Europe's first palace civilisation and a crossroads between the Aegean, Egypt and the Levant.

<!-- field: body -->
Crete is 260 km long, mountainous, and positioned where the sea routes from Egypt, the Levant and the Aegean converge. That position explains most of its history.

It was settled by around 7000 BC by farmers who brought livestock and crops by boat — one of the earliest maritime colonisations known. The palaces emerged around 1900 BC and dominated the Aegean until the destructions of 1450 BC.

After the Bronze Age it became comparatively marginal: a land of many small poleis, known for archers, mercenaries and, in the standard Greek stereotype, lying. The "Cretan paradox" — a Cretan saying all Cretans are liars — enters logic and eventually the New Testament.

Rome took the island in 69–67 BC.
