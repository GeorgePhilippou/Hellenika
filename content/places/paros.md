<!-- field: summary -->
A Cycladic island famous for its pure white marble, the birthplace of the poet Archilochus and the sculptor Scopas.

<!-- field: significance -->
The source of the finest sculptural marble of the ancient world.

<!-- field: body -->
Paros, in the central Cyclades, was among the wealthiest islands of the archaic Aegean. Its marble, quarried from underground galleries and prized for its translucency, was used by sculptors across Greece, and the Nike of Samothrace is carved from it.

The poet Archilochus, who wrote of soldiering, love and satire, was born there about 680 BC and took part in the colonisation of Thasos. After Marathon, the Athenian general Miltiades laid siege to Paros for twenty-six days, because the island had sided with the Persians, and withdrew wounded and unsuccessful.

The Parian Chronicle, carved about 264/263 BC, gives a chronological list of events from the age of Cecrops to its own day. Part of it was acquired in 1627 for the Earl of Arundel, and fragments are now in Oxford and Paros.
