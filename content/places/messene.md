<!-- field: summary -->
The capital founded in 369 BC for the freed Messenians, protected by one of the finest circuits of fortifications in the Greek world.

<!-- field: significance -->
A symbol of the end of Spartan hegemony, and now one of the best-preserved Hellenistic cities in Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
After the Theban victory at Leuctra in 371 BC, Epaminondas marched into the Peloponnese and invaded Laconia. In 369 BC he helped the Messenians, who had been held as helots by Sparta for generations, to found a city of their own at the foot of Mount Ithome. Its construction was celebrated with sacrifices and flute music, and it deprived Sparta of the land and labour on which its society depended.

The city walls ran for nine kilometres and had towers and gates, among them the Arcadian Gate, which still stands. Pausanias describes a city with a great marketplace, a sanctuary of Asclepius, a theatre and a stadium surrounded by statues, much of which excavation has since confirmed.

Messene was independent until the Achaean League took it in the second century BC, and flourished under Roman rule. Excavations since 1986 have uncovered the Asclepieion and a stadium with a colonnade that is among the best preserved in Greece.
