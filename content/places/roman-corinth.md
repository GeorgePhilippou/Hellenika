<!-- field: summary -->
The Roman colony founded by Julius Caesar on the ruins of Corinth in 44 BC, which became the capital of Roman Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
The richest city of Roman Greece, where Paul founded one of the most important early Christian communities.

<!-- field: body -->
Rome destroyed Greek Corinth in 146 BC. A century later, in 44 BC, Julius Caesar refounded it as a Roman colony, *Colonia Laus Iulia Corinthiensis*, settled largely with freed slaves from Italy. With its two harbours on the Saronic and Corinthian gulfs and its control of the Isthmus, it quickly grew rich again, and under Augustus it became the seat of the governor of the province of Achaea.

The city was rebuilt on a Roman plan, with Latin as its official language for over a century. Most of the ruins visitors see today are Roman: the great forum, with its speaker's platform (*bema*), basilicas, shops and temples, the Lechaion Road, and the Peirene fountain, rebuilt in marble with help from Herodes Atticus. Only the archaic temple of Apollo survived from the Greek city. The Isthmian Games were brought back under Corinthian control.

Paul arrived around AD 50 and stayed eighteen months, working as a tentmaker; he was accused before the governor Gallio, perhaps at the *bema*. His letters to the Corinthians, written in the 50s, give a vivid picture of the young church there. The city was damaged by earthquakes in the 4th century and sacked by the Goths under Alaric in 396.
