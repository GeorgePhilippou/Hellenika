<!-- field: summary -->
A northern Aegean island colonised from Paros in the seventh century BC, rich in gold, wine and marble, whose revolt from Athens in 465 BC lasted two years.

<!-- field: significance -->
A wealthy colony whose resources and defiance of Athens made it a test case for the Delian League.

<!-- field: body -->
Thasos lies off the Thracian coast, near the mouth of the Nestos. Settlers from Paros, including the poet Archilochus, colonised it in the 680s–650s BC to exploit the gold of the island and of the mainland opposite, where the Thasians also founded towns. The island was also known for its wine and marble.

When Thasos joined the Delian League, a quarrel with Athens over the Thracian mines led it to leave in 465 BC. Athens sent Cimon with a fleet and besieged the city for about two years. Thasos had to demolish its walls, surrender its fleet and mainland holdings, and pay tribute. Sparta had promised to invade Attica to aid it, but an earthquake and helot revolt prevented this.

The island's markets, harbour and walls survive, and its gates carry reliefs of divine figures. The painter Polygnotus came from Thasos. The French School at Athens has dug there since 1911.
