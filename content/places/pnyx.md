<!-- field: summary -->
The hill west of the Acropolis where the Athenian Assembly met to vote on war, peace, laws and ostracisms.

<!-- field: significance -->
The physical home of Athenian direct democracy, where citizens debated and decided state policy.

<!-- field: body -->
The Pnyx is a rocky hill west of the Acropolis, whose slope was shaped into an assembly ground for the Athenian Ekklesia. Citizens sat on the ground or on benches and faced a stepped speakers' platform, the bema. The Assembly met around forty times a year to decide on war, treaties, finance and legislation, and any citizen could speak.

Excavations show three main phases. The earliest is generally linked with the democratic reforms of Cleisthenes, though some scholars place it later; the second dates around 400 BC; and the last, in the later fourth century BC, enlarged the space with a great retaining wall. The capacity of the assembly place was several thousand, but the quorum for some votes was 6,000, and the number of citizens actually present was often less.

In the late fourth century BC the Assembly began to meet more often in the Theatre of Dionysus. Speeches by Pericles, Cleon and Demosthenes are associated with the Pnyx in the literary record, and the site is now an open archaeological park.
