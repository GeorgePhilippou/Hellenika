<!-- field: summary -->
"Rich in gold" — the citadel that gave its name to an entire civilisation, with its Lion Gate, shaft graves and tholos tombs.

<!-- field: significance -->
The dominant Mycenaean centre and, in Greek tradition, the seat of Agamemnon and the launching point of the Trojan War.

<!-- field: body -->
Mycenae occupies a defensible hill between two mountains commanding the Argive plain. Its cyclopean walls, built of blocks so large that later Greeks credited them to the Cyclopes, enclose a citadel entered through the Lion Gate — a relieving triangle carved with two lionesses flanking a column, the oldest monumental sculpture in Europe.

Schliemann excavated Grave Circle A inside the walls in 1876, finding six shaft graves with nineteen bodies and roughly 15 kg of gold: masks, breastplates, inlaid daggers, and amber that had travelled from the Baltic.

Outside the walls stand nine tholos tombs, of which the "Treasury of Atreus" has a corbelled dome 13.5 m across — the largest unsupported span in the world for over a thousand years. Late in the period the Mycenaeans cut a concealed stairway down to an underground cistern fed by a spring outside the walls, which strongly implies they expected to be besieged.
