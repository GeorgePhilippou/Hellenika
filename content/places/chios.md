<!-- field: summary -->
A large Ionian island off the coast of Asia Minor, famous for wine, mastic, a guild claiming descent from Homer, and the 1822 massacre.

<!-- field: significance -->
A leading Aegean state which remained loyal to Athens until 412 BC, and which suffered one of the worst atrocities of the Greek War of Independence.

<!-- field: body -->
Chios lies in the eastern Aegean, a few kilometres from the coast of Asia Minor. It was among the twelve Ionian cities, and an early centre of commerce, producing wine that was widely exported and the aromatic resin called mastic. Chios was also noted for its ships and for being one of the first Greek states to buy slaves from non-Greek lands.

The island was home to the Homeridae, whose members claimed descent from Homer and recited his poems. The poet and tragedian Ion and the historian Theopompus were born there. Chios supplied ships to Athens as an autonomous member of the Delian League, and in 412 BC rebelled against it with Spartan help during the Peloponnesian War. The island kept an independent identity under Roman and Byzantine rule, and under the Genoese from 1346 to 1566.

In April 1822 Ottoman forces landed after a local rising and killed or enslaved tens of thousands of inhabitants. The event moved western opinion and inspired a painting by Delacroix.
