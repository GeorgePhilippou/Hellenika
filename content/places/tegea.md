<!-- field: summary -->
The chief city of eastern Arcadia and a long-time Spartan ally, famous for its temple of Athena Alea designed by Scopas.

<!-- field: significance -->
A major sanctuary and an example of how Sparta turned former rivals into allies.

<!-- field: body -->
Tegea lay on the plain of Arcadia on the route between Sparta and Argos. For long it was Sparta's chief rival in the northern Peloponnese, and Herodotus describes battles in which the Spartans were beaten and Spartan prisoners were set to work in the Tegean fields in chains. Around 550 BC Sparta, advised by Delphi, recovered what it believed were the bones of Orestes from Tegean ground, and Tegea afterwards became a loyal ally.

The Tegean contingent fought at Plataea. After Leuctra, Tegea joined the Arcadian League and was one of the cities that sided against Sparta, but its position near the Laconian border kept its fortunes tied to Spartan power.

The temple of Athena Alea was rebuilt in the fourth century BC by the sculptor Scopas of Paros, after a fire of 395 BC. Pausanias admired its size and decoration, which included the tusks of the Calydonian boar. French excavations in the late nineteenth century and Norwegian ones in the 1990s have recovered its plan.
