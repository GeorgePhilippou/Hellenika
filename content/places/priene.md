<!-- field: summary -->
A small Ionian city rebuilt in the fourth century BC on a regular grid below a steep rock, and the best-preserved example of a planned Hellenistic town.

<!-- field: significance -->
A well-preserved Greek town showing how a planned city worked, with houses, assembly hall, theatre and gymnasium all surviving.

<!-- field: body -->
Priene was an Ionian city of the Panionian league. Its harbour on the Meander silted up, and in the mid-fourth century BC the city moved a few kilometres to a steep terraced site. The new town was laid out on a grid, with streets running east–west and north–south, and its public buildings, among them the agora, the council house, the theatre and the gymnasium, are unusually well preserved.

The temple of Athena Polias was designed by Pytheos, who wrote a book about it and also worked on the Mausoleum. An inscription found on the temple records Alexander's dedication in 334 BC, when he passed through Ionia. Houses of the period, mostly of a standard plan around a courtyard, survive in numbers, and show the standard of living of Hellenistic citizens.

The river continued to silt up, and Priene gradually declined, though it survived under Roman and Byzantine rule. German excavators started work in 1895.
