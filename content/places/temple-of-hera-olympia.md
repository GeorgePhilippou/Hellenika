<!-- field: summary -->
A Doric temple of about 600 BC at Olympia, dedicated to Hera, older than the great temple of Zeus and one of the oldest large stone temples in Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
A key monument in the early history of Doric architecture, and the place where the modern Olympic flame is lit.

<!-- field: body -->
The temple of Hera at Olympia was built around 600 BC and is one of the earliest large Doric temples in Greece. It is a long building of six by sixteen columns. Pausanias reports that one of the columns in its rear porch was still made of oak when he visited, which suggests that stone columns replaced wooden ones one at a time, and the surviving shafts do vary in proportion.

The temple held a gold and ivory image of Zeus and Hera and, in Pausanias' day, the Hermes of Praxiteles. Beside it stood the circular Philippeion and the altar on which the Olympic flame was kept. Women also ran a footrace here, the Heraia, in honour of Hera.

German excavations at Olympia from 1875 uncovered the temple, and the Hermes was found in its ruins in 1877. Since 1936 the temple has been the setting of the ceremony in which the torch for each modern Games is lit.
