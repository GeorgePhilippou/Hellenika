<!-- field: summary -->
A militarised society of full-time warrior-citizens sustained by an enslaved Messenian population many times their number.

<!-- field: significance -->
The dominant land power of Greece for two centuries, and the most systematically misunderstood society in the ancient world.

<!-- field: body -->
Sparta had no walls; Spartans said their men were the walls. Its constitution was mixed: two hereditary kings, a council of 28 elders, an assembly, and five annually elected ephors who could prosecute even kings.

Male citizens entered the *agoge* at seven and lived in barracks until thirty, contributing to communal messes for life. They did no agricultural work — that was done by *helots*, enslaved Messenians who outnumbered Spartiates by perhaps seven to one. Sparta annually declared war on the helots so killing them carried no ritual pollution, and its foreign policy was constrained throughout by fear of revolt at home.

Spartan women owned property, exercised publicly, and by Aristotle's hostile estimate eventually held two-fifths of Spartan land — a level of autonomy unmatched elsewhere in Greece.

The system was demographically fragile. Spartiate numbers fell from perhaps 8,000 to a few hundred over two centuries; after Leuctra in 371 BC and the liberation of Messenia, Sparta never recovered. Much of what we "know" comes from admirers writing after its decline — the *Spartan mirage*.
