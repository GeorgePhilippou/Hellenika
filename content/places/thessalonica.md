<!-- field: summary -->
The Macedonian port founded by Cassander around 315 BC, which became the second city of the Byzantine Empire.

<!-- field: significance -->
For over a thousand years the great city of the Greek north, whose churches are among the finest monuments of early and middle Byzantine art.

<!-- field: body -->
Thessalonica was founded around 315 BC by Cassander, king of Macedon, who merged some two dozen villages at the head of the Thermaic Gulf and named the city after his wife Thessalonike, half-sister of Alexander the Great. Under Rome it was the capital of the province of Macedonia and a station on the Via Egnatia, the road from the Adriatic to Byzantium. Paul founded one of the first Christian churches in Europe there around AD 50 and wrote it the earliest of his letters.

Around AD 300 the emperor Galerius made it his residence, building a palace, a triumphal arch and a great round building, the Rotunda, later a church. In 390 Theodosius I's soldiers massacred thousands of citizens in the hippodrome.

In the Byzantine period Thessalonica was the empire's second city and the centre of the cult of Saint Demetrios, a soldier-martyr believed to protect it; his great basilica still stands. The brothers Cyril and Methodius, apostles to the Slavs, came from the city. It was sacked by Arab raiders in 904 and by the Normans of Sicily in 1185, ruled by a Latin king after 1204, and governed by a radical "Zealot" regime in 1342–50. Ceded to Venice in 1423, it fell to the Ottoman sultan Murad II in 1430.
