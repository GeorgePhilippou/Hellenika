<!-- field: summary -->
Sanctuary of Zeus and home of the Olympic Games, held every four years from 776 BC for over a millennium.

<!-- field: significance -->
The Games created a shared Greek calendar and a Panhellenic identity that transcended constant warfare between the cities.

<!-- field: body -->
The traditional first Olympiad of 776 BC became the basis of Greek chronology — historians dated events by Olympiad number. A sacred truce allowed competitors and spectators safe passage through war zones.

The Temple of Zeus held Pheidias' chryselephantine statue, twelve metres high, one of the Seven Wonders. Pheidias' workshop has been excavated beside it, identified by tools, ivory offcuts and a cup inscribed with his name.

Events included the *stadion* sprint, wrestling, boxing, the brutal *pankration*, chariot racing and the pentathlon. Competitors were male, Greek and — from the seventh century — naked. Married women were barred from attending, though women could win the chariot race as owners: the Spartan princess Cynisca did so twice.

Theodosius I ended the Games around AD 393; earthquakes and river flooding buried the site, which is why so much survived.
