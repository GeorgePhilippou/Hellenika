<!-- field: summary -->
Alexander's greatest foundation: capital of Ptolemaic Egypt, home of the Library, the Museum and the Pharos lighthouse.

<!-- field: significance -->
The intellectual capital of the ancient Mediterranean for three centuries, where most Greek science was done.

<!-- field: body -->
Alexander chose the site in 331 BC; Ptolemy I and his son built it. Its grid plan, laid out by Dinocrates, was oriented to catch the sea breeze.

The Museum — a "shrine of the Muses" — salaried resident scholars who ate together and were free to research. Euclid, Eratosthenes, Aristarchus, Herophilus and Apollonius all worked here. The Library aimed to collect everything; estimates of its holdings range from 40,000 to 700,000 scrolls, and all are guesses.

The Pharos lighthouse, over 100 m tall, was one of the Seven Wonders and stood until earthquakes brought it down in the fourteenth century; blocks from it have been recovered from the harbour floor since 1994.

The Library was not destroyed in a single event. It declined through Caesar's harbour fire in 48 BC, the loss of Ptolemaic patronage, and the expulsion of scholars — attrition rather than catastrophe.
