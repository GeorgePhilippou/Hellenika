<!-- field: summary -->
Mycenaean palace, mythological centre of the Oedipus and Dionysus legends, and briefly the dominant power in Greece after Leuctra.

<!-- field: significance -->
The only city to break Spartan military supremacy — and the city Alexander destroyed as a warning to the rest of Greece.

<!-- field: body -->
Thebes was a major Mycenaean centre whose Linear B tablets record offerings and personnel, though the modern town sits directly on top of it and has limited excavation.

In myth it is unmatched: the founding by Cadmus, who sowed dragon's teeth; the labours of Oedipus; the Seven Against Thebes; the birth of Dionysus and Heracles. Greek tragedy uses Thebes as the city where things go wrong, a dark mirror of Athens.

Historically it sided with Persia in 480 BC — never forgotten — then rose under Epaminondas to defeat Sparta at Leuctra in 371 BC. Its Sacred Band, 150 pairs of male lovers, was annihilated at Chaeronea in 338; a mass grave of 254 men found under the Lion of Chaeronea is almost certainly them. In 335 BC Thebes revolted against Alexander, who razed the city, sparing only the temples and the house of the poet Pindar.
