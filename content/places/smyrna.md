<!-- field: summary -->
A Greek city on the Aegean coast of Asia Minor, claimed by tradition as Homer's birthplace, destroyed about 600 BC and refounded in the Hellenistic period.

<!-- field: significance -->
A long-lived Ionian city, later the site of modern Izmir.

<!-- field: body -->
Smyrna lay at the head of a deep gulf on the Aegean coast of what is now Turkey. The earliest Greek city, now called Old Smyrna, stood at Bayraklı on a peninsula. Aeolian Greeks settled there and it later joined the Ionian group of cities. Excavations show fortification walls from the ninth century BC onward and an early temple of Athena.

The city was one of those that claimed Homer, and it kept a shrine in his honour. Around 600 BC the Lydian king Alyattes captured the town, and it was left as villages for some three centuries. Smyrna was refounded on the slopes of Mount Pagos, probably under Antigonus and then Lysimachus, with a regular street plan.

Under Rome it was one of the great cities of Asia and a centre of the Second Sophistic. The orator Aelius Aristides lived near it and wrote about its rebuilding after the earthquake of about AD 178. It continued under Byzantine and later rulers as the city of Izmir.
