<!-- field: summary -->
An octagonal marble clock tower in the Roman Agora of Athens, carved with the eight winds and equipped with sundials and a water clock.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the earliest known meteorological stations and the best-preserved ancient timekeeping building.

<!-- field: body -->
The Tower of the Winds is an eight-sided Pentelic marble tower about twelve metres high, standing on the Roman Agora east of the old Agora. Its builder, according to the Roman writer Varro, was Andronikos of Kyrrhos, and it is usually dated to the first century BC, though a second-century date has also been proposed.

Each face is decorated with a frieze figure for one of the eight winds, such as Boreas of the north and Zephyros of the west, and beneath each is a sundial. A bronze Triton, now lost, probably turned on the roof as a weather vane, and inside a water clock was fed from a cistern attached to the tower, giving time on cloudy days and at night.

The tower survived antiquity, was used by dervishes in Ottoman times, and was cleared of accumulated earth in the nineteenth century. A recent conservation programme reopened it to the public.
