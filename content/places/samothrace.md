<!-- field: summary -->
Sanctuary of the Great Gods, whose mystery initiation attracted Philip II and Olympias — and findspot of the Winged Victory.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most important mystery cult after Eleusis, and open to everyone: men, women, citizens, foreigners and the enslaved.

<!-- field: body -->
The Mysteries of the Great Gods promised protection at sea and were unusually inclusive — no restriction by gender, status or origin, which was rare in Greek religion. Initiates received an iron ring and could take a second, higher grade requiring a form of confession.

Philip II is said to have met Olympias during initiation here. The Nike of Samothrace, carved c. 190 BC and set on a marble ship's prow in a fountain basin, was found in 1863 and is now the centrepiece of the Louvre staircase.

What actually happened during initiation is unknown; initiates kept the secret, and unlike Eleusis no ancient writer let anything substantial slip.

The sanctuary's prestige drew royal patronage from across the Hellenistic world: the Ptolemaic queen Arsinoe II funded a great rotunda there in the early third century BC, a building roughly 20 metres across that remains the largest known circular structure built by the Greeks, a scale that signals how seriously ruling dynasties took association with the cult.
