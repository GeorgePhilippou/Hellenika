<!-- field: summary -->
The limestone massif above Delphi in Phocis, sacred to Apollo, the Muses and Dionysus and a symbol of poetry in later Europe.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mountain of Apollo and the Muses, whose name became shorthand for poetry and learning.

<!-- field: body -->
Parnassus is a high limestone massif in central Greece, reaching about 2,457 metres. Delphi stands on its southern foot, below the cliffs of the Phaedriades, and the Castalian spring, where pilgrims purified themselves, emerges from a cleft in the rock.

The mountain was shared between gods. Apollo and the Muses were associated with its upper slopes and with poetic inspiration; in winter, Dionysus was honoured there by women known as Thyiades who climbed to the mountain for night-time rites. The Corycian Cave, high above Delphi, was sacred to Pan and the nymphs, and excavations there have recovered large numbers of knucklebones, lamps and other offerings.

In myth Deucalion's ark came to rest on Parnassus after the flood. In later Europe the name became a metaphor for the world of poets: the Parnassian poets of nineteenth-century France took their name from it.
