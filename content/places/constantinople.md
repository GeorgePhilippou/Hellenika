<!-- field: summary -->
The capital of the Byzantine Empire, founded by Constantine on the Bosporus in AD 330 and taken by the Ottomans in 1453.

<!-- field: significance -->
For over a thousand years the greatest city of the Christian world, "the city" of the Greeks, where Roman government, Orthodox Christianity and Greek learning were preserved.

<!-- field: body -->
Constantinople stood on a triangular peninsula between the Sea of Marmara and the Golden Horn, a deep natural harbour, commanding the Bosporus strait between Europe and Asia. It was built on the site of Byzantion, a Greek colony founded from Megara in the 7th century BC. In 324 Constantine chose it as his new capital and greatly enlarged it, dedicating it on 11 May 330 as a New Rome, with a senate, a hippodrome, palaces and churches.

The city became the largest and richest in Europe. Its walls — above all the land walls of Theodosius II, built in 413 — protected it from Huns, Avars, Persians, Arabs, Bulgars and Rus' for eight centuries. Its monuments included the great church of Hagia Sophia, the Great Palace, the Hippodrome with its obelisk and bronze serpent column brought from Delphi, and markets that drew merchants from across Europe and Asia.

The Greeks often called it simply *hē Polis*, "the City". It was sacked by the Fourth Crusade in 1204 and held by the Latins until 1261, then recovered but impoverished. On 29 May 1453 the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II took it and made it his capital. It is now Istanbul, whose name derives from the Greek phrase *eis tēn polin*, "to the City".
