<!-- field: significance -->
The home town of Aristotle, whose philosophy shaped western thought.

<!-- field: body -->
Stagira was a small harbour town on the eastern coast of Chalcidice, in northern Greece, founded about 655 BC by settlers from Andros. It was tributary to Athens in the fifth century BC and later joined the Chalcidian league.

Aristotle was born there in 384 BC. His father Nicomachus was physician to the Macedonian king Amyntas III, and the philosopher's ties to the Macedonian court shaped his career, as he later became tutor of the young Alexander. Philip II captured and destroyed Stagira during his campaign against the Chalcidian cities in 349 BC. A later tradition, recorded by Plutarch and others, says Philip restored the city and its exiles as a favour to Aristotle, although how far this is true is uncertain.

The site, on a peninsula near modern Olympiada, has been excavated since the 1990s. It shows fortification walls, houses and a sanctuary.
