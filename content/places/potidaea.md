<!-- field: summary -->
A Corinthian colony on the Pallene peninsula whose revolt from Athens in 432 BC and long siege helped bring on the Peloponnesian War.

<!-- field: significance -->
A flashpoint between Athens and Corinth in the years before the Peloponnesian War, and the setting for Socrates' military service.

<!-- field: body -->
Potidaea stood on the narrowest part of the Pallene peninsula in Chalcidice, in northern Greece. It was founded by Corinth around 600 BC but later paid tribute to the Athenian Empire. In 433/432 BC, Athens demanded that it tear down its walls on the seaward side, dismiss its Corinthian magistrates and give hostages. Potidaea revolted with Corinthian help, and Athens laid siege.

The Corinthians appealed to Sparta, and Thucydides counts the affair among the immediate causes of the war. The siege lasted until 430/429 BC, at a cost of two thousand talents; the city surrendered on terms and its people were expelled. Plato's Socrates says he served in the siege and fought bravely, and that he saved Alcibiades after he was wounded.

Athens settled its own colonists there, but the city was taken by Philip II in 356 BC. In 316 BC Cassander refounded it under his own name as Cassandreia, which became an important Macedonian city.
