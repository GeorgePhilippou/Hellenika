<!-- field: summary -->
The port at the Canopic Nile mouth that controlled maritime entry into Egypt before Alexandria.

<!-- field: significance -->
A principal customs and exchange point between Egypt and the Mediterranean before Alexandria displaced it.

<!-- field: body -->
Egyptian Thonis and Greek Heracleion were two names for the same port city at the mouth of the Canopic branch. A bilingual decree and other inscriptions settled what had once been treated as a question of two neighbouring settlements. Before Alexandria existed, ships arriving from the Greek world entered here, declared their cargoes and passed through a controlled gateway to the Nile network.

The city combined commercial infrastructure with major sanctuaries, especially a temple of Amun-Gereb connected to royal legitimacy. Finds of imported ceramics, weights, coins and more than sixty ancient shipwrecks reveal traffic on a scale that literary descriptions alone could not establish. Naukratis lay farther inland and operated within the same exchange system rather than serving as a simple rival port.

Alexandria redirected much of this traffic after 331 BC, but Thonis-Heracleion did not immediately disappear. It remained active into the Ptolemaic and Roman periods before unstable delta sediments, earthquakes and subsidence placed large areas beneath Abu Qir Bay. Underwater excavation has recovered the urban plan, monumental sculpture and ritual deposits in their archaeological context.
