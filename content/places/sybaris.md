<!-- field: summary -->
A rich Achaean colony in southern Italy, destroyed by its neighbour Croton in 510 BC and proverbial for luxury.

<!-- field: significance -->
Its name gave the language the word 'sybarite', and its fall is one of the best-known episodes of western Greek history.

<!-- field: body -->
Sybaris was founded about 720 BC by Achaean settlers on the Gulf of Taranto, between two rivers. Its farmland was rich and it traded across southern Italy, sending goods overland to the Tyrrhenian coast where it had a daughter colony at Laos and a trading partner in the Etruscans.

Greek writers made the city a standard example of luxury, with stories about citizens who forbade noisy trades within the city and who trained horses to dance. Most of these tales are probably later moralising, but the city's wealth was real.

In 510 BC a conflict with neighbouring Croton ended with the city's destruction, according to tradition after Sybaris expelled the nobles who fled to Croton. The site was resettled several times, notably as the Athenian-sponsored colony of Thurii in 444/443 BC, but archaic Sybaris lies deep below silt, which has made it hard to study.
