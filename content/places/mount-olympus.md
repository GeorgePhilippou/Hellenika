<!-- field: summary -->
The highest mountain in Greece, on the border of Macedonia and Thessaly, where the Greeks placed the home of Zeus and the Olympian gods.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mountain that gave its name to the Olympian gods and to the idea of a heavenly court of divine rulers.

<!-- field: body -->
Mount Olympus rises in northern Greece, near the Thermaic Gulf, to 2,917 metres at the peak of Mytikas. Its summit is often hidden in cloud, and it was easily seen as a dwelling place for gods.

In Homer the gods feast in halls on Olympus, hold assemblies there and gaze down on the fighting at Troy. In Hesiod's *Theogony*, Zeus and his siblings fight the Titans from the mountain. Later authors, including Pindar and the tragedians, increasingly treat Olympus as an unreachable abode in the sky rather than an actual mountain, though cult at Dion on its slopes kept the physical mountain sacred.

In the Bronze Age the area was an important route between Macedonia and Thessaly. The summit was first climbed in 1913, and the mountain, now a national park and UNESCO biosphere reserve, remains an emblem of Greek mythology.
