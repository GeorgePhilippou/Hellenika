<!-- field: summary -->
An inland Cypriot kingdom controlling copper-working country south of the Mesaoria plain.

<!-- field: significance -->
Its bilingual and Cypriot-syllabic inscriptions are central evidence for the island’s languages and political institutions.

<!-- field: body -->
Idalion was an inland city-kingdom centred on two acropoleis near modern Dali. Its location commanded routes between the Mesaoria plain, the south coast and copper-producing districts in the Troodos foothills. Sanctuaries, fortifications, workshops and cemeteries reveal an urban community whose importance did not depend on possessing a seaport.

The bronze Idalion Tablet records a contract between the king and the city concerning doctors who treated the wounded during a siege. Written in Greek using the Cypriot syllabary, it is unusually direct evidence for civic institutions, landholding and public obligation. Phoenician inscriptions from the area document another language and cult tradition within the same political landscape.

Kition absorbed the kingdom during the fifth century BC. Later inscriptions style its ruler “king of Kition and Idalion,” making the loss of independence visible in contemporary evidence rather than only in later narrative. Settlement and sanctuary activity continued after annexation and under the Ptolemies, so political extinction did not mean that the place or its population vanished.
