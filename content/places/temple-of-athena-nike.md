<!-- field: summary -->
The small Ionic temple of Athena as goddess of victory, built on a bastion at the entrance to the Acropolis in the 420s BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
A jewel of the Ionic order, whose sculptured parapet of Victories is among the most graceful works of classical art.

<!-- field: body -->
The temple of Athena Nike stands on a high bastion to the right of the entrance to the Acropolis, where an older shrine of Athena as giver of victory had stood. An inscription, usually dated around 448 BC, ordered a temple to be built to the design of Callicrates, but construction took place only in the 420s, during the Peloponnesian War.

It is a small Ionic temple with four slender columns at front and back. Its frieze showed the gods and battles of Greeks against Persians and Greeks, a rare depiction of historical warfare on a temple. Around the edge of the bastion ran a marble parapet carved with winged Victories leading cattle to sacrifice and setting up trophies; the best known, "Nike adjusting her sandal", with drapery clinging like wet cloth, is in the Acropolis Museum.

In 1686, preparing for a Venetian attack, the Ottomans dismantled the temple and used its blocks in a gun emplacement. After Greek independence the blocks were recovered and the temple was reassembled in 1835–36. It has been dismantled and rebuilt twice since, most recently in a restoration completed in 2010, to correct earlier errors and repair damage.
