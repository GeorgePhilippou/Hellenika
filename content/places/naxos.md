<!-- field: summary -->
The largest of the Cyclades, famous for marble, early monumental sculpture and as the island where Dionysus found Ariadne.

<!-- field: significance -->
A leading sculptural centre of the archaic Aegean whose siege in 499 BC set off the Ionian Revolt.

<!-- field: body -->
Naxos is the largest and most fertile of the Cyclades, with high mountains and quarries of fine marble and emery. It was inhabited from the Neolithic period and was a major centre of the early Cycladic culture, many of whose marble figurines come from its cemeteries.

In the seventh and sixth centuries BC, Naxos became one of the leading centres of marble sculpture in Greece. Its craftsmen exported large kouroi, and the Naxians dedicated a colossal statue and the row of marble lions at Delos. Quarries on the island still contain unfinished giant statues, one of them at Apollonas. The Portara, the giant marble doorway of an unfinished temple of Apollo, was begun about 530 BC under the tyrant Lygdamis and still stands by the harbour.

In myth, Theseus abandoned Ariadne on Naxos, where Dionysus found her. In 499 BC, Aristagoras of Miletus led a Persian-backed attack on Naxos that failed, an event Herodotus gives as the first cause of the Ionian Revolt.
