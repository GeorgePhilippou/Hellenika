<!-- field: summary -->
A hilltop city-kingdom commanding the fertile Kouris valley on Cyprus’s southern coast.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the island’s principal kingdoms, with a commanding acropolis and long-lived sanctuary landscape.

<!-- field: body -->
Kourion stood on a high coastal ridge overlooking the Kouris valley, with farmland below and access to the south-coast sea lanes. Settlement and rich burial evidence at nearby Bamboula connect the district with the transformations of the twelfth century BC. Later Greek tradition claimed settlers from Argos, but archaeology shows cultural change and continuing local development rather than a single recoverable migration event.

By the Iron Age Kourion was one of Cyprus’s city-kingdoms. Its rulers issued coinage and negotiated the same layers of Assyrian, Egyptian and Persian supremacy as their neighbours. The kingdom’s territory linked the coast to inland resources, while the extra-urban sanctuary of Apollo Hylates became a durable regional cult centre.

Most monuments visible on the acropolis—the theatre in its surviving form, bath buildings, mosaics and episcopal complex—are Hellenistic, Roman or later. They overlie the political centre of the earlier kingdom rather than illustrating it directly. Keeping those phases separate matters: an impressive Roman cityscape cannot by itself reconstruct the institutions or boundaries of Archaic Kourion.
