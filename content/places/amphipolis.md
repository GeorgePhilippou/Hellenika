<!-- field: summary -->
An Athenian colony on the river Strymon in Macedonia, whose capture by Brasidas in 424 BC and by Philip II in 357 BC both changed the course of Greek history.

<!-- field: significance -->
A city whose loss cost Athens its northern timber and silver and led to the exile of Thucydides.

<!-- field: body -->
Amphipolis was founded by Athens in 437/436 BC at a bend of the Strymon, in a region that supplied timber for ships and silver from the Pangaion mines. The Athenian general Hagnon led the settlers, who were joined by local Thracians.

In the winter of 424 BC the Spartan Brasidas approached the city suddenly and offered generous terms. Thucydides, commanding Athenian ships at Thasos, arrived too late to save it and was sent into exile, which gave him the time to write his history. In 422 BC Cleon and Brasidas both died fighting outside its walls. The Peace of Nicias required Sparta to return the city, but Amphipolis never rejoined Athens.

Philip II captured the city in 357 BC, gaining control of the mines, and it remained Macedonian. Excavations since 2012 have focused on the Kasta Tomb, a huge mound whose burial chamber has caryatids and a mosaic.
