<!-- field: summary -->
A northwest-coast city-kingdom whose Achaemenid-era loyalty to Athens made it, briefly, a target of its own Persian-backed neighbour.

<!-- field: significance -->
Its long excavation record, from Swedish and Canadian missions, traces continuous urban life from the Archaic period into Late Antiquity.

<!-- field: body -->
Soloi occupied a defensible position on the northwest coast above Morphou Bay, close to the copper-bearing foothills of the Troodos range. It is named as one of ten Cypriot kings paying tribute to the Assyrian king Esarhaddon around 673 BC, placing it firmly among the island's recognised Iron Age kingdoms from an early date.

According to Herodotus, Soloi held out during the Ionian Revolt-era Cypriot rebellion against Persian rule (497 BC) for longer than any other Cypriot city under siege. In response, a nearby pro-Persian ruler is thought to have built the hilltop palace at Vouni partly to keep watch over Soloi's activity — one of the clearer examples of Cyprus's city-kingdoms being played against each other by outside power.

Excavations by the Swedish Cyprus Expedition and later a Canadian mission from Laval University uncovered defensive walls, an agora, a temple of Athena, a Roman-period theatre, and a substantial fourth-century AD Christian basilica — evidence that the city remained a significant urban centre for roughly a thousand years after its kingdom lost independence.
