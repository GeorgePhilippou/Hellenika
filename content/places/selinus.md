<!-- field: summary -->
A Greek colony in south-west Sicily, notable for its monumental temples and for its quarrel with Segesta, which drew Athens into Sicily.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the best-preserved temple complexes of the western Greeks, and a cause of the Athenian Sicilian expedition.

<!-- field: body -->
Selinus was founded about 628 BC by Megarian colonists from Megara Hyblaea on the south-west coast of Sicily, at the frontier with Carthaginian and Elymian territory. It prospered from farmland and trade, and its wealth is visible in temples on the acropolis and on the eastern hill.

These are the largest Doric temples of the western Greeks. Temple G, never finished, was among the biggest of the Greek world, and sculpted metopes from earlier temples, now in the museum at Palermo, show the archaic Sicilian style. Selinus also produced a famous series of silver coins showing a celery leaf, the plant for which the city was named.

War with Segesta over borderland led Segesta to ask Athens for help in 416 BC. The Athenians sent an expedition that ended in disaster. In 409 BC a Carthaginian army took Selinus by storm and slaughtered much of its population. The city was partly resettled under Carthaginian control and finally abandoned in the third century BC.
