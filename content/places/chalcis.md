<!-- field: summary -->
The leading city of Euboea in the eighth and seventh centuries BC, a major colonising power and rival of Eretria.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the most active city-founders of the colonising age, which passed the Euboean alphabet and trade west.

<!-- field: body -->
Chalcis lay on the narrowest point of the Euripus, the channel between Euboea and the mainland. Its name may mean the 'bronze' city, and it was rich in metals and trade. In the eighth century BC it was a major sender of colonists, who founded cities in Chalcidice, which takes its name from them, and in the west, with Cumae in Campania, Naxos and Zancle in Sicily and Rhegion in Italy.

The city's long rivalry with Eretria is remembered as the Lelantine War, the first conflict said to have drawn in other Greek states. Archaeologists connect the decline of Lefkandi around 700 BC with this period, though details of the war remain uncertain.

In 506 BC the young Athenian democracy defeated Chalcis and shared out its best land among Athenian settlers. It stayed under Athens until 446 BC, and after that formed part of the Euboean league. The modern city of Chalkida continues on the site.
