<!-- field: summary -->
A major south-coast harbour whose Bronze Age settlement became a Phoenician-led Cypriot city-kingdom.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of Cyprus’s principal ports and the island’s strongest centre of Phoenician language and cult.

<!-- field: body -->
Kition developed around sheltered harbours at modern Larnaca. Late Bronze Age occupation included monumental buildings, industrial areas and evidence for processing copper brought from the island’s interior. Imported pottery and other finds place the settlement within maritime networks joining Cyprus to the Levant, Egypt and the Aegean before any historically documented kingdom existed.

Phoenician language and institutions became especially prominent during the early first millennium BC. Inscriptions name kings and deities such as Astarte and Reshef-Mikal, while Greek and Cypriot traditions remained present around them. It is therefore more accurate to describe Kition as a Phoenician-led Cypriot kingdom than as a foreign colony that replaced an empty landscape.

Its rulers expanded inland and absorbed Idalion in the fifth century BC, gaining access to copper routes and agricultural territory. A fourth-century Phoenician inscription records a king of “Kition and Idalion” commemorating victory. The kingdom participated in the shifting Assyrian, Egyptian and Persian systems that claimed authority over Cyprus, then lost its monarchy when Ptolemaic administration unified the island.
