<!-- field: summary -->
The small Ionian island that Greek tradition identifies as the home of Odysseus, though the match with Homer's description is debated.

<!-- field: significance -->
The island whose name became a symbol of home and the journey back to it.

<!-- field: body -->
Ithaca is a narrow, rocky island in the Ionian Sea, off the west coast of Greece, near Kefalonia. It is the traditional homeland of Odysseus, and the *Odyssey* is built around his twenty years' absence from it. By the classical period the island was identified as Homer's Ithaca, and the Polis cave, where offerings to Odysseus were left, supports a local hero-cult from at least Hellenistic times.

Homer's description does not fit the modern island in all details. He says Ithaca lies furthest to the west and describes its position relative to nearby islands in obscure terms. For this reason some scholars, from Wilhelm Dörpfeld in the early twentieth century, have placed Odysseus' home on Leucas, and others on the Paliki peninsula of Kefalonia. Ancient writers did not question Ithaca's identity, and Schliemann dug there in 1868.

In historical times Ithaca was a small and modest community. Its modern fame comes from the epic, and from Cavafy's poem which makes it a symbol of the purpose of travel.
