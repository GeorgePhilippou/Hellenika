<!-- field: summary -->
The oracular sanctuary of Apollo near Miletus, whose colossal Hellenistic temple was never completed.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most important oracle in Asia Minor, and one of the largest Greek temples ever begun.

<!-- field: body -->
Didyma lay on the coast of Ionia about 20 kilometres south of Miletus, to which it was joined by a Sacred Way lined with statues. It housed an oracle of Apollo run by the Branchidae, a priestly family, and was consulted by cities and kings. Herodotus reports that Croesus sent gifts to it.

The archaic temple was destroyed after the Ionian Revolt. Tradition says the Persians removed Apollo's bronze statue, and that Seleucus I restored it in the early third century BC. About 300 BC the Milesians began a vast new temple, designed by Paionios of Ephesus and Daphnis of Miletus. It was a double-colonnaded building with an unroofed inner court enclosing a small shrine, and was still unfinished centuries later.

The oracle faded after Christianity became the official religion, and a church was built inside the ruins. German archaeologists have excavated the site since 1905.
