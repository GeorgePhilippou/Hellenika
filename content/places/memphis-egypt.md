<!-- field: summary -->
The ancient capital near the head of the Nile Delta and the principal cult centre of Ptah.

<!-- field: significance -->
A political and religious centre for more than three millennia, including under Persian, Macedonian and Ptolemaic rule.

<!-- field: body -->
Memphis stood near the point where the Nile divides into its Delta branches, a strategic position between Upper Egypt, the Delta and the routes west towards the Fayyum. Tradition credited its foundation to Menes, the legendary unifier of Egypt; archaeology instead reveals a settlement and royal landscape that developed across the earliest dynasties. The nearby pyramid fields at Saqqara, Abusir and Giza belonged to this Memphite political and ceremonial world.

It was the principal cult centre of Ptah, patron of craftspeople, whose great temple gave the city one of its Egyptian names, Hut-ka-Ptah. Greek speakers ultimately used a related form for the country itself: *Aigyptos*. Even when royal residences moved elsewhere, Memphis retained workshops, temples, garrisons and a priesthood able to confer Egyptian legitimacy.

Assyrians, Persians and Macedonians all treated possession of Memphis as politically meaningful. Alexander was recognised there after entering Egypt in 332 BC. The Ptolemies ruled from Alexandria but continued to patronise Ptah’s temple; the Memphis Decree of 196 BC, preserved most famously on the Rosetta Stone, records priests honouring Ptolemy V in the traditional idiom of Egyptian kingship.
