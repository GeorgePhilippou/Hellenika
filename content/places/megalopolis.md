<!-- field: summary -->
A planned city founded in the 360s BC as the capital of the Arcadian League, home to the largest theatre in Greece and to the historian Polybius.

<!-- field: significance -->
A deliberate act of city-building meant to block Spartan power in the Peloponnese, and the home city of Polybius.

<!-- field: body -->
Megalopolis, 'the great city', was founded in about 371–368 BC after the Theban victory at Leuctra weakened Sparta. Arcadian villages were joined in a single city beside the Helisson to provide an Arcadian League with a capital and to guard the north of Laconia against Spartan revenge. Epaminondas encouraged the project.

The city's theatre was among the largest in Greece, and beside it stood the Thersilion, a roofed assembly hall with the seats radiating from the centre, built to hold the Ten Thousand delegates of the League. The British School at Athens excavated both in the 1890s.

Megalopolis stood by Macedon against Sparta, and joined the Achaean League in 235 BC. In 223 BC Cleomenes III took and destroyed much of the city. Philopoemen, the last great Achaean general, and Polybius, who wrote the history of Rome's rise, were both from Megalopolis. The city declined after the Roman conquest, and Pausanias found it largely deserted.
