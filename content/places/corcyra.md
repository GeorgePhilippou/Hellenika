<!-- field: summary -->
A Corinthian colony on the island of Corfu that became a naval power, quarrelled with Corinth and pulled Athens into the events leading to the Peloponnesian War.

<!-- field: significance -->
The quarrel between Corcyra and Corinth was a direct cause of the Peloponnesian War, and its civil war of 427 BC became Thucydides' picture of political breakdown.

<!-- field: body -->
Corcyra, modern Corfu, was founded by Corinth around 734 BC, and was positioned on the route to Italy and Sicily. The colony grew powerful, kept a large fleet, and was at odds with its mother city from early on.

In 435–433 BC a dispute over the colony of Epidamnus led to war between Corcyra and Corinth. Corcyra appealed to Athens, which made a defensive alliance with it and sent ships to the battle of Sybota in 433 BC. Corinth, angered, pressed Sparta to declare war on Athens. In 427 BC, democrats and oligarchs fought in Corcyra with great cruelty, and Thucydides saw in it the pattern for civil strife everywhere.

The city's most famous monument is the Gorgon pediment of its temple of Artemis, about 580 BC, found at Garitsa in 1911 and now in the Corfu museum. In antiquity some identified the island with Homer's Scheria, home of the Phaeacians.
