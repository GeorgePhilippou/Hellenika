<!-- field: summary -->
The largest Minoan palace complex — over a thousand interlocking rooms around a central court — and the source of the labyrinth tradition.

<!-- field: significance -->
Europe's first great architectural complex, the administrative heart of Minoan Crete, and the site where both Linear A and Linear B were first found.

<!-- field: body -->
Knossos sits on a tell occupied continuously from around 7000 BC. The first palace was built c. 1900 BC, destroyed and rebuilt c. 1700 BC, and finally abandoned around 1350 BC.

The complex is organised around a rectangular central court roughly 50 by 25 metres, with storage magazines to the west holding rows of giant *pithoi*, ceremonial rooms, workshops, and a light-well system that ventilated multiple storeys. Its drainage and sanitation were not equalled in Europe until the modern era.

The density of interconnected rooms plausibly lies behind the Greek word *labyrinthos* — possibly from *labrys*, the double axe carved repeatedly into its stones. Evans made the connection explicit and named the whole civilisation after Minos.

After c. 1450 BC, when other Cretan centres were destroyed, Knossos continued under administrators writing Linear B in Greek — the clearest single indication of Mycenaean control.
