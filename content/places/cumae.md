<!-- field: summary -->
The first Greek colony on the Italian mainland, founded by Euboeans on the coast of Campania, and the place through which the Greek alphabet reached Rome.

<!-- field: significance -->
An early gateway between the Greek and Italian worlds.

<!-- field: body -->
Cumae was founded by settlers from Chalcis and Eretria, probably in the mid-eighth century BC, a little after their trading post on the island of Pithekoussai (Ischia). It stood on a coastal hill north of the Bay of Naples and ruled a fertile plain, and for a time was the most powerful Greek city in the region.

Through contact with Etruscans and Latins, Cumae seems to have passed on the Euboean form of the Greek alphabet, from which the Etruscan and then the Latin alphabets derive. In 474 BC the Cumaeans appealed to Hieron I of Syracuse, whose fleet destroyed an Etruscan force off the coast and weakened Etruscan power in the south.

In 421 BC the city fell to the Samnites. Later it was a Roman town. In Roman tradition it was the home of the Sibyl who guided Aeneas, and a vaulted gallery on the acropolis has long been shown as her cave, although archaeologists doubt the link.
