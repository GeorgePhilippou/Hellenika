<!-- field: summary -->
The long island off the east coast of central Greece, home of the trading cities Chalcis and Eretria and of the earliest Greek contacts with the Levant and Italy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The island that led the Greek recovery after the Dark Age and opened the sea routes west.

<!-- field: body -->
Euboea is a long, mountainous island lying along the east coast of Attica and Boeotia. At the narrowest point of the Euripus channel, ships could pass only with the tide, and a bridge connects the island to the mainland near Chalcis.

In the tenth and ninth centuries BC, Euboeans, notably the people of Lefkandi, traded with Cyprus and the Levant, and Euboean pottery has been found at Al Mina in Syria and in Italy. The cities of Chalcis and Eretria founded colonies in Chalcidice, on Sicily and in southern Italy, where Pithekoussai and Cumae transmitted their alphabet to Italy. The Lelantine War between the two main cities is the first conflict in Greek tradition to involve other states.

In the Persian Wars, Eretria was destroyed in 490 BC and a naval battle was fought at Artemisium in 480. After the island joined the Athenian Empire, it rose against Athens in 446 BC, but Pericles crossed with an army and reconquered it. The island fell to Philip II in the fourth century.
