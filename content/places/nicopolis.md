<!-- field: summary -->
The "Victory City" founded by Octavian in north-western Greece to celebrate his victory at Actium in 31 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
The largest Roman foundation in Greece, a monument to the battle that ended the Hellenistic age, and later the home of Epictetus' school.

<!-- field: body -->
Nicopolis stands on the isthmus north of the Ambracian Gulf, opposite Actium, where Octavian camped before defeating Antony and Cleopatra in 31 BC. Soon afterwards he founded a city on the site of his camp and named it Nicopolis, "Victory City". He populated it by moving, often by force, the inhabitants of many towns of Epirus, Acarnania and Aetolia, and made it a free city with a vote in the Amphictyonic Council of Delphi.

On the hill where his tent had stood he built a victory monument decorated with the bronze rams of captured warships; the sockets for them can still be seen. He also refounded the Actian Games, held every four years in honour of Apollo, which ranked with the Olympic Games.

Nicopolis became the main city of north-western Greece. The Stoic Epictetus taught there from the 90s AD, and Paul wrote of planning to spend a winter there. The city had a theatre, an odeon, a stadium, baths and an aqueduct; in the early Christian period it was a bishop's seat with large mosaic-floored basilicas. It declined after raids in the early Middle Ages and was abandoned by the 11th century. Its walls and ruins survive.
