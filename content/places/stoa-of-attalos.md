<!-- field: summary -->
A two-storey colonnade on the east side of the Athenian Agora given to the city by Attalus II of Pergamon, and now rebuilt as the Agora museum.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the few ancient Greek public buildings reconstructed on its original site, showing how a Hellenistic market building looked.

<!-- field: body -->
The Stoa of Attalos was built in the mid-second century BC by Attalus II, king of Pergamon, who had studied in Athens and wished to show goodwill to the city. It formed the east boundary of the Agora: a long, two-storey colonnade of Doric columns outside and Ionic within, with twenty-one shops opening off each floor and a broad walkway in front.

Market stalls, offices and promenades filled such stoas, and this one became a busy commercial and social centre. It was burned in the Herulian raid of AD 267, and its ruins were built into the late Roman wall that crosses the Agora.

The American School of Classical Studies cleared the site in the 1930s and, funded by John D. Rockefeller Jr., rebuilt the stoa in 1953–56 from the surviving foundations and fragments. It houses the Museum of the Ancient Agora.
