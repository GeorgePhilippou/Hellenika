<!-- field: summary -->
A wealthy Greek colony on the south coast of Sicily, famous for the Doric temples of its Valley of the Temples and as the birthplace of Empedocles.

<!-- field: significance -->
One of the richest western Greek cities, with the best-preserved group of Greek temples outside Greece itself.

<!-- field: body -->
Akragas was founded around 580 BC by Gelan and Rhodian colonists on a ridge overlooking the south coast of Sicily. Its early history is dominated by the tyrant Phalaris, remembered for cruelty, and by Theron, who ruled in the early fifth century BC. With Gelon of Syracuse, Theron defeated the Carthaginians at Himera in 480 BC, and prisoners and spoils from the war paid for monumental building.

The temples along the southern ridge, among them the so-called Temple of Concordia, one of the best-preserved Doric temples anywhere, and the huge Temple of Olympian Zeus, reflect the city's wealth. Diodorus describes Akragas as one of the most luxurious cities of the Greek world, and claims its population was very large, though his numbers are probably inflated.

Carthage sacked Akragas in 406 BC after a long siege, and it never fully recovered. It later came under Roman rule as Agrigentum. The site is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
