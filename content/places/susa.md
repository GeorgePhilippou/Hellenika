<!-- field: summary -->
An ancient city of south-western Iran that Darius I made an administrative capital of the Persian Empire, and where Alexander held his mass wedding in 324 BC.

<!-- field: significance -->
A principal capital of the empire the Greeks fought in the Persian Wars and Alexander conquered.

<!-- field: body -->
Susa lay in the lowlands of Elam, in south-west Iran. It was already about three thousand years old when Cyrus the Great's empire absorbed it. Darius I rebuilt it from around 520 BC as a main royal residence, and it served as the winter capital of the Achaemenid court. He built a palace with a great audience hall and enamelled brick friezes, and his foundation inscriptions list materials from Lebanon, Ionia, India and Egypt.

The Royal Road, a staffed highway of the Persian state with relay stations, connected Susa to Sardis. Greek writers knew the city as the seat of the king: Aeschylus' *Persians* is set there. After Gaugamela, Alexander captured Susa with its treasury, and in 324 BC he married the daughter of Darius III there while about eighty of his companions married Persian noblewomen.

French excavators, starting with Marcel Dieulafoy in the 1880s, uncovered the palace and recovered glazed-brick friezes, now in the Louvre.
