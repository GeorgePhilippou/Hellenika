<!-- field: summary -->
A Dodecanese island, birthplace of Hippocrates and Ptolemy II, with one of the most famous healing sanctuaries of antiquity.

<!-- field: significance -->
The island most associated with the beginnings of rational medicine.

<!-- field: body -->
Kos is an island in the south-eastern Aegean, close to the Anatolian coast. Settled by Dorian Greeks, it was an early member of the Dorian Hexapolis. The physician Hippocrates was born there about 460 BC, and the medical school of Kos, which stressed careful observation and the natural causes of disease, was associated with him. A plane tree in the town is shown to visitors as the tree under which he taught, though it is much younger than that.

The Asclepieion of Kos, on terraces above the city, was built in the third century BC and continued to expand in later centuries. It combined a temple of Asclepius with baths, a gymnasium and a medical school, and drew pilgrims who sought cures.

Ptolemy II was born on Kos in 309 BC, and the island kept close ties with the Ptolemies. Excavations by Rudolf Herzog from 1902 revealed the sanctuary.
