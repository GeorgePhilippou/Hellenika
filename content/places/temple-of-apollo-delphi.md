<!-- field: summary -->
The Doric temple of Apollo at Delphi, in whose inner chamber the Pythia delivered the oracle consulted across the Greek world.

<!-- field: significance -->
The heart of the most important oracle in the ancient world.

<!-- field: body -->
The temple of Apollo stood on a terrace above the Sacred Way at Delphi. Tradition recalled several earlier temples, and a stone building of the sixth century BC burned in 548 BC. Herodotus says it was rebuilt with funds from across Greece and that the Alcmaeonid family of Athens, then in exile, paid for a marble front. In 373 BC the temple was wrecked, probably by an earthquake.

The temple whose foundations are visible today was built between about 366 and 329 BC. It was a Doric peripteral building of six by fifteen columns, with sculpture on its pediments. In the pronaos were inscribed Delphic maxims such as 'Know thyself', recorded by Pausanias. Inside, the *adyton* was a lower chamber in which the Pythia sat on a tripod, and where the omphalos and a flame burned continually.

Plutarch served as a priest here around AD 100. Pagan cult ended in the later fourth century AD. The French School at Athens excavated the site from 1892, and the six columns standing today were set up in 1938 from fallen pieces.
