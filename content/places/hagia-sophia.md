<!-- field: summary -->
The great church of Constantinople, built by Justinian in 532–537, whose vast dome was the wonder of the medieval world.

<!-- field: significance -->
The supreme achievement of Byzantine architecture and the cathedral of Orthodox Christianity for nine centuries.

<!-- field: body -->
An earlier church of Holy Wisdom (*Hagia Sophia*) on the site was burned in the Nika riots of 532. Justinian immediately ordered a new one, designed not by ordinary builders but by two scientists, Anthemius of Tralles, a mathematician, and Isidore of Miletus, a geometer. With some 10,000 workers it was finished in less than six years and dedicated on 27 December 537; Justinian is said to have exclaimed, "Solomon, I have surpassed you!"

The building combines a long basilica with a huge central dome, about 31 metres across, carried on four great arches and pendentives and buttressed by half-domes. A ring of forty windows at its base makes the dome seem to float; the historian Procopius wrote that it appeared to be hung from heaven by a golden chain. The interior was covered in gold mosaics and coloured marbles. The first dome, too shallow, collapsed after an earthquake in 558 and was rebuilt higher in 562.

Hagia Sophia was the seat of the patriarch and the setting of imperial coronations. After the Ottoman conquest in 1453 it became a mosque, with minarets added; in 1934 it was made a museum, and in 2020 it was turned back into a mosque. Many of its Byzantine mosaics survive.
