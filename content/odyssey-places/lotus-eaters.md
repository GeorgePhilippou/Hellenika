<!-- field: summary -->
A land where the fruit itself is the danger — eat it, and the desire to ever go home disappears.

<!-- field: myth -->
A storm off Cape Malea drives the fleet nine days south. Scouts sent ashore eat the lotus plant and lose all wish to return — Odysseus has to drag them back to the ships in tears and tie them under the rowing benches.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Odyssey Book 9.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
Homer gives no coordinates; "nine days before the north wind" is a narrative device, not a distance. Djerba is the most commonly cited traditional identification, on the strength of a local lotus-like plant and a long-standing (though modern) tourist tradition — not an ancient one.
