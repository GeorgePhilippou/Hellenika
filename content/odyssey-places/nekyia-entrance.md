<!-- field: summary -->
The edge of Ocean, where Odysseus digs a pit of blood to summon the dead and speaks with the prophet Tiresias.

<!-- field: myth -->
On Circe's instructions, Odysseus sails to the edge of the world, in permanent mist and darkness, and digs a trench filled with the blood of sacrificed animals. The shades of the dead crowd to drink it and speak; among them is the blind prophet Tiresias, who tells Odysseus how to get home and warns him never to touch the Cattle of the Sun. He also meets the ghosts of his mother and of dead companions from Troy, including Achilles and Agamemnon.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Odyssey Book 11.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
Homer places this at the edge of Ocean, in the land of the Cimmerians who never see the sun — deliberately at the boundary of the known world, not a real destination. Cape Tainaron (Cape Matapan), the southernmost point of mainland Greece, carried a genuine ancient reputation as an entrance to the underworld and is the most common Greek-world candidate, as against Italian sites (Lake Avernus) favoured by the later Roman tradition around Aeneas.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
A real sanctuary of Poseidon stood at Cape Tainaron in antiquity, and ancient sources including Pausanias describe a cave there popularly believed to be an entrance to Hades — genuine ancient tradition, though not one tied specifically to the Odyssey's own (deliberately vague) geography.
