<!-- field: summary -->
A strait so narrow that avoiding the whirlpool on one side sails you straight into the six-headed monster on the other.

<!-- field: myth -->
Odysseus must choose between two evils in a narrow channel: Charybdis, a whirlpool that would swallow the whole ship, and Scylla, a six-headed monster in a cliffside cave who is guaranteed to take exactly six men, one per head, but no more. On Circe's advice he steers toward Scylla, accepting a small certain loss over a total, uncertain one — Scylla seizes six sailors as he passes.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Odyssey Book 12.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
The Strait of Messina, between Sicily and mainland Italy — genuinely narrow, with real (if much milder than described) currents and whirlpools — has been identified with this episode since at least the time of Thucydides, making it one of the oldest and most persistent traditional identifications in the whole poem, though ancient geographers themselves (notably Strabo's report of Eratosthenes' scepticism) already doubted that Homer's fantastic geography could be pinned to any real strait.
