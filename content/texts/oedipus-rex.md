<!-- field: summary -->
A king investigating a plague discovers that he is himself the cause — the most tightly constructed plot in Greek drama.

<!-- field: body -->
Thebes is dying of plague. The oracle says the killer of the previous king must be found. Oedipus takes charge of the investigation with total confidence, and every step towards the truth is a step towards his destruction.

The irony is structural rather than decorative: the audience knows from the start, so every confident statement Oedipus makes lands differently for them than for him. He curses the killer without qualification. He mocks the blind prophet Teiresias for not seeing.

Aristotle used it in the *Poetics* as his model of plot construction, singling out the messenger scene where the news intended to reassure Oedipus is precisely what destroys him — *peripeteia* and *anagnorisis* arriving in the same moment.

Freud took the name for his complex, which has coloured every subsequent reading. The play itself is about knowledge, not desire: Oedipus is destroyed by finding out.
