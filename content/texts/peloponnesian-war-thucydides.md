<!-- field: summary -->
The war between Athens and Sparta, analysed without gods, omens or consolation — the founding work of critical history.

<!-- field: body -->
Thucydides announces his method against Herodotus: he has excluded the marvellous, cross-checked eyewitness accounts, and accepts that this makes the work less pleasant to hear. He intends it as "a possession for all time".

Its most quoted passages are structural analyses of political behaviour. The Melian Dialogue strips diplomacy to its mechanism — the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must — in an exchange that ends with Athens killing every man on Melos. The account of civil war at Corcyra describes how the meanings of words change under factional violence, so that recklessness becomes courage and moderation becomes cowardice.

The speeches are the central methodological problem. He admits he could not remember exact words and gives what the situation demanded, which makes them analysis in the mouths of participants.

It stops mid-sentence in 411 BC.
