<!-- field: summary -->
A tragedy in which the Titan Prometheus, chained to a rock by Zeus for giving fire to humans, defies the tyranny of the new king of the gods.

<!-- field: significance -->
The great drama of defiance against power, whose rebel Titan became a hero for Romantic poets and revolutionaries.

<!-- field: body -->
*Prometheus Bound* has come down to us among the plays of Aeschylus, but its style and ideas have led many scholars to doubt that he wrote it; it may be the work of a follower, or his son, in the 440s or 430s BC. It was probably part of a trilogy whose other plays, including *Prometheus Unbound*, are lost.

The play opens as the gods Power and Force, with a reluctant Hephaestus, chain the Titan Prometheus to a rock in the remote Caucasus. His crime was to steal fire and give it to humans, against the will of Zeus, the new ruler of the gods. Prometheus, whose name means "forethought", lists the arts he has taught mankind — building, writing, numbers, medicine, sailing — and presents himself as humanity's benefactor.

He is visited by the chorus of sea nymphs, by the god Oceanus, who urges him to submit, and by Io, a mortal woman driven across the world by the jealousy of Hera. Prometheus knows a secret that threatens Zeus' rule and refuses to reveal it. When Hermes comes to demand it, he still refuses, and the play ends as Zeus hurls him into the abyss with thunder and earthquake.

Its portrait of Zeus as a harsh new tyrant has made the play a lasting image of resistance, and inspired Shelley's *Prometheus Unbound*.
