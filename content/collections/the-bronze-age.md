<!-- field: summary -->
An interconnected world of palaces, archives and long-distance trade — and the two generations in which all of it failed.

<!-- field: intro -->
By 1300 BC the eastern Mediterranean was a single system: Mycenaean Greece, Minoan Crete, the Hittites, Egypt, Ugarit and Cyprus, all trading, corresponding and intermarrying. Bronze itself required it — copper from Cyprus, tin from Afghanistan. Within about fifty years almost all of it was gone. This route follows the system, and then its failure.
