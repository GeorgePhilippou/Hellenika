<!-- field: summary -->
Two life-size Greek bronze warriors recovered from the sea in 1972 — original Classical bronzes, not copies.

<!-- field: significance -->
Two of the very few large Greek bronzes to survive as originals, showing what Classical sculpture really looked like before Roman marble copies.

<!-- field: body -->
Two life-size bronze statues of nude warriors, about 2 m tall, made in Greece around 460–450 BC. They were spotted on the seabed off Riace in Calabria, southern Italy, by a snorkeller in 1972, probably lost overboard from an ancient ship. After conservation in Florence they were first shown in 1981, and are now in the National Museum of Magna Graecia, Reggio Calabria.

Both men stand with their weight on one leg, once holding shields and spears. Statue A is younger and more intense, with lips slightly parted; Statue B is older and calmer. Who they represent, and whether they come from a single monument, is debated.

Almost everything known about Classical sculpture comes from Roman marble copies; the Greek bronze originals were nearly all melted down for metal. These are originals, and the difference is striking. Freshly cast, the bronze was golden-brown, not green. Lips and nipples are inlaid in copper, teeth in silver, and the eyes in ivory and coloured stone, giving a lifelike effect no marble copy reproduces. Clay from the original casting was still sealed inside them.

They are among the most important finds of Greek art made in the 20th century.
