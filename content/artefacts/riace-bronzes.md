<!-- field: summary -->
Two life-size Greek bronze warriors recovered from the sea in 1972 — original Classical bronzes, not copies.

<!-- field: body -->
Found by a snorkeller off Riace in Calabria, probably jettisoned or lost from a Roman ship carrying looted Greek art.

They matter because they are originals. Almost everything we know of Classical sculpture is Roman marble copies; here is the real thing, and it is startlingly different. The bronze surface would have been a warm gold-brown, not green. Lips and nipples are inlaid in copper, teeth in silver, eyes in ivory and glass paste. Statue A's expression, with parted lips and inset eyes looking slightly aside, has a specificity no marble copy conveys.

They demonstrate what was lost when the bronzes were melted: not just objects but a whole visual register — polychrome, glinting, closer to a living body than white marble ever suggested.
