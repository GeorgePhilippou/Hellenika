<!-- field: summary -->
Pheidias' colossal gold-and-ivory statue of Athena inside the Parthenon, dedicated in 438 BC and now lost.

<!-- field: significance -->
The centrepiece of Periclean Athens and its treasury in gold, known today only through copies.

<!-- field: body -->
The Athena Parthenos was the great cult statue of the Parthenon, made by Pheidias and dedicated in 438 BC. The goddess stood about 12 m tall, in a long robe, wearing a helmet and the aegis, with a figure of Victory (Nike) in her outstretched right hand and her left resting on a shield, with a great snake beside her. Her skin was ivory and her clothing gold, over a wooden core.

The statue was also a reserve of wealth: Thucydides records that its gold weighed 40 talents, more than a tonne, and was removable, so that the city could use it in an emergency, provided it was replaced. Its shield, sandals and base were carved with scenes of battles against Amazons, giants and centaurs, and the birth of Pandora. A legend said Pheidias put portraits of himself and Pericles in the shield's Amazon battle, and was prosecuted for it.

The statue was damaged by fire and repaired in antiquity, and was probably taken to Constantinople in late antiquity; its fate is unknown. Pausanias described it in the 2nd century AD. Its appearance is known from small Roman marble copies, above all the Varvakeion Athena in the National Archaeological Museum, Athens, and from coins and gems.

A full-size modern replica stands in the Parthenon replica at Nashville, Tennessee.
