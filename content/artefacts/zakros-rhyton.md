<!-- field: summary -->
A libation vessel of carved rock crystal, reassembled from over 300 fragments — the finest surviving Minoan stone vessel.

<!-- field: body -->
Rock crystal is quartz: hard, brittle and unforgiving. Hollowing a vessel from it with bronze tools and abrasive powder is an enormous investment of skill and time, with total loss for a single misjudged stroke.

The handle is made of crystal beads threaded on bronze wire; the rim is faience with a gold band. It was crushed in the destruction of the palace and reconstructed from more than 300 pieces.

Because Zakros was never looted, its treasury was found as it stood — which is why this object exists at all. Comparable pieces elsewhere were removed in antiquity.
