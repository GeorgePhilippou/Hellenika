<!-- field: summary -->
A drinking cup from a Greek colony in Italy inscribed with a joke that assumes the audience knows Homer.

<!-- field: body -->
Found in a child's cremation burial at Pithekoussai on Ischia. Scratched on it, in three lines and largely in hexameter verse: "I am the cup of Nestor, good to drink from. Whoever drinks from this cup, desire for beautifully-crowned Aphrodite will seize him instantly."

The joke depends on the Iliad. Nestor's cup there is a massive golden vessel so heavy that only he can lift it. This is a small clay cup claiming to be it.

The implications are large. Around 720 BC, Greek settlers on an island off Italy were literate, wrote verse for fun, and could rely on a shared knowledge of Homeric material — which pushes the diffusion of epic and the alphabet earlier and further than was once assumed. That it was buried with a child adds a note nobody has satisfactorily explained.
