<!-- field: summary -->
A globular flask with an octopus wrapped around it, arms following the curve — the high point of Minoan Marine Style.

<!-- field: body -->
The Marine Style of c. 1500 BC covers vessels with octopuses, argonauts, dolphins, coral and seaweed, painted dark on light.

The octopus flask is its masterpiece because the design and the object are one thing: the arms curl around the body of the vessel so that the form dictates the composition, and the creature appears to be gripping the pot. The eyes sit at the visual centre of the front face.

Minoan art at its best works this way — pattern derived from observed nature rather than imposed geometry. The contrast with contemporary Egyptian or Mesopotamian formality is the clearest signature of Aegean visual culture.
