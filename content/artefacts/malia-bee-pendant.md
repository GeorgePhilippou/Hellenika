<!-- field: summary -->
Two bees holding a honeycomb between them, in gold — a technical showpiece of Minoan metalwork.

<!-- field: body -->
About 4.6 cm across, from the Chrysolakkos burial complex at Malia. Two insects face each other, curved bodies meeting around a granulated disc, with filigree wings, three suspended discs below and a small cage above.

It uses granulation — fusing minute gold spheres to a surface without visible solder — and repoussé and filigree, at a level not surpassed in the Aegean for a thousand years. Whether the insects are bees or wasps is argued.

Bees mattered: honey was the only concentrated sweetener, and Linear B tablets record honey offerings to deities including the Mistress of the Labyrinth.
