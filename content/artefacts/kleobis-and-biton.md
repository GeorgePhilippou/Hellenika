<!-- field: summary -->
A pair of massive Archaic marble youths dedicated at Delphi by the Argives around 580 BC, identified with the brothers of a story told by Herodotus.

<!-- field: significance -->
Among the earliest monumental kouroi, linked to one of Herodotus' most famous tales about human happiness.

<!-- field: body -->
Kleobis and Biton are two marble statues of naked young men (*kouroi*), about 2 m tall, dedicated at Delphi around 590–580 BC. They were found in 1893–94 during the French excavation of the sanctuary and are now in the Delphi Archaeological Museum. Heavy and powerful, with large heads, thick limbs and the left foot forward, they are among the earliest monumental kouroi of mainland Greece. An inscription names the Argive sculptor, perhaps Polymedes.

They are usually identified with the brothers in a story told by Herodotus. When Croesus of Lydia asked Solon who was the happiest man he had known, Solon named, second, two young men of Argos, Kleobis and Biton. When the oxen did not arrive to draw their mother, a priestess of Hera, to the goddess's festival, the brothers yoked themselves to the cart and pulled her 45 stades to the temple. She prayed that Hera grant them the best gift for mortals. That night they fell asleep in the temple and never woke. The Argives set up statues of them at Delphi.

Some scholars think the inscription instead refers to the divine twins Castor and Pollux. Either way, the statues show the confident, monumental style of early Archaic Greek sculpture.
