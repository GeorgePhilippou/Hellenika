<!-- field: summary -->
Names scratched in 591 BC by Greek mercenaries of the pharaoh Psammetichus II on a colossal statue of Ramesses II, far up the Nile.

<!-- field: significance -->
Among the earliest dated Greek inscriptions, and vivid evidence of Greeks serving in Egypt three centuries before Alexander.

<!-- field: body -->
From the 7th century BC the pharaohs of the 26th Dynasty employed Greek and Carian mercenaries, the "bronze men from the sea" of Herodotus. In 591 BC Psammetichus II sent an army south against the kingdom of Kush in Nubia. On its way, at Abu Simbel, some of his Greek soldiers carved graffiti into the left leg of one of the four colossal seated statues of Ramesses II outside the great temple, already about 650 years old.

The longest inscription reads, in translation: "When King Psammetichus came to Elephantine, this was written by those who sailed with Psammetichus son of Theocles. They came beyond Kerkis, as far as the river allowed. Potasimto led the foreign-speakers and Amasis the Egyptians." It is signed by Archon son of Amoibichos and Pelekos son of Eudamos. Other soldiers added their names and home cities, including Teos, Colophon and Ialysos on Rhodes.

Because the campaign is dated, the graffiti are among the earliest securely dated Greek inscriptions, and they show the forms of letters used by Greeks of different regions. When the temple was moved in the 1960s to save it from the waters of Lake Nasser, the inscribed statue went with it.
