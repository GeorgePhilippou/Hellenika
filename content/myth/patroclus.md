<!-- field: summary -->
The beloved companion of Achilles, whose death at Hector's hands brings Achilles back to battle.

<!-- field: significance -->
The hinge of the Iliad: his death turns Achilles' anger from the Greeks to Hector.

<!-- field: myth -->
Patroclus grew up in the house of Achilles' father Peleus after killing a boy in a quarrel. At Troy he accompanied Achilles. When Achilles withdrew from the fighting after his quarrel with Agamemnon, Patroclus borrowed his armour to rally the Greeks. He drove the Trojans back, but was struck by Apollo, wounded by Euphorbus and killed by Hector, who stripped the armour. Achilles returned to avenge him, killed Hector and held funeral games in Patroclus' honour. His ashes were later placed with those of Achilles.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Iliad, Books 9 to 23, is the main source. The Aethiopis and later epics treat his place in the cycle.

<!-- field: religious -->
Patroclus was honoured together with Achilles in hero cult around the Black Sea and at Troy, where travellers such as Alexander the Great laid wreaths at the tombs.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Aeschylus' lost *Myrmidons* made Patroclus and Achilles lovers, and Plato's *Symposium* discusses the idea. Madeline Miller's *The Song of Achilles* (2011) is a recent retelling.
