<!-- field: summary -->
A formidable hunter and runner whose competing traditions resist any single fixed biography or genealogy.

<!-- field: myth -->
Exposed as an infant and nursed by a bear, Atalanta grows into a hunter devoted to Artemis. She draws first blood in the Calydonian boar hunt, and Meleager awards her the hide, provoking a lethal quarrel with his relatives. In another famous story she agrees to marry only a man who can defeat her in a footrace; Hippomenes wins by distracting her with golden apples supplied by Aphrodite. Later the couple are transformed into lions.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Hesiodic fragments, lyric poetry and visual art preserve early elements. Later handbooks combine traditions that do not fit neatly together, including different fathers, regions and partners.

<!-- field: religious -->
Atalanta's closeness to Artemis places her in wilderness, hunting and resistance to marriage. Her eventual marriage and transformation make the story one of the many myths negotiating a girl's movement into adult social roles.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
It is misleading to force the Arcadian huntress, Boeotian runner and possible Argonaut into a single consistent life. Mythic figures could be shared, relocated and recombined as stories travelled.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
Greek vase painting and relief sculpture show Atalanta wrestling, hunting and racing. These images demonstrate her popularity and the range of episodes attached to her, not a historical athlete.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Atalanta has become a durable figure of female athletic independence. Modern versions often omit the coercive stakes of the marriage race or reinterpret her transformation as punishment for lost autonomy.
