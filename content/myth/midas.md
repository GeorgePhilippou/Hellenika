<!-- field: summary -->
The Phrygian king of Greek tradition who turned everything he touched into gold, and whose kingdom has been excavated at Gordion.

<!-- field: significance -->
A rare case in which a legendary king can be matched with a historical one.

<!-- field: myth -->
Midas took in Silenus, the old companion of Dionysus, who had been found drunk in his gardens. In return, Dionysus granted him a wish. He asked that all he touched should turn to gold, but food and drink were also changed, and he begged to be relieved. He washed in the river Pactolus, which then carried gold in its sands. Another story says that Midas preferred Pan's pipe to Apollo's lyre in a music contest, and Apollo gave him ass's ears, which he hid under a cap.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Herodotus (1.14) says that Midas dedicated his throne at Delphi. The golden touch and the donkey's ears are told by Ovid, but the stories have earlier roots. Assyrian inscriptions of Sargon II name a Mita of Mushki.

<!-- field: religious -->
The Phrygian king was an important political player in the eighth century BC. The largest burial mound at Gordion, once thought to be Midas' own, is now dated to about 740 BC and probably holds an earlier king.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
'The Midas touch' is a standard phrase for a gift for making money. Hawthorne retold the golden touch story in *A Wonder-Book* (1851).
