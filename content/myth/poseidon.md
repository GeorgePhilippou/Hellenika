<!-- field: summary -->
God of the sea, earthquakes and horses — and, on the Linear B evidence, the most important god of Bronze Age Pylos.

<!-- field: myth -->
Brother of Zeus and Hades, he received the sea in the division of the cosmos. He contested Athens with Athena and lost, and flooded the Attic plain in anger. He pursued Odysseus across the Mediterranean for blinding his son Polyphemus. He created the horse, and fathered the winged Pegasus.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Linear B, decisively. At Pylos, *po-se-da-o* receives more offerings than any other deity — more than Zeus. This is one of the clearest cases where the tablets contradict the later literary picture.

<!-- field: religious -->
Major sanctuaries at Sounion, the Isthmus of Corinth (where the Isthmian Games were held) and Helike. As *Enosichthon*, "earth-shaker", he was responsible for earthquakes — a serious matter in a seismically active region, and the reason Helike's destruction by earthquake and tsunami in 373 BC was read as his anger.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
His Bronze Age prominence and later demotion is a real historical pattern: the god of the palace states declines as the palaces do. The horse connection may be Indo-European inheritance, and his earthquake and sea functions may come from a pre-Greek Aegean deity absorbed into the same name.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
Pylos tablets recording extensive offerings; the Temple of Poseidon at Sounion; the Isthmian sanctuary; and the site of Helike, submerged in 373 BC and located by geologists in 2001 — the destruction Greeks attributed to Poseidon actually happened.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Identified with Roman Neptune. The Helike catastrophe is one proposed inspiration for Plato's Atlantis, which is chronologically much closer than Thera and equally unprovable.
