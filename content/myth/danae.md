<!-- field: summary -->
The Argive princess who bore Perseus to Zeus, who came to her as a shower of gold.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mother of Perseus, whose story shows the futility of trying to avoid a prophecy.

<!-- field: myth -->
King Acrisius of Argos learned from an oracle that his daughter's son would kill him, and locked Danaë in a bronze chamber or tower. Zeus entered as a stream of gold and she became pregnant. When Perseus was born, Acrisius refused to believe her, and put Danaë and the baby in a chest and set it adrift. It floated to Seriphos, where the fisherman Dictys took them in. Many years later, at games in Larissa, Perseus threw a discus that struck Acrisius and killed him.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
In *Iliad* 14, Zeus lists Danaë among his lovers. Sophocles' *Antigone* has an ode on her imprisonment, and Simonides' fragment on the chest is among the most celebrated pieces of Greek lyric.

<!-- field: religious -->
There was no cult of Danaë. Local tradition at Argos pointed to the place where she was shut up, and Virgil has her found the Italian town of Ardea.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Titian's painting of Danaë and the shower of gold, Rembrandt's and Klimt's, are famous. Danaë is also the subject of a lost play by Euripides.
