<!-- field: summary -->
The goddess of the dawn, who rises each morning from the Ocean and who lost her son Memnon at Troy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The personified dawn, whose myths give two classic images, the ageing Tithonus and the weeping mother of Memnon.

<!-- field: myth -->
Eos rose every morning from the edge of the world, her fingers rosy, to announce the sun. She carried off a series of mortal lovers, including Orion, Cephalus and Tithonus of Troy. For Tithonus she asked Zeus for eternal life, but not for eternal youth, and he withered until she shut him in a chamber, where he became the cicada. Her son Memnon came to help Troy and was killed by Achilles. Her tears for him are said to be the morning dew.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer's frequent formula 'rosy-fingered dawn' makes her one of the best-known figures in the epics. Hesiod's *Theogony* names her children, including the winds and the stars. The *Aethiopis*, a lost epic, told of Memnon.

<!-- field: religious -->
Eos had little worship, and was mainly honoured in poetry and art. In Roman Egypt, travellers recorded in Greek and Latin inscriptions that they had heard the 'voice of Memnon' from the northern colossus at Thebes.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Tennyson's *Tithonus* (1860) is a major reworking of the story. The Roman Aurora gives her name to the aurora.
