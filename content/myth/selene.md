<!-- field: summary -->
The goddess of the moon, sister of Helios and Eos, who loved the sleeping shepherd Endymion.

<!-- field: significance -->
The personified moon, who was gradually merged with other goddesses.

<!-- field: myth -->
Selene rode across the night sky in a chariot drawn by white horses, her light shining on earth and sea. She fell in love with Endymion, a beautiful shepherd or king on Mount Latmus in Caria, and Zeus granted him eternal sleep and youth so that she could visit him each night. In some stories she bore him fifty daughters, symbolising the fifty lunar months between Olympiads. Pan and Zeus were also said to have loved her.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
She appears in Hesiod's *Theogony* as a daughter of Hyperion and Theia, and the *Homeric Hymn to Selene* describes her. Sappho and Alcman mention her in fragments.

<!-- field: religious -->
Selene was honoured with offerings at the new and full moon, but she had few temples. She was often identified with Artemis, and associated with Hecate in magic. Roman Luna was her counterpart.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Endymion story was a favourite theme of painters and of Keats' poem *Endymion* (1818). The prefix 'seleno-' and the element selenium are named for her.
