<!-- field: summary -->
The legendary Phoenician founder of Thebes, who killed a dragon, sowed its teeth and was said to have brought the alphabet to Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
The founder figure of Thebes whose Phoenician origin reflects the real debt of the Greek alphabet to Phoenicia.

<!-- field: myth -->
Cadmus was sent by his father, the king of Tyre or Sidon, to search for his sister Europa after Zeus carried her off. At Delphi the oracle told him to give up and follow a cow, and to found a city where it lay down. This was the site of Thebes. He killed the dragon guarding the spring of Ares, and Athena told him to sow its teeth. Armed men sprang up and fought until five survived, the ancestors of the Theban nobility. He married Harmonia, daughter of Ares and Aphrodite, and their children included Semele, mother of Dionysus.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The story is told by Pindar and Herodotus and filled out by later writers. Herodotus says the Phoenicians who came with Cadmus brought writing.

<!-- field: religious -->
Thebes' citadel was called the Cadmeia. Excavations there show a Mycenaean palace with Linear B tablets, although no archaeological evidence supports a historical Cadmus.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides' *Bacchae* shows an aged Cadmus joining the Dionysiac revels. The transformation of Cadmus and Harmonia into serpents is told by Ovid.
