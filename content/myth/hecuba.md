<!-- field: summary -->
The queen of Troy, wife of Priam and mother of Hector and Paris, who lost her children and city and became a type of grief.

<!-- field: significance -->
The embodiment of the suffering of the defeated in Greek tragedy.

<!-- field: myth -->
Hecuba bore Priam many children. Before Paris' birth she dreamed she would give birth to a firebrand that would burn Troy. She saw her son Hector killed by Achilles, and Priam killed at the altar of Zeus, and her daughters carried off. After the sack she was given to Odysseus as a slave. In Thrace, on the way, she discovered that the king Polymestor had killed her youngest son Polydorus for his gold, and took her revenge, blinding him. She was said to have been transformed into a dog.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Iliad shows her as the grieving mother, especially in Book 22 as she begs Hector to come inside the walls, and Book 24. Her later fate is told in the epic cycle and in Euripides.

<!-- field: religious -->
A headland on the Thracian Chersonese was called Cynossema, 'the dog's tomb', and sailors were said to see Hecuba's grave there.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides wrote *Hecuba* and *The Trojan Women* about her. In Shakespeare's *Hamlet*, the player's speech on Hecuba prompts the question 'What's Hecuba to him?'.
