<!-- field: summary -->
The queen of Thebes who boasted that she was a better mother than Leto, lost all her children to Apollo and Artemis, and was turned to weeping stone.

<!-- field: significance -->
A warning against boasting before the gods, and a figure of unending grief.

<!-- field: myth -->
Niobe, proud of her seven sons and seven daughters, mocked Leto for bearing only two. Leto asked her children to avenge her insult. Apollo shot the sons while they were hunting, and Artemis killed the daughters in the house. Niobe was left childless, and in her grief fled to Mount Sipylus, where she turned to stone, with water still running from the rock like tears.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The story is told in *Iliad* Book 24, where Achilles says that gods themselves buried her children after nine days. Sophocles and Aeschylus wrote tragedies of this name, now lost, and Ovid tells a fuller version.

<!-- field: religious -->
Pausanias saw a rock on Mount Sipylus that looked like a woman weeping when seen from a distance. A rock-cut figure at Akpınar on the same mountain is often linked with the rock he saw.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
A group of statues of the Niobids, known from Roman copies, was found in Rome in 1583 and is now in Florence. Sculpture, painting and music have returned often to the theme of her grief.
