<!-- field: summary -->
The sun god, who sees and hears everything as he crosses the sky each day in his chariot.

<!-- field: significance -->
A god of the sun who was never one of the Olympians but who had an important cult at Rhodes.

<!-- field: myth -->
Helios rose each morning from the Ocean in the east, crossed the sky and sank in the west, returning by night in a golden cup. Because he sees everything, he informed Demeter that Hades had taken her daughter, and told Hephaestus of Aphrodite's adultery. His cattle grazed on the island of Thrinacia. When Odysseus' hungry men killed some, Helios threatened to shine among the dead, and Zeus smashed their ship. His son Phaethon, borrowing the chariot, lost control and was struck down by Zeus.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer and the *Homeric Hymn to Helios* name him as the sun. Hesiod gives his genealogy.

<!-- field: religious -->
The main cult was at Rhodes, where a four-horse chariot was thrown into the sea each year, and where the Colossus, built after the siege of 305–304 BC, represented him. From the Hellenistic period Helios was identified with Apollo and with Sol in Rome.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Roman emperors, including Aurelian, promoted Sol Invictus, and Helios appears as Christ in some mosaics of the third century AD.
