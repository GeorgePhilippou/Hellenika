<!-- field: summary -->
The beautiful youth loved by Aphrodite and killed by a boar, mourned each year in the women's festival of the Adonia.

<!-- field: significance -->
An import from the Near East that became a Greek symbol of beauty cut off in youth and of the seasonal death of vegetation.

<!-- field: myth -->
Adonis was born from a myrrh tree into which his mother Myrrha, in flight from her father, had been changed. Aphrodite hid the infant in a chest with Persephone, who became so fond of him that she would not return him. Zeus settled the quarrel by ordering that Adonis should spend a third of the year with each goddess and a third as he chose. As a young man he was killed by a boar while hunting, and Aphrodite's tears and his blood became the anemone.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
A fragment of Sappho (140) has women asking Aphrodite what to do about the dying Adonis. The mythographers Panyassis and later writers tell the birth and death. Theocritus describes the Adonia in Alexandria and Bion wrote a *Lament for Adonis*.

<!-- field: religious -->
At the Adonia, women placed 'gardens of Adonis', seedlings in pots, on rooftops, where they quickly sprouted and withered, and sang laments. The cult in Byblos, Cyprus and Syria shows older Near Eastern roots.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Shakespeare's *Venus and Adonis* (1593), Titian's painting, and Shelley's elegy for Keats, *Adonais* (1821), shaped a later tradition. The name Adonis denotes a handsome young man.
