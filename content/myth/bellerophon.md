<!-- field: summary -->
The Corinthian hero who rode the winged horse Pegasus to slay the Chimera.

<!-- field: significance -->
The model of a monster-slaying hero, whose fall from the sky was a warning against presumption.

<!-- field: myth -->
Bellerophon fled from Corinth after killing a man and was purified by King Proetus at Tiryns. Proetus' wife Stheneboea falsely accused him of approaching her, and her husband sent him to Lycia with a letter asking for his death. King Iobates set him to kill the Chimera, thinking it would destroy him. With a golden bridle from Athena, Bellerophon caught Pegasus and killed the monster from the air. He later defeated Lycian enemies and married Iobates' daughter. Bold with success, he tried to ride to Olympus and was thrown, and lived the rest of his life as a wanderer.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad*, Book 6, in the words of his grandson Glaucus. Pindar's *Olympian 13* tells of his taming of Pegasus.

<!-- field: religious -->
Bellerophon had a cult at Corinth, where he was honoured at a shrine outside the city and on the Craneion, according to Pausanias. In Lycia, tombs and reliefs show him.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides wrote a lost *Bellerophon*. He and Pegasus were adopted in Corinthian art, and Pegasus is a symbol of poetic inspiration.
