<!-- field: summary -->
The Theban princess who bore Dionysus to Zeus and died when she saw the god in his full glory.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mortal mother of Dionysus, whose rescue from the underworld shows his triumph over death.

<!-- field: myth -->
Semele, daughter of Cadmus, became the lover of Zeus. The jealous Hera, taking the form of her old nurse, persuaded her to ask Zeus to show himself as he was to Hera. Zeus had sworn on the river Styx to grant any request, and when he came in thunder and lightning, she was consumed. He snatched her unborn child from the flames, sewed him into his thigh and later brought him forth. Dionysus, grown, went down to Hades to fetch his mother and brought her to Olympus.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad* (14.323) mentions Semele as the mother of Dionysus, and Hesiod's *Theogony* (940) says she became immortal. Euripides' *Bacchae* opens with Dionysus at her tomb.

<!-- field: religious -->
Her tomb was shown at Thebes, and the site of her death was left unbuilt. Dionysus' descent for Semele was set at the Alcyonian lake at Lerna, where rites were held for him, and at Delphi the festival Herois recalled her return.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Handel's oratorio *Semele* (1744) and Ovid's account made her a standard subject in European art. Her rescue prefigures the later Orphic and mystery traditions.
