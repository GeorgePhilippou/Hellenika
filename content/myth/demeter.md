<!-- field: summary -->
Goddess of grain and the harvest, whose grief for her stolen daughter stopped the world growing.

<!-- field: myth -->
When Hades took Persephone, Demeter searched the earth with torches, and in her grief let nothing grow. Disguised as an old woman, she was taken in at Eleusis, where she nursed the queen's son and attempted to make him immortal by placing him in the fire. Discovered and interrupted, she revealed herself and demanded a temple. Only when famine threatened to end sacrifices altogether did Zeus intervene.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Homeric Hymn to Demeter. Possibly attested in Linear B, though the reading is disputed.

<!-- field: religious -->
The Eleusinian Mysteries, the most prestigious initiation cult in the Greek world, ran for close to two thousand years and admitted anyone who spoke Greek and had not committed murder — including women and the enslaved. Initiates were promised a better condition after death.

The Thesmophoria, a festival for married women only, was celebrated across the Greek world and involved retrieving decayed pig remains from pits and mixing them with seed grain. It is one of the few Greek rituals conducted entirely by women, from which men were rigorously excluded.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
The myth is a precise account of Mediterranean grain agriculture: the fallow, barren period is the dry summer when seed is stored underground in pits, and the return is the autumn sowing. The Thesmophoria's use of decayed organic matter with seed is effective agricultural practice as well as ritual.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
The Telesterion at Eleusis, a hall built for mass initiation and enlarged repeatedly over centuries; Thesmophoria pits with pig bones excavated at several sites; and the Ninnion Tablet, a painted votive plaque showing initiates in procession — among the very few images of the Mysteries.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Identified with Roman Ceres, from whom "cereal" derives. The Mysteries have attracted persistent speculation about psychoactive substances in the *kykeon*, which remains unevidenced.
