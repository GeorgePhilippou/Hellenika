<!-- field: summary -->
The one-eyed Cyclops of the Odyssey whom Odysseus blinds and outwits by calling himself 'Nobody'.

<!-- field: significance -->
The best-known monster of Odysseus' wanderings, and the origin of Odysseus' feud with Poseidon.

<!-- field: myth -->
Polyphemus lived in a cave with his flocks. Odysseus and twelve men entered it to take supplies, and were sealed in by a boulder. The giant ate two men at a time. Odysseus offered him strong wine and told him his name was Nobody. When Polyphemus fell asleep, the men heated an olive stake and drove it into his single eye. His neighbours, hearing him shout that Nobody was hurting him, did nothing. The men escaped under the bellies of the sheep. As he sailed away, Odysseus boasted his true name, and Polyphemus prayed to his father Poseidon to curse him.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Odyssey Book 9. Folktale parallels of a blinded ogre are found in many cultures, and scholars have studied how Homer adapted such a tale.

<!-- field: religious -->
There was no cult. Ancient writers located the Cyclopes near Etna and around Sicily, and giant fossil bones found there were interpreted as their remains.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides' satyr play *Cyclops* is the only complete example of its type. Theocritus and Ovid gave Polyphemus a lovelorn side, and the story has been often retold.
