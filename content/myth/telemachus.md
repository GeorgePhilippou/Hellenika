<!-- field: summary -->
The son of Odysseus, who grows from a passive boy into a man while searching for his father and then fights beside him.

<!-- field: significance -->
The model of a son's passage to maturity in Greek epic.

<!-- field: myth -->
Telemachus grew up in the household overrun by his mother's suitors. Athena, disguised as an old friend of Odysseus, told him to call an assembly and sail to look for news. He travelled to Nestor at Pylos and Menelaus at Sparta, who told him his father was alive on Calypso's island. On his return he avoided an ambush by the suitors and found Odysseus at the swineherd's hut. Together they planned and carried out the slaughter of the suitors. Later traditions give him a marriage to Nestor's daughter Polycaste or to Circe.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Odyssey is the earliest source, and the Telemachy in Books 1–4 shows his development. Lost epics such as the Telegony continued the story.

<!-- field: religious -->
There was no major cult. Ithaca's local hero worship focused on Odysseus himself.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Fénelon's *Les Aventures de Télémaque* (1699) was among the most read books of the eighteenth century. James Joyce used Telemachus for Stephen Dedalus in *Ulysses*.
