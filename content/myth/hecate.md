<!-- field: summary -->
The goddess of crossroads, magic and the night, who appears with torches and dogs and guards the thresholds between worlds.

<!-- field: significance -->
The best-known divinity of magic in the Greek world, later feared as the patron of witches.

<!-- field: myth -->
In Hesiod, Hecate is the daughter of the Titan Perses and Asteria, honoured by Zeus with a share of earth, sea and sky, and able to grant or withhold success in the assembly, in battle and at sea. In the Homeric Hymn to Demeter she hears Persephone's cries and joins Demeter with torches to seek her, and afterwards is Persephone's attendant in the underworld. She was shown with torches, daggers and snakes, and was accompanied by dogs.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Hesiod's *Theogony*, lines 411–452, praises her extensively. The Homeric Hymn to Demeter and later authors including Apollonius Rhodius connect her with magic. In the *Argonautica*, Medea is her priestess.

<!-- field: religious -->
Shrines were placed at crossroads and house doors, where Hecate-statues with three bodies were set. Monthly offerings of food, 'Hecate's suppers', were left for her at crossroads. Her great sanctuary at Lagina in Caria drew a festival until the Roman period.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Her connection with ghosts and magic is stressed in late antiquity, in the Greek magical papyri. Shakespeare's Hecate appears among the witches in *Macbeth*.
