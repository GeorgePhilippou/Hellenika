<!-- field: summary -->
The winged horse born from the blood of Medusa, ridden by Bellerophon, whose hoof opened the spring of the Muses on Helicon.

<!-- field: significance -->
A symbol of poetic inspiration and the emblem of Corinth's coinage.

<!-- field: myth -->
When Perseus beheaded Medusa, Pegasus and his brother Chrysaor sprang from her neck. Poseidon was their father. Pegasus flew to Olympus and carried Zeus' thunder and lightning. He was caught by Bellerophon, using a bridle from Athena, and together they slew the Chimera. After Bellerophon fell, Pegasus carried on to Olympus. On Mount Helicon, he struck the rock with his hoof and the spring Hippocrene gushed forth, from which the Muses drank. In later legend he became a constellation.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Hesiod's *Theogony* gives his birth and name, deriving it from *pegai*, 'springs', where he was born near the Ocean.

<!-- field: religious -->
Pegasus had no worship of his own, but the Corinthians put him on their silver staters, called 'colts' by other Greeks, from the sixth century BC.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Renaissance and later poets made Pegasus the steed of poets, and his name is used for a constellation and for many brands and projects.
