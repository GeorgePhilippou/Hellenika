<!-- field: summary -->
The greatest warrior of Troy, son of King Priam, who died at the hands of Achilles defending his city.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most sympathetic hero of the Iliad — a family man fighting for his home — whose death and burial frame its ending.

<!-- field: myth -->
Hector was the eldest son of Priam and Hecuba, king and queen of Troy, and the leader of the Trojan army. In the *Iliad* he drives the Greeks back to their ships while Achilles refuses to fight, and kills Patroclus, Achilles' friend, who has gone into battle wearing Achilles' armour. In Book 6 he says farewell to his wife Andromache and baby son Astyanax, who is frightened by the crest of his helmet; Hector takes it off and laughs. When Achilles returns, Hector faces him alone outside the walls, flees three times around the city, and is killed. Achilles drags his body behind his chariot, until Priam comes by night to ransom it. The *Iliad* ends with Hector's funeral.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer's *Iliad*, where he is the main Trojan hero.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
No historical Hector is known. Like other heroes of the Trojan War, he belongs to legend shaped by centuries of oral poetry.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Hector became a model of the noble warrior and patriot. In the Middle Ages he was counted among the Nine Worthies, and his farewell to Andromache was a favourite subject of painters.
