<!-- field: summary -->
King of Ithaca, deviser of the wooden horse, and the hero who survives by intelligence rather than strength.

<!-- field: myth -->
Odysseus feigned madness to avoid the war, was exposed, and became the Greeks' most valuable strategist — the wooden horse was his idea. His return took ten years: the Cyclops Polyphemus, whom he blinded after giving his name as Nobody; Circe; the Sirens; Scylla and Charybdis; seven years with Calypso. He reached Ithaca alone, in disguise, and killed the suitors who had besieged his wife Penelope and consumed his household.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Iliad, where he is already the cunning one, and the Odyssey. Later Greek tradition turns increasingly against him — in Athenian tragedy, especially Sophocles' Philoctetes, his cleverness becomes unscrupulousness.

<!-- field: religious -->
Odysseus had limited cult, mainly on Ithaca. A cave on Ithaca produced tripod dedications of the eighth century BC, which some connect to the Odyssey's account of tripods given to Odysseus by the Phaeacians — possibly cult inspired by the poem rather than the reverse.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
None identifiable. The poem's geography beyond the Greek mainland is deliberately fantastic, and attempts to locate Circe's island or the Cyclopes on a map treat a fairy tale as a chart.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
Excavations on Ithaca and nearby Kefalonia have found Mycenaean-period remains, including a substantial site at Agios Athanasios sometimes promoted as "Odysseus' palace". The identification is not accepted by most specialists. Homeric Ithaca's description does not match the modern island cleanly, which has generated a long literature and no resolution.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Dante puts Ulysses in Hell for the fraud of the horse but gives him a magnificent speech about sailing past the known world in pursuit of knowledge. Tennyson turned that into "Ulysses". Joyce mapped the whole poem onto a single day in Dublin.
