<!-- field: summary -->
The enchantress of Aeaea who turned Odysseus' crew into swine, then became his lover and adviser.

<!-- field: significance -->
The archetype of the sorceress who changes men's shape.

<!-- field: myth -->
Odysseus' ship landed at Aeaea. A party of men sent to explore the island found Circe's house surrounded by tame lions and wolves, were given a drugged drink and were changed into pigs. Hermes gave Odysseus the herb moly as a protection and told him to threaten her with his sword. She restored the men and welcomed them. After a year of feasting, she told Odysseus how to visit the underworld, avoid the Sirens and steer between Scylla and Charybdis.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Odyssey Book 10 is the earliest account, and Book 12 gives her warnings. Hesiod's *Theogony* gives her sons by Odysseus.

<!-- field: religious -->
The Romans identified Circe's island with Monte Circeo on the coast of Latium, where there was a cult place and local tradition of her residence.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Ovid, Spenser, Milton in *Comus*, and Joyce have all reworked the story. Madeline Miller's novel *Circe* (2018) retells the story from her view.
