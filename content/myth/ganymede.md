<!-- field: summary -->
The Trojan prince whom Zeus carried off to become cupbearer to the gods.

<!-- field: significance -->
An example of divine favour and the origin of the constellation Aquarius and of one of the larger moons of Jupiter.

<!-- field: myth -->
Ganymede, the son of the Trojan king Tros, was tending flocks on Mount Ida when Zeus, disguised as an eagle or sending one, carried him to Olympus. The gods made him immortal and he served nectar at the feasts, a task formerly done by Hebe, who was thereby displaced. Zeus gave Tros a pair of immortal horses to console him. In some versions Ganymede was set in the sky as Aquarius, the water-carrier.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Iliad Book 20 (lines 231–235) names him among the ancestors of Priam and says the gods took him for his beauty. The *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* retells the story.

<!-- field: religious -->
Ganymede appears on Greek vases from the late sixth century BC, often as a boy pursued by Zeus. A terracotta group of Zeus carrying Ganymede, of about 480 BC, was found at Olympia.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Michelangelo, Correggio and Rembrandt painted his rape by the eagle. The Latin form Catamitus gave the English word 'catamite', and Jupiter's largest moon is named after him.
