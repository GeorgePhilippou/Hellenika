<!-- field: summary -->
The Phoenician princess abducted to Crete by Zeus in the form of a bull, who became the mother of King Minos and gave her name to Europe.

<!-- field: significance -->
The myth that links Phoenicia, Crete and the origin of the name of a continent.

<!-- field: myth -->
Europa, daughter of the king of Tyre or Sidon, was gathering flowers by the shore when Zeus appeared as a gentle white bull. She climbed on his back, and he swam with her across the sea to Crete, where he took his own form and fathered Minos, Rhadamanthys and Sarpedon. In Gortyn they were said to have lain under a plane tree, which never lost its leaves. Europa married the Cretan king Asterion, who raised her sons.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad* mentions her as the mother of Minos and Rhadamanthys, with no details. The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* uses 'Europe' for central Greece. Her abduction is told in Hesiodic poems, by Moschus and by Ovid.

<!-- field: religious -->
In Crete, a festival called the Hellotia honoured Europa as Hellotis, and her bones were said to be carried in a garland of myrtle. The bull recalls Minoan bull imagery.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Titian's *Rape of Europa* and Rembrandt's drawing made it a standard subject, and the myth is used for the name and imagery of the European Union, including the Greek 2-euro coin.
