<!-- field: summary -->
The cunning king of Corinth condemned in the underworld to roll a boulder uphill for eternity.

<!-- field: significance -->
An image of futile, endless labour.

<!-- field: myth -->
Sisyphus, son of Aeolus, founded Corinth and grew rich by cunning. He revealed to the river god Asopus that Zeus had carried off his daughter Aegina, and Zeus sent Death to punish him. Sisyphus chained Death, so that no one could die until Ares freed him. He then tricked Hades again by ordering his wife to omit funeral rites, and talked Persephone into letting him go back to the world of the living. When he died of old age, he was condemned in Tartarus to push a great stone to the top of a hill, only to see it roll back.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad* calls him the craftiest of men, and Odysseus sees his torment in the underworld in *Odyssey* Book 11.

<!-- field: religious -->
The Isthmian Games were said to have been founded by Sisyphus in honour of his dead nephew Melicertes. Pausanias reports that his tomb was shown on the Isthmus.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Albert Camus' essay *The Myth of Sisyphus* (1942) makes the figure a symbol of absurdity and defiance, concluding that one must imagine Sisyphus happy.
