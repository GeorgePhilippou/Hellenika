<!-- field: summary -->
Agamemnon's daughter, offered as a sacrifice to Artemis at Aulis so that the Greek fleet could sail for Troy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most notorious sacrifice of the Trojan War, and the root of Clytemnestra's hatred of Agamemnon.

<!-- field: myth -->
When the Greek fleet gathered at Aulis, it was becalmed because Agamemnon had killed a deer sacred to Artemis. The seer Calchas said the goddess demanded the sacrifice of Agamemnon's daughter. Agamemnon summoned Iphigenia, telling her she was to marry Achilles. At the altar, according to Euripides, Artemis spirited her away to Tauris and left a deer; in the older story she died. In Tauris she served as priestess in the temple of Artemis until her brother Orestes arrived, and she escaped with him to Attica, where she was a priestess at Brauron.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Cypria told of the sacrifice, and Aeschylus' *Agamemnon* describes it in the choral ode. Euripides' two plays give the rescue.

<!-- field: religious -->
At Brauron and in Megara, there were shrines of Iphigenia, and girls' garments were dedicated there to her as a priestess of Artemis.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides' plays were adapted by Racine (1674), Gluck's operas (1774, 1779) and Goethe's *Iphigenie auf Tauris*.
