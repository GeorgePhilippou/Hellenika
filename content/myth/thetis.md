<!-- field: summary -->
The sea goddess, wife of the mortal Peleus and mother of Achilles, who pleads with Zeus for her son in the Iliad.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mother whose grief over a short-lived son shapes the Iliad.

<!-- field: myth -->
Thetis, daughter of Nereus, was so beautiful that Zeus and Poseidon both desired her, until a prophecy said her son would surpass his father. She was married by force to Peleus of Phthia in Thessaly, whom she tried to escape by changing shapes. Their wedding was attended by the gods, and the golden apple thrown by Eris began the quarrel that led to Troy. Their son was Achilles. In the Iliad, Thetis goes to Zeus and to Hephaestus on her son's behalf and later mourns him with the Nereids.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Iliad Book 1 and Book 18 give her most important scenes, and the Cypria told of her marriage. Dipping Achilles in the Styx is first told in Statius.

<!-- field: religious -->
Thetis had a shrine at Cape Sepias in Thessaly, where she was said to have been seized by Peleus, and a temple at Sparta. In the Iliad she also tells of how she rescued Hephaestus and Dionysus.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Thetis appears in Pindar and in Catullus' poem 64 on the wedding of Peleus and Thetis. Ingres' *Jupiter and Thetis* (1811) shows her pleading for her son.
