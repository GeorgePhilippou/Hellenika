<!-- field: summary -->
The queen of Mycenae who killed her husband Agamemnon after his return from Troy and was killed in turn by her son Orestes.

<!-- field: significance -->
The most powerful female figure in Greek tragedy, whose crime drives the entire Oresteia.

<!-- field: myth -->
Clytemnestra was the wife of Agamemnon, who sacrificed their daughter Iphigenia at Aulis so that the Greek fleet would reach Troy. During the ten years he was away she ruled Mycenae with Aegisthus, who became her lover. When Agamemnon returned with Cassandra, she greeted him with a purple carpet, led him to the bath and killed him with an axe or sword, in a net or robe. Years later her son Orestes, urged by Apollo, killed both her and Aegisthus. The Furies then hounded him.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer knows the murder from the Odyssey, where Aegisthus plays the leading role and Clytemnestra a lesser one. Stesichorus' *Oresteia* is a lost lyric version, and Aeschylus gives her the central role.

<!-- field: religious -->
She had no cult. The beehive tomb called the Tomb of Clytemnestra at Mycenae, from the thirteenth century BC, got its name in modern times.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
She appears in the plays of Sophocles and Euripides and in O'Neill's *Mourning Becomes Electra*. Her image as a murderous wife became a standard theme in opera and poetry.
