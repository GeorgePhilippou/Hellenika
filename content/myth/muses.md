<!-- field: summary -->
The nine goddesses of poetry, music and knowledge, daughters of Zeus and Memory, invoked by poets for inspiration.

<!-- field: significance -->
The source of inspiration in Greek thought — and of the words music and museum.

<!-- field: myth -->
The Muses were daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne, the goddess of Memory, born in Pieria near Mount Olympus. They sang for the gods at their feasts, led by Apollo, and lived on Mount Helicon in Boeotia and on Parnassus. In Hesiod's *Theogony* they appear to him as he tends sheep on Helicon and give him the gift of song. Later tradition assigned each a field: Calliope (epic), Clio (history), Euterpe (music), Erato (love poetry), Melpomene (tragedy), Polyhymnia (hymns), Terpsichore (dance), Thalia (comedy) and Urania (astronomy).

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer invokes the Muse at the start of the *Iliad* and *Odyssey*; Hesiod first names all nine.

<!-- field: religious -->
The Muses had sanctuaries on Helicon and elsewhere, and schools and philosophers honoured them: Plato's Academy had a shrine to them. The Ptolemies' Museum at Alexandria was literally their "shrine".

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Poets from Virgil to Milton invoked the Muse. Their name lives on in "music", "museum" and "amuse".
