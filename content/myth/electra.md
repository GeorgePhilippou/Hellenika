<!-- field: summary -->
Agamemnon's daughter who waits for her brother Orestes and urges him to avenge their father by killing their mother.

<!-- field: significance -->
The grieving avenger of the house of Atreus, central to three surviving Greek tragedies.

<!-- field: myth -->
After Agamemnon's death, Electra lived at Mycenae under the rule of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, treated as little better than a servant. She mourned for her father and waited for Orestes, who had been sent away as a child. When he returned in disguise, brother and sister recognised each other, and in the best-known versions she took part in the plot. Orestes killed both Clytemnestra and Aegisthus. Afterwards Electra was married to Orestes' friend Pylades.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Electra is not found in Homer. Stesichorus and the poets of the sixth century are the earliest to mention her. She appears in Aeschylus' *Libation Bearers* (458 BC) and in the plays named for her by Sophocles and Euripides.

<!-- field: religious -->
There was no cult of Electra. A tomb at Mycenae was shown to visitors as hers in Pausanias' time.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Hofmannsthal's play and Richard Strauss's opera *Elektra* (1909) and O'Neill's *Mourning Becomes Electra* (1931) reinterpret the figure. Jung proposed the 'Electra complex' in 1913.
