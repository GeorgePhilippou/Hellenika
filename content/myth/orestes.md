<!-- field: summary -->
The son of Agamemnon who avenged his father by killing his mother, the last act of the curse on the House of Atreus.

<!-- field: significance -->
The centre of Greek tragedy's greatest family saga, whose trial in Aeschylus' Oresteia stages the birth of justice by law.

<!-- field: myth -->
The House of Atreus, rulers of Mycenae, was cursed across generations. Tantalus served his son to the gods; Atreus murdered his brother Thyestes' children and fed them to him. Atreus' son Agamemnon sacrificed his daughter Iphigenia to win a wind for the fleet to Troy. On his return, his wife Clytemnestra, with her lover Aegisthus, son of Thyestes, killed him in his bath. Their son Orestes, sent away as a child, came back years later with his friend Pylades, was recognised by his sister Electra, and on Apollo's command killed Clytemnestra and Aegisthus. Pursued by the Furies for shedding a mother's blood, he fled to Delphi and then Athens, where he was tried and acquitted by Athena's court.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Odyssey* holds up Orestes' revenge as a model for Telemachus. Aeschylus' *Oresteia* (458 BC), Sophocles' *Electra* and Euripides' *Electra* and *Orestes* dramatise it.

<!-- field: religious -->
Herodotus says the Spartans, told by Delphi to find Orestes' bones, found and brought them home from Tegea in the 6th century BC, claiming his heroic protection.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The story has been retold endlessly, from Eugene O'Neill's *Mourning Becomes Electra* to Sartre's *The Flies*, and gave psychology the "Electra complex".
