<!-- field: summary -->
The Spartan king whose wife Helen was taken by Paris, and who joined his brother Agamemnon in the war against Troy.

<!-- field: significance -->
The wronged husband whose cause drew the Greek kings to Troy.

<!-- field: myth -->
Menelaus, son of Atreus, married Helen and became king of Sparta. When Paris took her to Troy, he called on the oath of her former suitors and joined his brother Agamemnon in leading the expedition. In the Iliad he fights a duel with Paris, which he almost wins. After Troy fell he was driven off course for seven years, reaching Egypt, where Proteus told him how to get home. Once back in Sparta, he lived with Helen in wealth.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Menelaus appears throughout the Iliad as a brave but not outstanding fighter, and in Book 4 of the Odyssey as a prosperous host.

<!-- field: religious -->
At the Menelaion, on a ridge east of the Eurotas, worshippers made offerings to Menelaus and Helen from the eighth century BC, and Isocrates and Pausanias mention the cult. Excavations found a Late Bronze Age mansion beneath the shrine.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides' *Andromache* and *Helen* show Menelaus as a petty, boastful figure, and *Orestes* makes him a self-serving relative; in the Odyssey he is a more dignified host.
