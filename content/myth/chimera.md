<!-- field: summary -->
The fire-breathing monster of Lycia, part lion, part goat and part serpent, killed by Bellerophon from the back of Pegasus.

<!-- field: significance -->
The origin of the word 'chimera' for a hybrid creature or an unattainable fantasy.

<!-- field: myth -->
The Chimera was a monster, lion in front, serpent behind and goat in the middle, breathing flames. It was bred by Amisodarus, king of Caria, and ravaged Lycia. Iobates, the king of Lycia, sent Bellerophon to destroy it, expecting him to die. Bellerophon rode Pegasus above the creature and shot it from out of range, or, in some versions, thrust a lump of lead on his lance into its throat, which melted in the flames and killed it.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad*, Book 6, is the first account, and Hesiod's *Theogony* gives its parents Typhon and Echidna.

<!-- field: religious -->
In Lycia, the burning vents on Mount Chimaera near modern Çıralı, known in antiquity for their eternal flames, were identified with the monster's home and the area had a shrine of Hephaestus.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Chimera of Arezzo, an Etruscan bronze of about 400 BC, became an icon of Renaissance collecting. In modern language a chimera is a fanciful idea or, in biology, an organism with mixed tissues.
