<!-- field: summary -->
The Trojan prince who judged the contest of three goddesses and carried off Helen of Sparta, setting off the Trojan War.

<!-- field: significance -->
The man whose choice and elopement brought about the destruction of Troy.

<!-- field: myth -->
Paris was born to Priam and Hecuba, but because of a prophecy that he would destroy Troy, he was exposed on Mount Ida and raised by shepherds. Zeus chose him to judge which of Hera, Athena and Aphrodite was the fairest. Each offered a bribe; he chose Aphrodite, who promised him Helen, wife of Menelaus. He sailed to Sparta and took her to Troy. During the war he fought mainly with the bow, and with Apollo's help killed Achilles. He was himself fatally wounded by an arrow of Philoctetes.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Iliad shows Paris as handsome and cowardly, quarrelling with Hector and fighting a duel with Menelaus from which Aphrodite rescues him. The judgement and abduction belong to the lost Cypria.

<!-- field: religious -->
There was no significant cult of Paris. A Hittite letter from the thirteenth century BC mentions Alaksandu, king of Wilusa (probably Ilios), and some scholars link him with Alexandros.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Judgement of Paris became a favourite subject for Greek vases and for painters from Cranach to Rubens, and it inspired Offenbach's operetta *La belle Hélène*.
