<!-- field: summary -->
A legendary nation of women warriors from the edges of the Greek world, fought by heroes such as Heracles, Theseus and Achilles.

<!-- field: significance -->
A favourite subject of Greek art and myth, and perhaps a memory of real women warriors among the steppe peoples.

<!-- field: myth -->
The Amazons were a people of women warriors, said to live by the river Thermodon on the Black Sea or in the far north-east. They fought on horseback with bows, axes and spears, and in legend lived without men. Greek heroes repeatedly fought them. Heracles took the belt of their queen Hippolyta as one of his labours. Theseus carried off the Amazon Antiope, and the Amazons invaded Attica in revenge, a battle the Athenians later compared to the Persian invasion. Achilles killed the Amazon queen Penthesilea at Troy and, in some versions, fell in love with her as she died.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer mentions the Amazons in the *Iliad*; later poets and Herodotus told more.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
Battles of Greeks and Amazons (*Amazonomachies*) decorated the Parthenon, the temple at Bassae, the Mausoleum and countless vases. Archaeologists have found many graves of women buried with weapons among the Scythians and Sarmatians of the steppe, which may lie behind the legend.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Amazons have stood for female strength and independence; the Amazon river was named after them by Spanish explorers.
