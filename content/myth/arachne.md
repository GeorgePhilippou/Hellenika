<!-- field: summary -->
The Lydian weaver who rivalled Athena, lost the contest and was turned into a spider.

<!-- field: significance -->
A myth about skill, pride and divine jealousy, and the explanation of the spider.

<!-- field: myth -->
Arachne, daughter of a dyer from Colophon in Lydia, wove so well that nymphs gathered to watch. She boasted she was better than Athena. The goddess, disguised as an old woman, warned her to be humble, but Arachne repeated her challenge. Athena wove the gods' victories over mortals who had defied them, and Arachne wove the gods' deceptions of women. Her work was faultless. Furious, Athena struck her. Arachne hanged herself, and Athena changed her into a spider, always spinning.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The story is told at length by Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 6, and was perhaps known to Virgil. The Greek forms of the legend are preserved only in late writers.

<!-- field: religious -->
Weaving was central to Athena's cult, and at the Panathenaia women wove her robe, so the story reflects the goddess's role as patron of the craft.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Velázquez's *The Spinners* is related to the story. Arachnids take their scientific name from her, and Dante placed her in the *Purgatorio* as an example of pride.
