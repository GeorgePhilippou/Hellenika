<!-- field: summary -->
The twin heroes Castor and Polydeuces, brothers of Helen, who were worshipped at Sparta and protected sailors and warriors.

<!-- field: significance -->
Patrons of Sparta, travellers and sailors, and the origin of the constellation Gemini.

<!-- field: myth -->
Castor, a horseman, and Polydeuces, a boxer, were born to Leda, one the son of the mortal Tyndareus and the other of Zeus. They sailed with the Argonauts and rescued their sister Helen from Theseus. Their cousins Idas and Lynceus quarrelled with them over cattle and a bride, and killed Castor. Polydeuces, immortal, begged Zeus to let him die with his brother, and Zeus allowed them to share each day between the underworld and Olympus. They were placed in the sky as the constellation Gemini.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
In the Iliad (3.236) Helen looks for her brothers among the Greek warriors and does not see them, as they are dead. The *Homeric Hymn to the Dioscuri* and Pindar give fuller accounts.

<!-- field: religious -->
At Sparta they were symbolised by two upright beams joined by a crossbar, and one of the two Spartan kings always went to war with their images. They also had cult at Athens (as the Anakes), Cyrene and Rome, where their temple stood in the Forum.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Acts 28 mentions a ship with the Dioscuri as figurehead. Their names were given to the stars Castor and Pollux in Gemini.
