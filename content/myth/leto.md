<!-- field: summary -->
The goddess who bore Apollo and Artemis on Delos after Hera banned her from giving birth anywhere else.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mother of two of the great Olympians, honoured especially in Lycia and on Delos.

<!-- field: myth -->
Pregnant by Zeus, Leto was refused refuge everywhere by order of Hera, who also kept Eileithyia, goddess of childbirth, away. The floating island of Delos agreed to take her on promising to be honoured thereafter. After nine days of labour she bore Artemis and then Apollo beneath a palm tree. Later, in Lycia, she was refused water by herdsmen at a pool and turned them into frogs. She also sought to protect her children against Niobe and the giant Tityus.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad* names Leto as the mother of Apollo and Artemis. The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* describes the birth on Delos in detail.

<!-- field: religious -->
The Letoon of Xanthos in Lycia, a UNESCO site, was her chief sanctuary, and Delos had a Letoon of the sixth century BC. In Crete, at Phaistos, she had a cult as Leto Phytia.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Romans called her Latona. The fountain of Latona at Versailles shows the Lycian peasants being turned into frogs.
