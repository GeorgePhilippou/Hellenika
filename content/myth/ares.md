<!-- field: summary -->
The Olympian god of war in its violent, chaotic aspect — powerful, feared and little loved, even by the other gods.

<!-- field: significance -->
The god of the brute force of battle, set against Athena's strategy; his Roman counterpart Mars became one of Rome's greatest gods.

<!-- field: myth -->
Ares was the son of Zeus and Hera. In the *Iliad* he fights for the Trojans, and is wounded by the Greek hero Diomedes with Athena's help, bellowing like ten thousand men; Zeus tells him he is the most hateful of all the gods. In the *Odyssey* the bard Demodocus sings of his affair with Aphrodite, wife of Hephaestus, who traps the lovers in an invisible net for the gods to laugh at. His children with Aphrodite included Phobos (Fear) and Deimos (Terror), who rode with him into battle.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
His name, *a-re*, appears on Linear B tablets from Knossos, possibly as a title of war. Homer gives the fullest early portrait.

<!-- field: religious -->
Ares had fewer major cults than most Olympians. He was worshipped at Sparta and in Thrace, which Greeks saw as his homeland, and had a temple in the Athenian Agora. The Areopagus, the "hill of Ares", where Athens' ancient council met, bore his name.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The Romans identified him with Mars, a far more honoured god, father of Romulus and patron of the Roman army. The planet Mars and the month of March are named after him.
