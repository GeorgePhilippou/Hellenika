<!-- field: summary -->
The goddess of retribution who punishes arrogance and restores balance, honoured at Rhamnous in Attica.

<!-- field: significance -->
The embodiment of the Greek fear that excessive success invites a fall.

<!-- field: myth -->
Nemesis punished those who showed hubris, whether by pride, by wealth or by unequal fortune. When Zeus pursued her, she changed shape repeatedly, and ended as a goose; Zeus took the form of a swan, and she laid an egg from which Helen was born, according to the Cypria. In Attica it was said that she punished the Persians who brought a block of marble to Marathon to set up a victory monument before the battle, and that the block became her statue.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Hesiod's *Theogony* names her among the children of Night. The Cypria gives her myth with Zeus.

<!-- field: religious -->
The temple at Rhamnous, with the colossal cult statue of Nemesis crowned with a diadem, was the centre of her cult, and the site of an Attic fortress and sanctuaries. Her worship was widespread in the Roman era, when she was associated with the arena.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
In English, 'nemesis' means a long-standing rival or inescapable agent of downfall.
