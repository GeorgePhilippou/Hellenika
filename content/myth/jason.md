<!-- field: summary -->
Leader of the Argonauts, whose quest for the Golden Fleece succeeds through Medea's aid and ends in betrayal at Corinth.

<!-- field: myth -->
Jason's uncle Pelias has seized the throne of Iolcus. Ordered to fetch the Golden Fleece from distant Colchis, Jason gathers the Argonauts and sails in the Argo through a sequence of dangers. King Aeetes demands impossible tasks, but his daughter Medea falls in love with Jason and supplies the knowledge and drugs that make success possible. They flee together. Years later at Corinth, Jason abandons Medea for a royal marriage, provoking the catastrophe dramatised by Euripides.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Homer already refers to the Argo as famous, showing that some form of the voyage was familiar by the early Archaic period. Pindar gives an extended version, and Apollonius' third-century BC *Argonautica* is the fullest surviving epic narrative.

<!-- field: religious -->
Jason was woven into local genealogies and place traditions rather than worshipped through a single pan-Greek cult. Communities along imagined routes could attach landmarks, rites and founders to the voyage.

<!-- field: historicalBackground -->
The journey projects Greek navigation toward the Black Sea into heroic time. It may reflect later familiarity with eastern trade and colonisation, but the poem's geography and magical trials do not preserve a usable log of one Bronze Age voyage.

<!-- field: archaeology -->
Archaeology demonstrates Bronze Age and later contact across the Aegean and Black Sea. It cannot identify the Argo, Golden Fleece or crew. Proposed technical explanations for the fleece remain speculative.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Jason's reputation varies with the source. Adventure traditions centre his leadership, while Euripides exposes his opportunism and makes Medea's sacrifice and abandonment impossible to ignore.
