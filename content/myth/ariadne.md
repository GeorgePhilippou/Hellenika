<!-- field: summary -->
The Cretan princess who gave Theseus the thread to escape the labyrinth, was abandoned by him on Naxos and married the god Dionysus.

<!-- field: significance -->
The heroine of the labyrinth story, and an image of abandonment and rescue that has inspired art, poetry and opera.

<!-- field: myth -->
Ariadne was the daughter of King Minos of Crete and Pasiphae, and half-sister of the Minotaur. When the Athenian hero Theseus came to Crete among the young people sent as tribute to be fed to the Minotaur, Ariadne fell in love with him. On the advice of Daedalus she gave him a ball of thread, which he tied at the entrance of the labyrinth and unwound as he went in; after killing the Minotaur he followed it back out. Ariadne fled with him, but Theseus left her asleep on the island of Naxos. There the god Dionysus found her and made her his wife, setting her bridal crown among the stars as the constellation Corona Borealis.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Iliad* mentions the dancing floor Daedalus made for her at Knossos; Hesiod and later writers tell her story.

<!-- field: religious -->
Ariadne was honoured on Naxos and elsewhere, sometimes as a goddess, perhaps reflecting an older Cretan or Aegean deity whose name meant "most holy".

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The abandoned Ariadne was a favourite subject of Catullus, Ovid, Titian and Richard Strauss's opera *Ariadne auf Naxos*. "Ariadne's thread" means a way out of a complex problem.
