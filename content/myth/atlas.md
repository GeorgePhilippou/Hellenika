<!-- field: summary -->
The Titan condemned to hold up the sky at the western edge of the world, whose name was given to a mountain range, an ocean and a book of maps.

<!-- field: significance -->
A figure of endurance whose name came to stand for the whole of the known world.

<!-- field: myth -->
After the Titans lost their war against the Olympians, Zeus sentenced Atlas to stand at the western end of the earth and hold up the sky on his shoulders. In the myth of Heracles' eleventh labour, Heracles asked Atlas to fetch golden apples from the Hesperides and took the sky meanwhile, then tricked him into taking it back. When Perseus came by with Medusa's head, Atlas refused him hospitality, and was turned to stone, becoming the Atlas Mountains in north-west Africa.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Hesiod's *Theogony* (line 517) gives his task and parentage, and the *Odyssey* calls him the father of Calypso and knower of the sea's depths.

<!-- field: religious -->
Atlas had no significant worship. The geographers identified him with the mountains in Libya and Mauretania, and gave the name Atlantic to the sea beyond them.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Mercator used Atlas for the title of his collection of maps in 1595, giving us the word 'atlas'. The statue of Atlas at Rockefeller Center is a modern landmark.
