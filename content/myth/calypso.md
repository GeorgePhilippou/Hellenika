<!-- field: summary -->
The nymph of Ogygia who kept Odysseus on her island for seven years and offered him immortality.

<!-- field: significance -->
A figure who tests Odysseus' desire to go home against the offer of eternal ease.

<!-- field: myth -->
After the wreck of his last ship, Odysseus drifted for nine days to Ogygia, where Calypso took him in and made him her lover. She kept him seven years, though he spent his days weeping on the shore. When Athena complained to Zeus, he sent Hermes to order her to let him go. Calypso, resentful of the gods who permit goddesses no mortal lovers, obeyed. She helped him to build a raft, and gave him food and wind. Odysseus had refused her offer of immortality in preference for his wife.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
Book 5 of the Odyssey gives the main account, and Odysseus retells it in Book 7. Hesiod makes her a daughter of Oceanus.

<!-- field: religious -->
There is no known cult of Calypso. Later writers placed Ogygia in the western Mediterranean, and Malta and Gozo claim it.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
The story inspired Fénelon, Joyce and many poets. The name has been given to calypso music of Trinidad, and Calypso is also a moon of Saturn.
