<!-- field: summary -->
Hector's wife, who loses her husband, son and city at Troy and is taken as a slave to Greece.

<!-- field: significance -->
The type of the war widow in Greek literature.

<!-- field: myth -->
Andromache was the daughter of Eëtion, king of Thebe, whom Achilles killed along with her seven brothers. She married Hector, and bore Astyanax. When Hector went out to fight Achilles she begged him to stay on the walls. After his death, she watched Achilles drag his body behind his chariot. At the fall of Troy her son was killed and she was given to Neoptolemus, to whom she bore a son. In some versions she later married Helenus, a son of Priam, and ruled in Epirus.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The Iliad gives her scenes in Books 6, 22 and 24, with the most famous lament over Hector. Her fate after the war is told in the epic cycle and in Euripides.

<!-- field: religious -->
There was no extended cult of Andromache, but the kings of Epirus, including Pyrrhus, traced their descent from Neoptolemus and Andromache.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Euripides' *Andromache* and *Trojan Women* centre on her. Racine's *Andromaque* (1667) and Virgil's picture of her in Book 3 of the *Aeneid* made her a figure of sorrow and fidelity in later European literature.
