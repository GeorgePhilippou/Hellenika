<!-- field: summary -->
The Spartan queen visited by Zeus in the form of a swan, and mother of Helen, Clytemnestra and the Dioscuri.

<!-- field: significance -->
The mother of the Trojan War's leading figures, and a favourite of European art.

<!-- field: myth -->
Leda married Tyndareus of Sparta. Zeus, disguised as a swan chased by an eagle, took refuge in her arms and seduced her. On the same night she slept with her husband. She laid eggs from which her children hatched. The tradition about which children were Zeus's varies, but Helen is always his, and Castor and Polydeuces are known as the Dioscuri, 'sons of Zeus', even where only one is.

<!-- field: earliestSource -->
The *Odyssey* (11.298) names Leda as mother of Castor and Polydeuces. The *Cypria* gives Helen a different mother, Nemesis, and later poets combined the two. Euripides' *Helen* mentions the swan.

<!-- field: religious -->
At Sparta, a large egg hung from the roof of a temple was said to be Leda's. The Spartans honoured Helen and the twins.

<!-- field: laterInterpretation -->
Leonardo, Michelangelo, Correggio and others painted 'Leda and the Swan', and Yeats's poem of 1923 treats the union as a violent beginning of history.
