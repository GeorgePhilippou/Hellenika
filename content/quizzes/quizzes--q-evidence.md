questions:
  - q: 'A claim resting on an inscription carved in stone is classified as:'
    options:
      - Literary evidence
      - Epigraphic evidence
      - Archaeological evidence
      - Ancient tradition
    answer: 1
    explain: Epigraphic evidence is text inscribed on durable material. It is often contemporary with the events, which makes it unusually valuable.
  - q: 'Coin hoards are especially useful to historians because they:'
    options:
      - Always name the ruler who issued them
      - Provide precise dating and show trade circulation
      - Are never forged
      - Record political speeches
    answer: 1
    explain: Numismatic evidence dates deposits tightly and maps economic reach. Athenian owls turning up in Egypt tells you about trade no text records.
    link: athenian-owl-tetradrachm
  - q: Which confidence level fits "Agamemnon ruled Mycenae"?
    options:
      - Established
      - Strong evidence
      - Debated
      - Legendary
    answer: 3
    explain: The claim comes solely from poetic tradition with no supporting contemporary evidence. Legendary is not a synonym for false — it marks the kind of claim it is.
    link: agamemnon
  - q: Which confidence level fits "The Parthenon was built between 447 and 432 BC"?
    options:
      - Established
      - Probable
      - Debated
      - Speculative
    answer: 0
    explain: Inscribed building accounts survive recording expenditure year by year. This is about as securely dated as anything in Greek history.
    link: parthenon-built
  - q: Why is "the Library of Alexandria burned down in one catastrophe" classified as debated?
    options:
      - No ancient source mentions the Library
      - Evidence points to gradual decline through several causes
      - The Library has never been located
      - The claim originates in the 19th century
    answer: 1
    explain: Caesar's harbour fire, lost patronage and the expulsion of scholars all contributed over centuries. The single-catastrophe story is tidier than the evidence.
    link: founding-library-alexandria
  - q: 'A Minoan fresco reconstructed from a few original fragments should be treated as:'
    options:
      - Direct archaeological evidence for what Minoans depicted
      - Partly archaeological evidence, partly modern interpretation
      - Worthless for historical purposes
      - Literary evidence
    answer: 1
    explain: The surviving fragments are evidence. The connecting passages are a modern painter's hypothesis, and the two need to be visually distinguishable.
    link: bull-leaping-fresco
