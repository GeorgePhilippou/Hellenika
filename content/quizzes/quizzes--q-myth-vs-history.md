questions:
  - q: Which of these is NOT narrated in the Iliad?
    options:
      - The quarrel between Achilles and Agamemnon
      - The death of Hector
      - The wooden horse and the fall of Troy
      - Priam's visit to Achilles' tent
    answer: 2
    explain: The Iliad covers about fifty days in the tenth year and ends with Hector's funeral. The horse comes from the lost Iliupersis and, in full, from Virgil.
    link: trojan-horse
  - q: What has never been found in Minoan art?
    options:
      - Bull-leaping scenes
      - Double axes
      - A bull-headed human figure
      - Women in flounced skirts
    answer: 2
    explain: The Minotaur is a Greek story about Crete, not a Minoan one. Bulls are everywhere in Minoan art; the hybrid monster is not.
    link: minotaur
  - q: 'Hittite archives support a historical Troy connection by naming:'
    options:
      - Agamemnon and Menelaus
      - Wilusa, a king Alaksandu, and a power called Ahhiyawa
      - A ten-year siege of a coastal city
      - A queen taken from Sparta
    answer: 1
    explain: Independent, contemporary, non-Greek documents naming a place and a power. That is evidence for a context, not for the Iliad's war.
    link: hattusa-ref
  - q: 'Poseidon''s importance in the Linear B tablets from Pylos is notable because:'
    options:
      - He receives more offerings than any other deity, including Zeus
      - He is not mentioned at all
      - He appears only as a place name
      - He is identified with Hades
    answer: 0
    explain: The Bronze Age pantheon was not the Homeric one. Poseidon was pre-eminent at Pylos and was later demoted as the palace states disappeared.
    link: poseidon
  - q: 'Helen received cult worship at Sparta, which suggests:'
    options:
      - She was certainly a historical queen
      - Spartans venerated all Homeric characters
      - She may have originated as a goddess later demoted to a heroine
      - The cult was invented in the Hellenistic period
    answer: 2
    explain: Cult worship is a different kind of evidence from epic narrative, and it points the opposite way from the usual assumption that heroes get promoted.
    link: helen-of-troy
